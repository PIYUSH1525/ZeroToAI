    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        A simple way to think about choosing is <strong className="text-white">expected utility</strong>: how likely is the action to work, how valuable is success, and what does it cost (time, money, risk)?
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060913] p-5 text-center text-white">

      <div>
        In the code, four options for the same question are scored. The database query wins because it is both reliable and cheap, even though asking the user is the most likely to succeed.

      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-emerald-300">Why this matters:</strong> Real agents do not compute numbers like these, but the model is implicitly weighing the same factors. You can <strong className="text-white">shape its choices</strong> by changing tool descriptions, adding costs to your policy or setting rules such as <em>ask when unsure</em>.
