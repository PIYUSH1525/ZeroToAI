    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Intermediate: How Automatic Differentiation (Autograd) Works
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        In our Calculus lessons, we calculated partial derivatives $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w}$ by hand using the Chain Rule. When a model has $100$ million parameters, doing derivatives by hand is impossible. Instead, PyTorch uses <strong className="text-emerald-300">Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (Autograd)</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">1. Turn On Tracking (requires_grad=True)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            When you create a learnable weight tensor with <code>requires_grad=True</code>, PyTorch starts recording every single operation applied to that tensor in a <strong className="text-white">Dynamic Computational Graph (DAG)</strong>.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 font-mono text-xs text-emerald-300">
            w = torch.tensor([3.0], requires_grad=True)
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">2. Run Backward Pass (loss.backward())</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            When you call <code>loss.backward()</code> on the final scalar Loss, PyTorch walks backward through the recorded graph using the Chain Rule and stores the exact derivative $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w}$ inside <strong className="text-white"><code>w.grad</code></strong>!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">
            loss.backward()  # Populates w.grad!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/15 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-4 text-lg font-bold text-white">Under the Hood: Leaf Tensors vs. grad_fn Pointers</h3>
        <div className="space-y-4 text-sm">
          <div>
            Suppose $x = 2$, weight $w = 3$ (<code>requires_grad=True</code>), bias $b = 1$ (<code>requires_grad=True</code>), and we compute $y = wx + b$ followed by $L = (y - 10)^2$:
          </div>
          <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">Leaf Tensors (w and b)</div>
              <div>Created directly by the user. They are the leaves of the graph where PyTorch accumulates <code>w.grad</code> ($-12.0$) and <code>b.grad</code> ($-6.0$).</div>
            </div>
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-violet-300">Intermediate Tensors (y and L)</div>
              <div>Created by math ops. Each stores a <code>.grad_fn</code> pointer (like <code>&lt;AddBackward0&gt;</code> or <code>&lt;PowBackward0&gt;</code>) telling Autograd which derivative formula to run!</div>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Intermediate: Why Gradients Accumulate (zero_grad) &amp; Disabling Autograd
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Every time you call <code>loss.backward()</code>, PyTorch <strong className="text-orange-300">ADDS (accumulates)</strong> the new gradient into <code>w.grad</code> instead of overwriting it:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-orange-400/20 bg-orange-400/[0.04] px-6 py-6 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$\text{w.grad}_{\text{new}} = \text{w.grad}_{\text{old}} + \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w}$$</div>
      </div>
      <div>
        <strong className="text-white">Why did PyTorch design it this way?</strong> Because of the <strong className="text-white">Multivariable Chain Rule</strong> (when a weight is used in two places, its gradients must sum) AND so engineers with small GPUs can run <strong className="text-cyan-300">Gradient Accumulation</strong> (running $4$ small micro-batches of size $8$ before updating weights to simulate a large batch of $32$)!
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-rose-300">optimizer.zero_grad()</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Resets all <code>.grad</code> buffers back to $0$ before the next training step so old gradients do not corrupt your new batch.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-emerald-300">with torch.no_grad():</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Temporarily turns off graph recording during <strong className="text-white">validation, testing, and manual weight updates</strong>—cutting GPU memory usage in half!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-cyan-300">tensor.detach()</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Returns a new tensor sharing the same data, but <strong className="text-white">detached from the computational graph</strong> (needed before converting to NumPy/Matplotlib).
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          Suppose <code>w = torch.tensor(3.0, requires_grad=True)</code> and $L = w^2$ (so $\dfrac{dL}{dw} = 2w = 6$). If you accidentally call <code>L = w ** 2; L.backward()</code> THREE times in a row without clearing gradients, what value will be stored inside <code>w.grad</code>?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) 18.0 (because 6.0 + 6.0 + 6.0 accumulates!)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>

            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>Every call to <code>.backward()</code> adds $+6.0$ onto the existing <code>w.grad</code> buffer ($6 + 6 + 6 = 18.0$). Always clear gradients between steps using <code>w.grad.zero_()</code> or <code>optimizer.zero_grad()</code>.</span>
            </div>
          </details>

            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) 6.0 (because backward overwrites w.grad)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>PyTorch never overwrites <code>.grad</code> automatically during <code>.backward()</code>; it always accumulates via addition.</span>

        </div>
      </div>

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Advanced: Memory Strides (.contiguous), In-Place Traps, and Mixed Precision
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        When building production Transformers and training loops, three advanced PyTorch mechanics are essential to master:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Memory Strides: .view() vs .contiguous()</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            When you transpose a tensor (<code>x.transpose(1, 2)</code>), PyTorch does not move numbers in memory—it only swaps the metadata <strong className="text-white">strides</strong>! Calling <code>.view()</code> on a transposed tensor crashes because the memory is no longer contiguous.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">
            Fix: x.transpose(1, 2).contiguous().view(...)  # Or use .reshape()
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-rose-300">2. The In-Place Operation Trap (add_, mul_)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            In PyTorch, any method ending with an underscore (like <code>x.add_(1)</code> or <code>x += 1</code>) modifies the tensor <strong className="text-white">in-place</strong>, overwriting its original memory. Doing this on an active autograd tensor destroys the forward values needed for the Chain Rule!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 font-mono text-xs text-rose-300">
            RuntimeError: one of the variables needed for gradient computation has been modified by an inplace operation
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <strong className="text-white">3. Advanced LLM Speedup (Mixed Precision <code>bfloat16</code> &amp; <code>torch.inference_mode()</code>):</strong> Modern GPUs (Ampere/Hopper Tensor Cores) multiply 16-bit Brain Floating Point (<code>torch.bfloat16</code>) matrices up to $4\times$ faster than 32-bit floats while using half the VRAM. For production inference, prefer <code>with torch.inference_mode():</code> over <code>torch.no_grad()</code>—it completely disables view tracking and version counters for maximum speed!
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          When writing a custom Gradient Descent weight update on a leaf tensor <code>w</code> (<code>requires_grad=True</code>), why must you wrap the update inside <code>with torch.no_grad(): w -= lr * w.grad</code> instead of writing <code>w = w - lr * w.grad</code> outside <code>no_grad</code>?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) So the weight update itself is not recorded in the graph, keeping w as a leaf tensor</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>

              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>Writing <code>w = w - lr * w.grad</code> with autograd enabled creates a brand-new non-leaf tensor connected to the old graph, leaking memory across steps and breaking future <code>w.grad</code> access!</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) Because PyTorch cannot subtract two tensors without no_grad</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>PyTorch subtracts tensors normally during the forward pass; we use <code>torch.no_grad()</code> during optimizer updates specifically so the optimizer step is not tracked as part of the neural network&apos;s forward math.</span>

        Look at how PyTorch builds the <strong className="text-cyan-300">Dynamic Computational Graph</strong> on the fly during the Forward Pass, attaches a <code>grad_fn</code> backward pointer to every intermediate tensor, and walks backward on <code>loss.backward()</code> to populate the leaf gradients <code>W.grad</code> and <code>b.grad</code>[cite: 12]:
      </div>
      <Mermaid chart="graph LR; W[Leaf Tensor W: requires_grad=True] -->|MatMul: Z = X @ W| Z[Tensor Z: grad_fn=MmBackward0]; B[Leaf Tensor b: requires_grad=True] -->|Add: A = Z + b| A[Tensor A: grad_fn=AddBackward0]; Z -->|Add: A = Z + b| A; A -->|MSE: L = mean of A - Y squared| L[Scalar Loss L: grad_fn=MseLossBackward0]; L -->|loss.backward walks graph in reverse| WGrad[Populates W.grad and b.grad];" />
    </div>

      <div className="space-y-6">
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Forgetting to zero out gradients between batches (missing zero_grad).
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            If you do not clear <code>.grad</code> after updating weights, the next batch&apos;s gradients are added onto the old batch&apos;s gradients, causing weights to overshoot wildly.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Appending the raw loss tensor (history.append(loss)) instead of loss.item().
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Storing a tensor that has a <code>grad_fn</code> inside a Python list prevents Python&apos;s garbage collector from freeing the entire forward activation graph, quickly crashing your GPU with an Out-Of-Memory error.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Mixing CPU and GPU tensors in the same operation.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Both the model parameters and the input batch must live on the exact same device (e.g., <code>batch = batch.to(device)</code>) before running the forward pass.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Calling .view() directly after .transpose() without .contiguous() or .reshape().
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Transposing a tensor changes its stride metadata without rearranging physical memory. Call <code>.contiguous().view(...)</code> or simply <code>.reshape(...)</code> to avoid stride errors.
