    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Vectors: The Atom of Machine Learning
    </h2>

    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        A <strong className="text-white">Vector</strong> (written in bold lowercase as $\mathbf{x}$ or with an arrow $\vec{x}$) is a 1-dimensional array of numbers. In mathematics, vectors are written vertically as a <strong className="text-white">column vector</strong> by default:
      </div>

      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-violet-400/20 bg-violet-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        $$\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1800 \\ 3 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3$$
      </div>

      <div>
        The notation $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ means that $\mathbf{x}$ is a vector containing $3$ real numbers (a 3-dimensional vector). There are two complementary ways to think about what a vector actually represents:
      </div>

      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-400/20 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">

          </div>
        </div>

        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.05] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-emerald-300">2. Geometric View (Arrow in Space)</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            A vector is a <strong className="text-white">point or arrow in space</strong> starting from the origin $(0,0)$. It has both a <strong className="text-white">magnitude</strong> (length) and a <strong className="text-white">direction</strong>. Items with similar meanings point in the same direction in space.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Essential Vector Operations and Norms
    </h2>

    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Whenever a model adjusts its weights or combines features, it uses three basic vector operations:
      </div>

      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">Vector Addition</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-400">Add corresponding elements together (requires identical lengths).</div>
          <div className="rounded bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
          </div>
        </div>

        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-blue-300">Scalar Multiplication</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-400">Scales (stretches or shrinks) the vector by a single number $c$.</div>
          <div className="rounded bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            $$3 \times \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$$
          </div>
        </div>

        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-violet-300">Hadamard Product ($\odot$)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-400">Element-wise multiplication of two same-sized vectors.</div>
          <div className="rounded bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            $$\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} \odot \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 8 \\ 15 \end{bmatrix}$$
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">Vector Norms: Measuring Length and Magnitude</h3>
      <div>
        How "big" is a vector? In Machine Learning, we measure a vector's length using a <strong className="text-white">Norm</strong> (denoted with double bars $\|\mathbf{x}\|$). Norms are used everywhere to calculate model errors and prevent weights from exploding (Regularization):
      </div>

      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-400/20 bg-amber-400/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-amber-300">L1 Norm (Manhattan Distance)</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm text-slate-300">
            The sum of the absolute values of the elements. Like walking along a city grid of blocks (powers <strong className="text-white">Lasso Regularization</strong>).
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4 text-center">
            <div>$$\|\mathbf{x}\|_1 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i|$$</div>
            <div className="mt-2 text-xs text-slate-400">For $\mathbf{x} = [3, -4]$: $|3| + |-4| = 7$</div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-400/20 bg-cyan-400/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">L2 Norm (Euclidean Distance)</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm text-slate-300">
            The straight-line distance from the origin using the Pythagorean theorem (powers <strong className="text-white">Ridge Regularization</strong>).
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4 text-center">
            <div>$$\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2}$$</div>
            <div className="mt-2 text-xs text-slate-400">For $\mathbf{x} = [3, -4]$: $\sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. The Dot Product and Cosine Similarity (The Engine of AI)
    </h2>

    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        If there is <strong className="text-white">one single operation</strong> that powers all of modern AI—from Linear Regression to Neural Networks, RAG Vector Databases, and Transformer Attention—it is the <strong className="text-orange-400">Dot Product</strong>.
      </div>

      <div>
        The dot product (written as $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}$ or $\mathbf{a}^T \mathbf{b}$) takes two vectors of equal length, multiplies their matching elements together, and sums them into a <strong className="text-white">single scalar number</strong>:
      </div>

      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-orange-400/20 bg-orange-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        $$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + \dots + a_n b_n$$
      </div>

      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/15 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-4 text-lg font-bold text-white">Step-by-Step Worked Example:</h3>
        <div className="space-y-4 text-sm">
          <div>Suppose we have an input vector $\mathbf{x} = [2, 3, 1]$ and a weight vector $\mathbf{w} = [4, -1, 5]$:</div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/70 p-4 text-center text-base">
            $$\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{x} = (4 \times 2) + (-1 \times 3) + (5 \times 1) = 8 - 3 + 5 = 10$$
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">Geometric Intuition: Measuring Similarity</h3>
      <div>
        Why does AI care so much about the dot product? Because geometrically, the dot product is directly tied to the angle $\theta$ between two vectors:
      </div>

      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-6 py-6 text-center text-lg">
        $$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\|_2 \|\mathbf{b}\|_2 \cos(\theta) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{Cosine Similarity} = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{a}\|_2 \|\mathbf{b}\|_2}$$
      </div>

      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-1 text-lg font-bold text-emerald-300">Positive ($\cos\theta \approx 1$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Angle $\theta \approx 0^\circ$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Vectors point in the <strong className="text-white">same direction</strong>. E.g., embeddings for <em>"King"</em> and <em>"Monarch"</em>.
          </div>
        </div>

        <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-600/40 bg-slate-800/30 p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-1 text-lg font-bold text-slate-200">Zero ($\cos\theta = 0$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Angle $\theta = 90^\circ$ (Orthogonal)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Vectors are perpendicular and <strong className="text-white">completely unrelated</strong>. E.g., <em>"Pizza"</em> and <em>"Quantum Physics"</em>.
          </div>
        </div>

        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-1 text-lg font-bold text-rose-300">Negative ($\cos\theta \approx -1$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Angle $\theta \approx 180^\circ$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Vectors point in <strong className="text-white">opposite directions</strong>. They represent opposing features or concepts.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">

              When vectors have unit length, their dot product equals $\cos(\theta)$. A score of $0.92$ (close to $1$) means the vectors point in almost the exact same direction in high-dimensional space, indicating high semantic similarity.

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Matrices: Datasets and Neural Network Layers
    </h2>

    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        While a vector holds a single data sample, a <strong className="text-white">Matrix</strong> (denoted in bold uppercase like $\mathbf{X}$ or $\mathbf{W}$) stacks multiple vectors into a 2D rectangular grid with $m$ <strong className="text-cyan-300">rows</strong> and $n$ <strong className="text-violet-300">columns</strong> (written as shape $m \times n$).
      </div>

      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-cyan-400/20 bg-cyan-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center">
        <div>$$\mathbf{X}_{3 \times 2} = \begin{bmatrix} x_{1,1} & x_{1,2} \\ x_{2,1} & x_{2,2} \\ x_{3,1} & x_{3,2} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1500 & 3 \\ 2200 & 4 \\ 900 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
        <div className="mt-4 text-xs text-slate-400">
          In ML datasets: Each <strong className="text-cyan-300">Row ($m=3$)</strong> is a house sample. Each <strong className="text-violet-300">Column ($n=2$)</strong> is a feature (SqFt, Bedrooms).
        </div>
      </div>

      <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">Critical Matrix Operations</h3>

      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Matrix Transpose ($\mathbf{A}^T$)</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm text-slate-300">
            Flips a matrix over its main diagonal—turning its <strong className="text-white">rows into columns</strong> and columns into rows. An $(m \times n)$ matrix becomes $(n \times m)$.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-4 text-center text-sm">
            $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix}^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
          </div>
        </div>

        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">2. The Identity Matrix ($\mathbf{I}$)</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm text-slate-300">
            The matrix equivalent of the number $1$. It is a square matrix with $1$s along the main diagonal and $0$s everywhere else ($\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I} = \mathbf{A}$).
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-4 text-center text-sm">
            $$\mathbf{I}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-pink-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      6. Matrix Multiplication (The Shape Compatibility Rule)
    </h2>

    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Matrix multiplication ($\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}$) is <strong className="text-white">not</strong> multiplying matching positions together. Instead, every entry $c_{i,j}$ in the output matrix is the <strong className="text-pink-400">dot product</strong> of <strong className="text-cyan-300">Row $i$ from $\mathbf{A}$</strong> and <strong className="text-violet-300">Column $j$ from $\mathbf{B}$</strong>.
      </div>

      <div className="my-8 rounded-2xl border border-pink-500/25 bg-slate-950/80 p-6 text-center shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(236,72,153,0.06)]">
        <div className="mb-4 text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-[0.2em] text-pink-400">
          The Golden Shape Rule of Matrix Multiplication
        </div>
        <div className="flex flex-wrap items-center justify-center gap-3 font-mono text-lg sm:text-2xl">
          <span className="rounded-lg border border-cyan-500/30 bg-cyan-500/10 px-3 py-2 text-cyan-300">

            ✕ Confusing element-wise multiplication (*) with matrix multiplication (@).

          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            In NumPy and PyTorch, the asterisk operator (<code>A * B</code>) performs Hadamard element-wise multiplication, whereas the <code>@</code> operator (<code>A @ B</code>) computes the true linear algebra matrix product.
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Assuming matrix multiplication is commutative (A @ B = B @ A).
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Order matters strictly in matrix multiplication. Swapping $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ changes the mathematical result completely or triggers a runtime shape mismatch crash.
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Multiplying matrices with mismatched inner dimensions without transposing.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Attempting to multiply two $(32 \times 128)$ matrices directly fails because $128 \neq 32$. You must transpose the second matrix to $(128 \times 32)$ first so the inner dimensions align.
          </div>
        </div>

        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Using unnormalized dot products to compare documents of different lengths.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Raw dot products scale with vector magnitude ($\|\mathbf{a}\|$). For semantic search and RAG retrieval, always normalize vectors to unit length (Cosine Similarity) so direction matters rather than document word count.
