  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. What is a Linear Transformation?
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        In algebra, a function $f(x)$ takes a scalar number as input and outputs another number. In Linear Algebra, a <strong className="text-white">Transformation</strong> $T(\mathbf{x})$ is a function that takes an entire <strong className="text-blue-300">input vector $\mathbf{x}$</strong> and maps it to a new <strong className="text-emerald-300">output vector $\mathbf{y}$</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/20 bg-blue-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$T(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}$$</div>
      </div>
      <div>
        Why is it called <strong className="text-white">Linear</strong>? Visually, a transformation is strictly linear if and only if it obeys two geometric rules:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-400/20 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-blue-300">Rule 1: Grid Lines Remain Parallel</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            All straight lines in space stay straight after the transformation—they never curve or bend. Furthermore, parallel grid lines remain <strong className="text-white">parallel and evenly spaced</strong>.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm text-cyan-300">
            <div>$$T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.05] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-emerald-300">Rule 2: The Origin Remains Fixed</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The center of the coordinate space $(0, 0)$ never shifts. If you pass a zero vector into a matrix multiplication, you always get the zero vector back.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm text-emerald-300">
            <div>$$T(c\mathbf{u}) = cT(\mathbf{u}) \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{A}\mathbf{0} = \mathbf{0}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <strong className="text-white">AI Connection (Why Neural Networks Need Bias $\mathbf{b}$):</strong> Because pure matrix multiplication $\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}$ locks the origin at $(0,0)$, a neural network layer adds a bias vector $\mathbf{b}$ to create an <strong className="text-cyan-300">Affine Transformation</strong> ($\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}$), allowing the decision boundary to shift away from the origin.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. The Secret of Basis Vectors: Reading Any Matrix Instantly
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        How can you look at a $2 \times 2$ matrix like $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and immediately see what it does to space? The secret lies in the <strong className="text-white">Standard Basis Vectors</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/30 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-1 text-base font-bold text-cyan-300">Horizontal Unit Vector ($\hat{i}$)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-400">Points 1 unit right along the X-axis</div>
          <div className="rounded-lg border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-3 text-sm">
            <div>$$\hat{i} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-pink-500/30 bg-pink-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-1 text-base font-bold text-pink-300">Vertical Unit Vector ($\hat{j}$)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-400">Points 1 unit up along the Y-axis</div>
          <div className="rounded-lg border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-3 text-sm">
            <div>$$\hat{j} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div>
        Every vector $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}$ is just a sum of scaled basis vectors: $x\hat{i} + y\hat{j}$. When you multiply a matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ by $\mathbf{x}$, look at what happens to the columns:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-violet-400/20 bg-violet-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center">
        <div>$$\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = x \begin{bmatrix} a \\ c \end{bmatrix} + y \begin{bmatrix} b \\ d \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
        <div className="mt-4 grid gap-3 text-xs text-slate-300 sm:grid-cols-2">
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950/80 p-3">
            <strong className="text-cyan-300">Column 1 $\begin{bmatrix} a \\ c \end{bmatrix}$</strong> is literally where the X-axis basis vector $\hat{i} = [1, 0]^T$ lands!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950/80 p-3">
            <strong className="text-pink-300">Column 2 $\begin{bmatrix} b \\ d \end{bmatrix}$</strong> is literally where the Y-axis basis vector $\hat{j} = [0, 1]^T$ lands!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          A 2D linear transformation moves the horizontal basis vector $\hat{i} = [1, 0]^T$ to $[2, -1]^T$, and moves the vertical basis vector $\hat{j} = [0, 1]^T$ to $[0, 4]^T$. Without doing any algebra, what is the $2 \times 2$ transformation matrix $\mathbf{A}$?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) First col [2, -1], Second col [0, 4]</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>The columns of any transformation matrix are simply the landing coordinates of the basis vectors $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$, giving $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) First row [2, -1], Second row [0, 4]</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Transformed basis vectors always populate the columns of the matrix, not the rows.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. The Visual Catalog of Geometric Matrix Transformations
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        By changing the entries of a matrix $\mathbf{A}$, we can perform specific geometric operations on data points (such as image augmentation in Computer Vision or feature projection in Transformers):
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 sm:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-emerald-300">1. Scaling (Stretch / Shrink)</span>
            <span className="font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Diagonal Matrix</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-300">
            Stretches X by factor $s_x$ and Y by factor $s_y$. Off-diagonal zeros mean axes do not mix.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} s_x & 0 \\ 0 & s_y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-500/25 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-blue-300">2. Shear (Slanting Space)</span>
            <span className="font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Upper Triangular</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-300">
            Keeps $\hat{i}$ fixed at $[1,0]^T$, but pushes $\hat{j}$ sideways to $[k, 1]^T$, slanting the grid like italics.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & k \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1.5 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-violet-300">3. Counter-Clockwise Rotation</span>
            <span className="font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Orthogonal Matrix</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-300">
            Rotates every vector by angle $\theta$ around the origin without changing lengths (powers <strong className="text-white">RoPE</strong> in LLMs!).
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\mathbf{R}(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-amber-300">4. Reflection &amp; Projection</span>
            <span className="font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Rank-Deficient</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-xs text-slate-300">
            Flips space across an axis or squashes 2D space onto a 1D line by zeroing out a dimension.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\mathbf{P}_{\text{x-axis}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Two Ways to Compute Matrix Multiplication
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        When multiplying an $(m \times k)$ matrix $\mathbf{A}$ by a $(k \times n)$ matrix $\mathbf{B}$ to produce $\mathbf{C}$ of shape $(m \times n)$, mathematicians and AI engineers use two complementary mental models:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-6 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-2xl border border-orange-400/20 bg-orange-400/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-orange-300">1. The Row-by-Column Dot Product View</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Each individual cell $c_{i,j}$ is computed by taking the <strong className="text-white">dot product</strong> of Row $i$ from $\mathbf{A}$ and Column $j$ from $\mathbf{B}$.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-4 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$c_{i,j} = \sum_{p=1}^{k} a_{i,p} b_{p,j}$$</div>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-3 text-xs text-slate-400">
            Best for: Computing single entries by hand and checking feature-to-neuron similarity scores.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-2xl border border-cyan-400/20 bg-cyan-400/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-cyan-300">2. The Column Combination View</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Each column of $\mathbf{C}$ is a <strong className="text-white">linear combination of the columns of $\mathbf{A}$</strong>, weighted by the numbers in that column of $\mathbf{B}$.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-4 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\mathbf{A}\mathbf{b}_j = b_{1,j}\mathbf{a}_{:,1} + b_{2,j}\mathbf{a}_{:,2} + \dots + b_{k,j}\mathbf{a}_{:,k}$$</div>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-3 text-xs text-slate-400">
            Best for: Understanding how Attention blends Value vectors and how Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) works.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/15 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-4 text-lg font-bold text-white">Step-by-Step Worked Example (Both Views Yield the Same Answer):</h3>
        <div className="space-y-4 text-sm">
          <div>Multiply $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ by vector $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$:</div>
          <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4 text-center">
              <div className="mb-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase text-orange-300">Row Dot-Product Method</div>
              <div>$$\begin{bmatrix} (1\cdot 5 + 3\cdot 2) \\ (2\cdot 5 + 4\cdot 2) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 + 6 \\ 10 + 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 11 \\ 18 \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
            </div>
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4 text-center">
              <div className="mb-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase text-cyan-300">Column Combination Method</div>
              <div>$$5 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + 2 \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 10 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 11 \\ 18 \end{bmatrix}$$</div>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Matrix Multiplication as Function Composition
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Why do we multiply two matrices $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{A}$ together? Because if matrix $\mathbf{A}$ applies one transformation (say, a <strong className="text-cyan-300">Rotation</strong>) and matrix $\mathbf{B}$ applies a second transformation (say, a <strong className="text-violet-300">Shear</strong>), then the product matrix $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}$ is a <strong className="text-white">single combined transformation</strong> that performs both in sequence!
      </div>
      <div className="my-8 rounded-2xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-slate-950/80 p-6 text-center">
        <div className="mb-3 text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-[0.2em] text-cyan-400">
          Right-to-Left Execution Order (Just Like Nested Functions)
        </div>
        <div className="my-4 text-lg sm:text-xl">
          <div>$$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}$$</div>
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm text-slate-300">
          Because $\mathbf{x}$ is on the right, <strong className="text-cyan-300">Matrix $\mathbf{A}$ acts on $\mathbf{x}$ FIRST</strong>, and <strong className="text-violet-300">Matrix $\mathbf{B}$ acts on the result SECOND</strong>.
        </div>
      </div>
      <div>
        This immediately explains why <strong className="text-white">matrix multiplication is NOT commutative ($\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A} \neq \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}$)</strong>: rotating an image by $90^\circ$ and then shearing it produces a completely different geometric result than shearing it first and rotating it second. However, it IS <strong className="text-white">associative</strong>: $(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{B})\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A})$.
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          Suppose you stack 3 linear neural network layers $\mathbf{W}_1$, $\mathbf{W}_2$, and $\mathbf{W}_3$ directly after one another WITHOUT any non-linear activation functions (like ReLU) in between: $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}_3(\mathbf{W}_2(\mathbf{W}_1\mathbf{x}))$. What happens mathematically?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) All 3 layers collapse into a single matrix W_total</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>By associativity, $\mathbf{W}_3(\mathbf{W}_2(\mathbf{W}_1\mathbf{x})) = (\mathbf{W}_3\mathbf{W}_2\mathbf{W}_1)\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{single}}\mathbf{x}$. Without non-linear activations (ReLU/GELU) between matrices, a 100-layer deep network has the exact same expressive power as a 1-layer linear model!</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) It learns complex non-linear curves</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Multiplying linear matrices together always produces another strictly linear matrix. You must insert non-linear activation functions between matrix multiplications to bend space and learn curves.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-pink-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      6. Dimensionality Changes: Expanding and Compressing Space
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Matrices do not have to be square! A non-square matrix $\mathbf{W}$ of shape $(m \times n)$ maps vectors from <strong className="text-pink-300">$n$-dimensional input space ($\mathbb{R}^n$)</strong> into <strong className="text-cyan-300">$m$-dimensional output space ($\mathbb{R}^m$)</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-pink-500/25 bg-pink-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-pink-300">Tall Matrix ($m &gt; n$): Up-Projection</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm text-slate-300">
            Maps low-dimensional inputs into a higher-dimensional feature space. In Transformer Feed-Forward networks, hidden vectors of size $768$ are multiplied by a $(3072 \times 768)$ matrix to expand into $3072$ dimensions.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-pink-300">
            Shape: (3072, 768) · (768, 1) = (3072, 1)
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">Wide Matrix ($m &lt; n$): Down-Projection</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm text-slate-300">
            Compresses high-dimensional vectors down into a lower-dimensional bottleneck (used in Autoencoders, PCA, and Multi-Head Attention query projections).
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">
            Shape: (64, 768) · (768, 1) = (64, 1)
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">Determinants, Rank, and Invertibility</h3>
      <div>
        When a square matrix $\mathbf{A}$ transforms space, how much does the area (or volume) of a region scale? That scaling factor is called the <strong className="text-white">Determinant</strong>, written $\det(\mathbf{A})$:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-6 py-6 text-center">
        <div>$$\det\left(\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}\right) = ad - bc$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-emerald-300">$\det(\mathbf{A}) \neq 0$ (Full Rank)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Space is stretched or rotated without being squashed flat. The transformation is <strong className="text-white">reversible</strong> via the Inverse Matrix $\mathbf{A}^{-1}$ ($\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{I}$).
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-amber-300">$\det(\mathbf{A}) &lt; 0$ (Flipped Space)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The orientation of space is inverted (like flipping a sheet of paper over), while areas scale by $|\det(\mathbf{A})|$.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-rose-300">$\det(\mathbf{A}) = 0$ (Singular)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Columns are linearly dependent! Space is squashed into a lower dimension (e.g., 2D plane collapsed onto a 1D line). <strong className="text-white">No inverse exists</strong>; information is permanently lost.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          Consider the matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$. Notice that Column 2 is simply $2 \times$ Column 1. What is $\det(\mathbf{A})$, and can you recover an input vector $\mathbf{x}$ after multiplying $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}$?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) det(A) = 0; Impossible to invert (Information Lost)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>$\det(\mathbf{A}) = (2)(2) - (4)(1) = 0$. Because both basis vectors land on the exact same line, the entire 2D plane is crushed onto a 1D line. Infinitely many inputs $\mathbf{x}$ map to the same output $\mathbf{y}$, so $\mathbf{A}^{-1}$ does not exist.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) det(A) = 8; Fully invertible via A^-1</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Remember the formula is $ad - bc = (2 \times 2) - (4 \times 1) = 0$, not $ad + bc$. Whenever one column is a multiple of another, the determinant is zero and the matrix is non-invertible (singular).</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-rose-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      Common Mistakes
    </h2>
    <div className="rounded-2xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-950/[0.15] p-6 sm:p-8">
      <div className="space-y-6">
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Reading matrix transformations from left to right instead of right to left.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            In the expression $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}$, matrix $\mathbf{A}$ multiplies $\mathbf{x}$ first, then $\mathbf{B}$, and finally $\mathbf{C}$. Reversing this order produces an entirely different geometric transformation.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Stacking linear layers without non-linear activation functions.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Because matrix multiplication is associative, stacking $\mathbf{W}_2(\mathbf{W}_1\mathbf{x})$ without an activation function in between simply collapses into a single matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{combined}}\mathbf{x}$, preventing the network from learning non-linear patterns.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Calling np.linalg.inv(A) directly in production ML code.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Explicitly inverting large matrices is slow ($\mathcal{O}(n^3)$) and numerically unstable when $\det(\mathbf{A})$ is close to zero. Always use linear system solvers like <code>np.linalg.solve(A, b)</code> or gradient-based optimization instead.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Confusing mathematical column-vector notation (W @ x) with batched row-vector code (X @ W.T).
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Textbooks treat a single sample as a column vector $(d, 1)$, whereas PyTorch and NumPy stack batches as rows $(B, d)$. Always check whether your weight matrix needs to be transposed when moving from paper equations to code.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
