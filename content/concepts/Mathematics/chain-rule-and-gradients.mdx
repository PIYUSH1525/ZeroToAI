  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Why Do We Need the Chain Rule? (Composite Functions)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        In basic algebra, a function maps an input $x$ directly to an output $y = f(x)$. In Deep Learning, however, operations are stacked inside one another as <strong className="text-white">Composite Functions</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/20 bg-blue-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$L(w) = f\big(g(h(w))\big)$$</div>
      </div>
      <div>
        Think of this as a <strong className="text-blue-300">gear train</strong> or a chain of dominoes:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-cyan-300">Step 1: Linear Layer</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">$z = wx + b$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Weight $w$ directly controls the pre-activation logit $z$.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-violet-300">Step 2: Activation</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">$a = \sigma(z)$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Logit $z$ controls the non-linear neuron activation $a$.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-rose-300">Step 3: Loss Function</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">$L = (a - y)^2$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Activation $a$ controls the final prediction error $L$.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div>
        Notice the problem: the weight $w$ does <strong className="text-white">not</strong> appear directly inside the Loss formula $L = (a - y)^2$! To know how nudging $w$ changes the Loss $L$, we must multiply the sensitivities across every link in the chain.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. The Single-Variable Chain Rule (Multiplying Local Slopes)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        If variable $w$ affects $z$, and $z$ affects $a$, and $a$ affects $L$, the <strong className="text-white">Chain Rule</strong> states that the overall rate of change $\dfrac{dL}{dw}$ is the product of each intermediate step&apos;s rate of change:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-violet-400/20 bg-violet-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$\dfrac{dL}{dw} = \dfrac{dL}{da} \cdot \dfrac{da}{dz} \cdot \dfrac{dz}{dw}$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <strong className="text-white">The Gear Ratio Analogy:</strong> Suppose Gear A turns <strong className="text-cyan-300">$3\times$ faster</strong> than your hand ($\dfrac{dz}{dw} = 3$), Gear B turns <strong className="text-violet-300">$2\times$ faster</strong> than Gear A ($\dfrac{da}{dz} = 2$), and Gear C turns <strong className="text-emerald-300">$5\times$ faster</strong> than Gear B ($\dfrac{dL}{da} = 5$). How fast does Gear C turn when you move your hand? Exactly $5 \times 2 \times 3 = 30\times$ faster!
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/15 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-4 text-lg font-bold text-white">Step-by-Step Worked Example (A 1-Neuron Network):</h3>
        <div className="space-y-4 text-sm">
          <div>Suppose input $x = 2$, weight $w = 3$, bias $b = 1$, and true target $y = 10$. Let us use a simple linear activation $a = z$ with Squared Loss $L = (a - y)^2$:</div>
          <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Forward Pass (Compute Values)</div>
              <div>$$z = wx + b = (3)(2) + 1 = 7$$</div>
              <div className="mt-2">$$L = (z - y)^2 = (7 - 10)^2 = (-3)^2 = 9$$</div>
            </div>
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-rose-300">2. Backward Pass (Chain Rule)</div>
              <div>$$\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z} = 2(z - y) = 2(-3) = -6$$</div>
              <div className="mt-2">$$\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial w} = x = 2 \implies \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w} = (-6)(2) = -12$$</div>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check</span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          During backpropagation, a neuron computes $z = wx + b$ with input $x = 4$. It receives an upstream gradient from the next layer of $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z} = -2.5$. What is the weight gradient $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w}$ and the bias gradient $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial b}$?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) dL/dw = -10.0 and dL/db = -2.5</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>Since $\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial w} = x = 4$ and $\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial b} = 1$, multiplying by the upstream gradient $-2.5$ gives $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w} = (-2.5)(4) = -10.0$ and $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial b} = (-2.5)(1) = -2.5$.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) dL/dw = 1.5 and dL/db = -2.5</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>The Chain Rule multiplies the upstream gradient by the local derivative ($-2.5 \times 4 = -10.0$), rather than adding them ($-2.5 + 4 = 1.5$).</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. The Multivariable Chain Rule (When Branches Split and Merge)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        What happens when a single parameter or activation feeds into <strong className="text-white">multiple downstream pathways</strong>? For example, in a Transformer or ResNet, an embedding vector $\mathbf{x}$ splits into both a <strong className="text-cyan-300">Skip Connection (Residual Path)</strong> and a <strong className="text-violet-300">Sub-Layer Path</strong>, which later merge back together.
      </div>
      <div>
        The <strong className="text-emerald-300">Multivariable Chain Rule (Total Derivative Rule)</strong> states that when a variable $x$ influences the Loss $L$ through multiple parallel paths $u_1(x)$ and $u_2(x)$, you <strong className="text-white">SUM the gradients from all paths</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial x} = \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial u_1}\dfrac{\partial u_1}{\partial x} + \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial u_2}\dfrac{\partial u_2}{\partial x} = \sum_{k} \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial u_k}\dfrac{\partial u_k}{\partial x}$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">Rule 1: Gradients in Series MULTIPLY</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            When operations happen one after another in a single line ($w \to z \to a \to L$), their local derivatives <strong className="text-white">multiply</strong> together.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">Rule 2: Gradients in Parallel ADD</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            When a variable fans out into multiple branches that all affect the Loss, the gradients flowing back from those branches <strong className="text-white">add</strong> together at the split point!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <strong className="text-white">Why Residual Connections ($\mathbf{y} = F(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$) Saved Deep Learning:</strong> By the multivariable chain rule, the gradient of a Residual block is $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \cdot \left(\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial \mathbf{x}} + 1\right)$. Even if the layer gradient $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$ shrinks to $0$, the $+1$ from the skip branch guarantees the upstream gradient $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{y}}$ flows backward unharmed!
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Computational Graphs and Local Gate Patterns
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Deep learning frameworks like PyTorch and JAX break every complex formula down into a <strong className="text-white">Computational Graph</strong> made of tiny elementary gates. Every gate only needs to know its own local inputs and the <strong className="text-orange-300">Upstream Gradient</strong> arriving from the right:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full min-w-[720px] border-collapse text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.06] text-white">
            <tr>
              <th className="border-b border-white/10 px-5 py-4 font-semibold">Gate Type</th>
              <th className="border-b border-white/10 px-5 py-4 font-semibold">Forward Formula</th>
              <th className="border-b border-white/10 px-5 py-4 font-semibold">Backward Rule (Given Upstream $g = \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z}$)</th>
              <th className="border-b border-white/10 px-5 py-4 font-semibold">Intuitive Behavior</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="text-slate-300">
            <tr className="border-b border-white/10">
              <td className="px-5 py-4 font-semibold text-emerald-300">Add Gate (+)</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4">$z = x + y$</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4">$\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial x} = 1 \cdot g, \quad \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial y} = 1 \cdot g$</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4"><strong className="text-white">Gradient Distributor:</strong> Copies upstream gradient equally to both branches.</td>
            </tr>
            <tr className="border-b border-white/10">
              <td className="px-5 py-4 font-semibold text-cyan-300">Multiply Gate (*)</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4">$z = x \cdot y$</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4">$\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial x} = y \cdot g, \quad \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial y} = x \cdot g$</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4"><strong className="text-white">Swap-Multiplier:</strong> Multiplies upstream gradient by the <em>other</em> input&apos;s forward value.</td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
              <td className="px-5 py-4 font-semibold text-violet-300">Max Gate (ReLU)</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4">$z = \max(x, y)$</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4">$g$ to larger input, $0$ to smaller</td>
              <td className="px-5 py-4"><strong className="text-white">Gradient Router:</strong> Routes $100\%$ of gradient to the winning input and $0$ to the loser.</td>
            </tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          A Multiply Gate computes $z = x \cdot y$ where $x = 3$ and $y = -4$ (so $z = -12$). During backpropagation, the upstream gradient arriving at $z$ is $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z} = 5$. What are the gradients $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial x}$ and $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial y}$?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) dL/dx = -20 and dL/dy = 15</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>A Multiply Gate acts as a swap-multiplier: $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial x} = y \cdot \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z} = (-4)(5) = -20$, and $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial y} = x \cdot \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z} = (3)(5) = 15$.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) dL/dx = 15 and dL/dy = -20</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Remember that $\dfrac{\partial (xy)}{\partial x} = y$ (not $x$). You must multiply the upstream gradient by the <em>other</em> input variable!</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. The Gradient Vector, Contour Lines, and Directional Derivatives
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Once the Chain Rule has computed the partial derivative $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w_i}$ for every parameter in our network, we assemble them into the <strong className="text-cyan-300">Gradient Vector $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} L$</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-cyan-400/20 bg-cyan-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} L = \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w_1} &amp; \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w_2} &amp; \dots &amp; \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w_d} \end{bmatrix}^T \in \mathbb{R}^d$$</div>
      </div>
      <div>
        Why does the Gradient Vector always point in the direction of steepest ascent? Because of the <strong className="text-white">Directional Derivative</strong> formula! If you take a small step in any unit direction $\mathbf{u}$ ($\Vert{}\mathbf{u}\Vert{}_2 = 1$), the rate of change of the Loss in that direction is the <strong className="text-cyan-300">dot product</strong> of the gradient with $\mathbf{u}$:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-6 py-6 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$D_{\mathbf{u}} L = \nabla L \cdot \mathbf{u} = \|\nabla L\|_2 \|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \cos(\theta) = \|\nabla L\|_2 \cos(\theta)$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-rose-300">$\theta = 0^\circ$ ($\cos\theta = +1$)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Stepping <strong className="text-white">along $+\nabla L$</strong> maximizes the dot product, giving the <strong className="text-rose-300">steepest uphill increase</strong> in error.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-600/40 bg-slate-800/30 p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-slate-200">$\theta = 90^\circ$ ($\cos\theta = 0$)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Stepping <strong className="text-white">perpendicular to $\nabla L$</strong> causes zero change in Loss! This means $\nabla L$ is always <strong className="text-white">orthogonal to contour lines</strong>.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">$\theta = 180^\circ$ ($\cos\theta = -1$)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Stepping <strong className="text-white">along $-\nabla L$</strong> makes $\cos\theta = -1$, giving the <strong className="text-emerald-300">steepest downhill decrease</strong> in error!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-pink-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      6. Vanishing vs. Exploding Gradients (When the Chain Breaks)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Because a deep network with $K$ layers multiplies $K$ local derivatives together via the Chain Rule, repeated multiplication creates two famous failure modes:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-pink-400/20 bg-pink-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center">
        <div>$$\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{h}_1} = \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{h}_K} \cdot \prod_{j=2}^{K} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf{h}_j}{\partial \mathbf{h}_{j-1}}$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-amber-300">1. Vanishing Gradients ($\left\vert{}\dfrac{\partial h_j}{\partial h_{j-1}}\right\vert{} \lt 1$)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            If each layer&apos;s local derivative is around $0.25$ (like Sigmoid), then across $20$ layers the gradient shrinks to $0.25^{20} \approx 10^{-12} \approx 0$. Early layers receive zero signal and freeze completely!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-xs text-emerald-300">
            <strong>Modern Fixes:</strong> ReLU / GELU activations, Residual Skip Connections, and LayerNorm.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-rose-300">2. Exploding Gradients ($\left\vert{}\dfrac{\partial h_j}{\partial h_{j-1}}\right\vert{} \gt 1$)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            If each layer&apos;s local derivative is around $2.0$, then across $20$ layers the gradient balloons to $2^{20} \approx 1{,}048{,}576$, causing weights to overflow into <code>NaN</code> in a single step!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-xs text-emerald-300">
            <strong>Modern Fixes:</strong> Gradient Clipping (<code>clip_grad_norm_</code>), Kaiming/Xavier initialization, and AdamW.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check</span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          Why does a 50-layer neural network built with Sigmoid activations fail to train its early layers, whereas a 50-layer network with ReLU and Residual Connections ($\mathbf{y} = F(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$) trains smoothly?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) Sigmoid derivatives (&le; 0.25) multiply to ~0; Skip connections add a +1 gradient highway</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>By the Chain Rule, multiplying fifty numbers smaller than $0.25$ crushes the gradient to zero. ReLU has a derivative of $1$ for positive inputs, and Residual blocks add $+1$ to the local derivative via the parallel-branch sum rule.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) Sigmoid cannot output positive numbers</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Sigmoid outputs strictly positive numbers between $0$ and $1$. Its flaw in deep hidden layers is that its <em>derivative</em> $\sigma^\prime(z)$ is at most $0.25$, causing repeated Chain Rule multiplications to vanish.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-indigo-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      7. Visual Explanation: The Chain Rule Computational Graph
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Look at how a 2-layer neural network executes the <strong className="text-cyan-300">Forward Pass</strong> from left to right to cache intermediate activations, and then executes the <strong className="text-rose-300">Backward Pass (Reverse-Mode Autodiff)</strong> from right to left by multiplying local derivatives:
      </div>
      <Mermaid chart="graph LR; W1[Layer 1 Weights W1] -->|z1 = W1 * x| H1[Hidden ReLU h1]; H1 -->|z2 = W2 * h1| Y[Prediction yHat]; Y -->|L = MSE| L[Loss L]; L -->|Upstream dL/dyHat| Y; Y -->|dL/dW2 and dL/dh1| H1; H1 -->|Chain Rule: dL/dW1| W1;" />

  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      8. Python Implementation: 2-Layer Backpropagation From Scratch
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Here is a complete, runnable NumPy implementation of the <strong className="text-white">Matrix Chain Rule</strong> across a 2-layer neural network ($\mathbf{X} \to \mathbf{W}_1 \to \text{ReLU} \to \mathbf{W}_2 \to \text{Loss}$), showing how shapes and transposes align perfectly during backpropagation:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
        <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
          <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">chain_rule_backprop.py</span>
          <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3 · NumPy</span>
        </div>
        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">import numpy as np</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 1. Setup Input Batch X (4 samples, 3 features) and Targets Y (4, 1)</span>
            <span className="block">np.random.seed(42)</span>
            <span className="block">X = np.random.randn(4, 3)</span>
            <span className="block">Y = np.array([[1.0], [0.0], [1.0], [0.0]])</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># Initialize Layer 1 (3 -&gt; 5 neurons) and Layer 2 (5 -&gt; 1 output)</span>
            <span className="block">W1 = np.random.randn(3, 5) * 0.5</span>
            <span className="block">W2 = np.random.randn(5, 1) * 0.5</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 2. FORWARD PASS (Cache intermediates for the Chain Rule)</span>
            <span className="block">Z1 = X @ W1                                 # Shape: (4, 5)</span>
            <span className="block">H1 = np.maximum(0, Z1)                      # ReLU Activation: (4, 5)</span>
            <span className="block">YHat = H1 @ W2                              # Final Prediction: (4, 1)</span>
            <span className="block">loss = np.mean((YHat - Y) ** 2)             # Mean Squared Error</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 3. BACKWARD PASS (Matrix Chain Rule from Output to Input)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">dYHat = (2.0 / len(X)) * (YHat - Y)         # dL/dYHat -&gt; Shape: (4, 1)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">dW2 = H1.T @ dYHat                          # dL/dW2 = H1^T @ dYHat -&gt; (5, 1)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">dH1 = dYHat @ W2.T                          # Pass gradient to Layer 1 -&gt; (4, 5)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">dZ1 = dH1 * (Z1 &gt; 0)                        # Multiply by ReLU local derivative</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">dW1 = X.T @ dZ1                             # dL/dW1 = X^T @ dZ1 -&gt; (3, 5)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;Initial Loss:&quot;, round(float(loss), 4))</span>

        <strong className="text-teal-300">Pro Tip (The Golden Shape Invariant of Gradients):</strong> Notice that <code>W1</code> has shape <code>(3, 5)</code> and its gradient <code>dW1</code> also has shape <code>(3, 5)</code>! A parameter&apos;s gradient tensor <strong>must always have the exact same shape as the parameter itself</strong>. If you ever forget whether to transpose <code>X.T @ dZ1</code> or <code>dZ1 @ X.T</code>, just match the outer shapes!

      Key Points
    </h2>
    <div className="rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#0B0E17] p-6 sm:p-8">
      <div className="space-y-4 text-[15px] leading-7 text-slate-200">
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>The Chain Rule computes the derivative of nested composite functions by multiplying local derivatives along the path ($\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial a}\dfrac{\partial a}{\partial z}\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial w}$).</span>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>When a variable splits into multiple parallel paths that later merge (such as Residual skip connections), the multivariable Chain Rule sums the gradients from all branches.</span>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>In computational graphs, Add gates distribute upstream gradients equally, Multiply gates swap and multiply inputs, and Max/ReLU gates route gradients to the winning input.</span>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>The Gradient Vector $\nabla L$ is always orthogonal (perpendicular) to loss contour lines and points in the direction of steepest ascent; $-\nabla L$ points toward steepest descent.</span>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>Repeatedly multiplying local derivatives smaller than $1$ across deep layers causes Vanishing Gradients, while multiplying derivatives larger than $1$ causes Exploding Gradients.</span>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>For any weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$ of shape $(m \times n)$, its gradient $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{W}}$ always has the exact same shape $(m \times n)$.</span>
        </div>
      </div>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-rose-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      Common Mistakes
    </h2>
    <div className="rounded-2xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-950/[0.15] p-6 sm:p-8">
      <div className="space-y-6">
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Discarding forward-pass activations before running the backward pass.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Because the local derivative of a linear layer $\mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}$ with respect to $\mathbf{W}$ is $\mathbf{X}^T$, backpropagation requires the forward activations $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{H}_1$ to be stored in GPU memory until gradients are computed.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Forgetting to sum gradients when a tensor is used in two places.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Whenever a tensor branches into two operations (like Query/Key/Value projections or skip connections), overwriting the gradient instead of accumulating (`+=`) violates the multivariable Chain Rule.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Forgetting to zero out accumulated gradients between training steps in PyTorch.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Because PyTorch automatically sums gradients on `.backward()` to support branching graphs and gradient accumulation, failing to call `optimizer.zero_grad()` mixes old gradients from previous batches into the current step.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Mismatching matrix transpose order during manual backpropagation.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            For $\mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}$, the weight gradient is always $\mathbf{X}^T \cdot \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{Z}}$ and the input gradient is $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{Z}} \cdot \mathbf{W}^T$. Always verify that the output shape matches the target tensor&apos;s shape.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
