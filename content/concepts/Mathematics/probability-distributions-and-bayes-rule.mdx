  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Why AI Speaks in Probabilities (Random Variables)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        When ChatGPT generates the next word or a medical AI scans an X-ray, it does not simply output a hard-coded label. Instead, it models a <strong className="text-white">Random Variable $X$</strong> and assigns a probability score $P(X = x)$ between $0$ and $1$ to every possible outcome:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/20 bg-blue-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$0 \le P(X = x) \le 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{x} P(X = x) = 1$$</div>
      </div>
      <div>
        To read any machine learning paper or loss function, you must distinguish between the three fundamental types of probability:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Marginal Probability</div>
          <div className="mb-3 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">$P(A)$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The unconditional probability of a single event occurring on its own (e.g., the baseline percentage of all emails that are spam, $P(\text{Spam}) = 0.20$).
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-violet-300">2. Joint Probability</div>
          <div className="mb-3 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">$P(A, B)$ or $P(A \cap B)$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The probability that <strong className="text-white">both</strong> event $A$ and event $B$ happen together (e.g., an email is spam AND contains the word &quot;Lottery&quot;).
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-emerald-300">3. Conditional Probability</div>
          <div className="mb-3 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">$P(A \mid B)$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The probability of $A$ happening <strong className="text-white">given that we already know $B$ occurred</strong>:
          </div>
          <div className="mt-3 rounded bg-slate-950 p-2 text-center text-xs">
            <div>$$P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{P(A, B)}{P(B)}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <strong className="text-white">AI Connection:</strong> Every supervised classification model and LLM is literally a <strong className="text-cyan-300">Conditional Probability Engine</strong>! Given input features $\mathbf{x}$, the model computes $P(y \mid \mathbf{x})$—the conditional probability of label $y$ given input $\mathbf{x}$.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Expectation and Variance (Summarizing Distributions)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Before looking at specific probability distributions, we need two numbers that summarize any distribution&apos;s <strong className="text-white">center</strong> and <strong className="text-white">spread</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-violet-300">1. Expected Value / Mean ($\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]$)</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm text-slate-300">
            The probability-weighted average of all possible values. It represents the center of gravity of the distribution:
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-4 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_{x} x \cdot P(X = x)$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">2. Variance ($\sigma^2 = \text{Var}(X)$)</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm text-slate-300">
            Measures how far values spread out from the mean $\mu$. Standard deviation $\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Var}(X)}$ puts this spread back in the original units:
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-4 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\text{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\big[(X - \mu)^2\big] = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          When an autoregressive Large Language Model (like GPT-4) is given the prompt &quot;The cat sat on the&quot; and predicts the next token &quot;mat&quot;, what mathematical expression is its output Softmax layer computing?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) Conditional Probability: P(next_token | previous_tokens)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>An LLM computes the conditional probability distribution $P(w_t \mid w_1, w_2, \dots, w_{t-1})$ over its entire vocabulary, conditioned on the preceding tokens in the context window.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) Marginal Probability: P(next_token)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Marginal probability $P(w_t)$ ignores the prompt completely and would just output the most common word in the English language (&quot;the&quot;) every single time.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. The Four Essential Probability Distributions of AI
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        A <strong className="text-white">Probability Distribution</strong> is a mathematical law that describes how total probability ($1.0$) is distributed across all possible outcomes. Discrete variables use a <strong className="text-emerald-300">Probability Mass Function (PMF)</strong>, while continuous variables use a <strong className="text-cyan-300">Probability Density Function (PDF)</strong>. Four distributions power almost all of Machine Learning:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 sm:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-emerald-300">1. Bernoulli Distribution</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-emerald-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-emerald-300">Discrete (Binary)</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Models a single binary coin flip $y \in \{0, 1\}$ with success probability $p$. Powers <strong className="text-white">Logistic Regression</strong> and <strong className="text-white">Sigmoid Binary Classification</strong>!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$P(y \mid p) = p^y (1 - p)^{1 - y}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-violet-300">2. Categorical (Multinoulli)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-violet-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-violet-300">Discrete ($K$ Classes)</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Models rolling a $K$-sided die with class probabilities $\mathbf{p} = [p_1, \dots, p_K]$ where $\sum p_k = 1$. Powers <strong className="text-white">Softmax Multi-Class Classification</strong> and <strong className="text-white">LLM Token Sampling</strong>!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$P(y = k) = \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{z})_k = \dfrac{e^{z_k}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K} e^{z_j}}$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-cyan-300">3. Gaussian (Normal) Distribution</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-cyan-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">Continuous Bell Curve</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Defined entirely by its mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. Powers <strong className="text-white">Linear Regression noise</strong>, <strong className="text-white">Weight Initialization</strong>, <strong className="text-white">LayerNorm</strong>, and <strong className="text-white">Diffusion Models</strong>!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\mathcal{N}(x \mid \mu, \sigma^2) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\left(-\dfrac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-amber-300">4. Uniform Distribution</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-amber-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-amber-300">Continuous Flat</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Every value in the interval $[a, b]$ is equally likely. Used for <strong className="text-white">Dropout masks</strong>, <strong className="text-white">Random Hyperparameter Search</strong>, and <strong className="text-white">Exploration in RL</strong>.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center text-sm">
            <div>$$\mathcal{U}(x \mid a, b) = \dfrac{1}{b - a} \quad \text{for } x \in [a, b]$$</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Bayes&apos; Rule: How AI Updates Beliefs From Evidence
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Often we know how likely a symptom or word is given a class—$P(\text{Evidence} \mid \text{Hypothesis})$—but what we actually want to predict is the reverse: <strong className="text-white">given the observed evidence, how likely is the hypothesis?</strong> <strong className="text-orange-400">Bayes&apos; Theorem</strong> flips conditional probabilities around:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-orange-400/20 bg-orange-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$P(H \mid E) = \dfrac{P(E \mid H) \cdot P(H)}{P(E)}$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 sm:grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-4">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-4">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold text-emerald-300">Posterior: $P(H \mid E)$</div>
          <div className="text-xs text-slate-300">Updated belief in hypothesis $H$ <em>after</em> seeing evidence $E$.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-4">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold text-cyan-300">Likelihood: $P(E \mid H)$</div>
          <div className="text-xs text-slate-300">Probability of observing evidence $E$ if $H$ were true.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.05] p-4">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold text-violet-300">Prior: $P(H)$</div>
          <div className="text-xs text-slate-300">Initial baseline belief in $H$ <em>before</em> seeing any evidence.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-600/40 bg-slate-800/30 p-4">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold text-slate-200">Evidence: $P(E)$</div>
          <div className="text-xs text-slate-300">Total probability of $E$ across all possible hypotheses.</div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/15 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-4 text-lg font-bold text-white">Step-by-Step Worked Example (The Base Rate Medical / Spam Trap):</h3>
        <div className="space-y-4 text-sm">
          <div>
            Suppose $20\%$ of all emails are Spam ($P(\text{Spam}) = 0.20$) and $80\%$ are Normal ($P(\text{Ham}) = 0.80$). The word <strong className="text-white">&quot;Free&quot;</strong> appears in $50\%$ of Spam emails ($P(\text{Free} \mid \text{Spam}) = 0.50$), and in $5\%$ of Normal emails ($P(\text{Free} \mid \text{Ham}) = 0.05$). If a new email contains the word &quot;Free&quot;, what is the probability it is Spam?
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-5 text-center">
            <div>$$P(\text{Free}) = P(\text{Free} \mid \text{Spam})P(\text{Spam}) + P(\text{Free} \mid \text{Ham})P(\text{Ham}) = (0.50)(0.20) + (0.05)(0.80) = 0.10 + 0.04 = 0.14$$</div>
            <div className="mt-4">$$P(\text{Spam} \mid \text{Free}) = \dfrac{0.10}{0.14} \approx 0.714 \text{ (or } 71.4\%\text{)}$$</div>
          </div>
          <div>
            Seeing the word &quot;Free&quot; jumped our belief that the email is Spam from a <strong className="text-violet-300">$20\%$ Prior</strong> up to a <strong className="text-emerald-300">$71.4\%$ Posterior</strong>!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          A rare condition affects $1\%$ of people ($P(D) = 0.01$). An AI screening test has a $90\%$ true positive rate ($P(+ \mid D) = 0.90$) and a $9\%$ false positive rate on healthy people ($P(+ \mid \text{Healthy}) = 0.09$). If a random person tests positive, what is the actual Posterior probability $P(D \mid +)$?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) Only ~9.2% (because the Prior is so rare!)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>By Bayes&apos; Rule: $P(D \mid +) = \dfrac{(0.90)(0.01)}{(0.90)(0.01) + (0.09)(0.99)} = \dfrac{0.009}{0.009 + 0.0891} \approx 9.17\%$. Ignoring the $1\%$ Prior is the famous <em>Base Rate Fallacy</em>.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) 90%</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>$90\%$ is the Likelihood $P(+ \mid D)$, not the Posterior $P(D \mid +)$. Confusing $P(E \mid H)$ with $P(H \mid E)$ ignores the fact that $99\%$ of the population is healthy.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE): Why We Use MSE &amp; Cross-Entropy
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Have you ever wondered <em>why</em> Linear Regression uses <strong className="text-white">Mean Squared Error (MSE)</strong> while Classification and LLMs use <strong className="text-white">Cross-Entropy Loss</strong>? Neither formula was invented by accident—both come directly from <strong className="text-cyan-300">Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)</strong>!
      </div>
      <div>
        Given a dataset of $N$ independent training examples, MLE chooses the model weights $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ that <strong className="text-white">maximize the joint probability of observing the true training labels</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-cyan-400/20 bg-cyan-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center">
        <div>$$\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \prod_{i=1}^{N} P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i; \boldsymbol{\theta})$$</div>
      </div>
      <div>
        Because multiplying thousands of tiny probabilities (like $0.01 \times 0.02 \times \dots$) causes numerical underflow to $0.0$ in floating-point hardware, we take the <strong className="text-white">Natural Logarithm ($\ln$)</strong>—which turns products into sums—and negate it so we can <strong className="text-rose-300">minimize Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL)</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-rose-400/20 bg-rose-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center">
        <div>$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = -\sum_{i=1}^{N} \ln P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i; \boldsymbol{\theta})$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Gaussian Noise $\implies$ MSE Loss</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            If you assume continuous targets have <strong className="text-white">Gaussian noise</strong> $y \sim \mathcal{N}(\hat{y}, \sigma^2)$, taking $-\ln P(y \mid \hat{y})$ cancels the exponential $e^{-(y-\hat{y})^2}$ and leaves <strong className="text-white">Mean Squared Error $(y - \hat{y})^2$</strong>!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-violet-300">2. Bernoulli/Categorical $\implies$ Cross-Entropy</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            If you assume discrete labels follow a <strong className="text-white">Bernoulli or Categorical</strong> distribution, taking $-\ln P(y \mid \hat{y})$ yields <strong className="text-white">Cross-Entropy Loss $-\sum y_k \ln(\hat{y}_k)$</strong>!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          Why do we minimize Negative Log-Likelihood $-\sum \ln P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$ during neural network training instead of directly maximizing the product of probabilities $\prod P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) Logs turn products into sums, preventing float underflow and simplifying derivatives</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>Multiplying thousands of numbers between $0$ and $1$ underflows 32-bit floats to zero. Because $\ln(a \cdot b) = \ln(a) + \ln(b)$ is strictly monotonic, minimizing $-\sum \ln P$ finds the exact same optimal weights safely.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) Because probabilities can be negative</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Probabilities are strictly bounded between $0$ and $1$. Notice however that for $P \in (0, 1)$, $\ln(P)$ is negative, which is why we negate it ($-\ln P$) to get a positive Loss value.</span>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      7. Python Implementation: Softmax Distributions, Bayes&apos; Rule &amp; NLL
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Here is a complete NumPy implementation showing how to turn raw logits into a temperature-scaled probability distribution, compute Cross-Entropy (NLL) loss, and run a Bayesian belief update:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
        <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
          <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">probability_and_bayes.py</span>
          <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3 · NumPy</span>
        </div>
        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">import numpy as np</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 1. Numerically Stable Softmax (Logits -&gt; Categorical Distribution)</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def softmax(logits, temperature=1.0):</span>
            <span className="block">    scaled = logits / temperature</span>
            <span className="block">    shifted = scaled - np.max(scaled)       # Subtract max for numerical stability</span>
            <span className="block">    expScores = np.exp(shifted)</span>
            <span className="block">    return expScores / np.sum(expScores)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">logits = np.array([2.0, 1.0, 0.1])</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">probs = softmax(logits, temperature=1.0)</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;Categorical Probabilities:&quot;, np.round(probs, 4))  # [0.659, 0.2424, 0.0986]</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;Sum of Probabilities:&quot;, round(float(np.sum(probs)), 2))  # 1.0</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 2. Maximum Likelihood / Negative Log-Likelihood (Cross-Entropy Loss)</span>
            <span className="block">trueClassIndex = 0                          # Ground-truth token is index 0</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">nllLoss = -np.log(probs[trueClassIndex])</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;Negative Log-Likelihood Loss:&quot;, round(float(nllLoss), 4))  # 0.417</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 3. Bayes Rule Posterior Update (Spam Filter Example)</span>
            <span className="block">priorSpam = 0.20                            # P(Spam)</span>
            <span className="block">priorHam = 0.80                             # P(Ham)</span>
            <span className="block">likelihoodFreeGivenSpam = 0.50              # P(&quot;Free&quot; | Spam)</span>
            <span className="block">likelihoodFreeGivenHam = 0.05               # P(&quot;Free&quot; | Ham)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">evidenceFree = (likelihoodFreeGivenSpam * priorSpam) + (likelihoodFreeGivenHam * priorHam)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">posteriorSpam = (likelihoodFreeGivenSpam * priorSpam) / evidenceFree</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;Posterior P(Spam | Free):&quot;, round(posteriorSpam, 4))     # 0.7143</span>
          </code>
        </pre>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-teal-500/20 bg-teal-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-7 text-slate-300">
        <strong className="text-teal-300">Pro Tip (LLM Temperature Scaling):</strong> Notice the <code>temperature</code> parameter in <code>softmax(logits / temperature)</code>! Lowering temperature ($T \to 0.1$) sharpens the probability distribution so the top token gets $\approx 99\%$ probability (deterministic/greedy), while raising temperature ($T \to 1.5$) flattens the distribution toward a Uniform distribution (more creative/random sampling).

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-rose-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      Common Mistakes
    </h2>
    <div className="rounded-2xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-950/[0.15] p-6 sm:p-8">
      <div className="space-y-6">
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Confusing P(A | B) with P(B | A) (The Prosecutor&apos;s / Base Rate Fallacy).
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The probability of a positive test given a rare disease $P(+ \mid D)$ is completely different from the probability of having the disease given a positive test $P(D \mid +)$. Always weight by the Prior $P(D)$ using Bayes&apos; Rule.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Assuming a continuous Gaussian PDF value f(x) is a probability bounded by 1.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            For continuous random variables, $f(x)$ is a probability <em>density</em> (height of the curve), which can exceed $1.0$ when $\sigma$ is very small. Only the area under the curve over an interval $\int_a^b f(x)dx$ is a probability.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Assuming features are conditionally independent when they are heavily correlated.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The Naive Bayes classifier assumes $P(x_1, x_2 \mid y) = P(x_1 \mid y)P(x_2 \mid y)$. When input features are duplicate or strongly correlated, Naive Bayes double-counts evidence and produces overconfident probabilities near $0$ or $1$.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Computing Softmax without subtracting the maximum logit first.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            In Python, <code>np.exp(1000)</code> overflows to <code>inf</code> and produces <code>NaN</code> probabilities. Always shift logits by subtracting <code>np.max(logits)</code> before exponentiating.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
