  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Medium: How AI Learns Embeddings Automatically (Word2Vec &amp; GloVe)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Here is the million-dollar question: if nobody types in the slider numbers by hand, <strong className="text-white">how does the computer figure out that &quot;coffee&quot; and &quot;tea&quot; should have similar numbers?</strong>
      </div>
      <div>
        By playing a simple <strong className="text-emerald-300">&quot;Fill-in-the-Blank&quot; guessing game</strong> across millions of sentences! Think about these two sentences:
        <br />• <em>&quot;I poured a hot cup of <strong className="text-cyan-300">coffee</strong> this morning.&quot;</em>
        <br />• <em>&quot;I poured a hot cup of <strong className="text-violet-300">tea</strong> this morning.&quot;</em>
      </div>
      <div>
        Notice that the surrounding neighbor words (<em>&quot;poured&quot;, &quot;hot&quot;, &quot;cup&quot;, &quot;morning&quot;</em>) are identical! If a neural network is trained to <strong className="text-white">guess a word from its neighbors</strong>, it is forced to give <em>&quot;coffee&quot;</em> and <em>&quot;tea&quot;</em> almost the exact same embedding vectors so both words predict the same neighbors!
      </div>
      <div>
        In 2013, Google released <strong className="text-emerald-300">Word2Vec</strong>, which uses this exact neighbor-guessing game in two ways:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. CBOW (Guess the Center Word)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            <strong className="text-white">Continuous Bag of Words:</strong> Give the model the surrounding neighbor words (<em>&quot;The&quot;, &quot;cat&quot;, &quot;on&quot;, &quot;the&quot;</em>) and train it to guess the missing middle word (<strong className="text-cyan-300">&quot;sat&quot;</strong>).
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">
            Neighbors → Guess Middle Word
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">2. Skip-Gram (Guess the Neighbors)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            Flips the game around: give the model just the middle word (<strong className="text-emerald-300">&quot;sat&quot;</strong>) and train it to guess the words sitting next to it (<em>&quot;cat&quot;, &quot;on&quot;</em>). Works especially well for rare words!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-emerald-300">
            Middle Word → Guess Neighbors
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">
          The Clever Speed Trick: Negative Sampling (True Pairs vs. Fake Pairs)
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          Predicting a neighbor out of all $100{,}000$ words in a dictionary is slow. So Word2Vec uses a shortcut called <strong className="text-cyan-300">Negative Sampling</strong>: it shows the model $1$ <strong className="text-emerald-300">real word pair</strong> from the text (like <code>(&quot;orange&quot;, &quot;juice&quot;) → True</code>) and $5$ <strong className="text-rose-300">random fake pairs</strong> (like <code>(&quot;orange&quot;, &quot;tractor&quot;) → False</code>). The model simply nudges real pairs closer together on the map and pushes fake pairs apart!
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-violet-300">GloVe (Stanford, 2014)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Instead of scanning one sentence at a time, <strong className="text-white">GloVe (Global Vectors)</strong> counts how often every pair of words appears near each other across the <em>entire</em> encyclopedia first, and trains vectors to match those global counts.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-amber-300">FastText (Meta AI, 2016)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Learns vectors for <strong className="text-white">3-letter chunks</strong> (like <code>&quot;tea&quot;</code>, <code>&quot;eac&quot;</code>, <code>&quot;ach&quot;</code> inside <em>&quot;teacher&quot;</em>) and adds them up—so even if a user makes a typo, FastText can still build a good vector for it!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Advanced: Static vs. Contextual Embeddings (How ChatGPT &amp; RAG Work)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Now let us bridge from classic embeddings (Word2Vec/GloVe) to modern AI (<strong className="text-white">BERT, GPT-4, Llama-3, and Vector Databases</strong>).
      </div>
      <div>
        Classic Word2Vec had one big blind spot: <strong className="text-rose-300">it gave every word only ONE fixed vector in the table</strong>, even when the same spelling has two completely different meanings! Look at the word <strong className="text-white">&quot;apple&quot;</strong> in these two sentences:
        <br />1. <em>&quot;I ate a sweet red <strong className="text-emerald-300">apple</strong> for lunch.&quot;</em> (The fruit)
        <br />2. <em>&quot;<strong className="text-cyan-300">Apple</strong> released a new iPhone today.&quot;</em> (The tech company)
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-6">
