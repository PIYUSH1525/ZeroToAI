  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Beginner: The &quot;Surprise-O-Meter&quot; (What is Perplexity?)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Before we ask an AI to translate or summarize, how do we check if it understands basic English grammar and flow? We use a score called <strong className="text-cyan-300">Perplexity (PPL)</strong>.
      </div>
      <div>
        In plain English, the word <em>&quot;perplexed&quot;</em> means <strong className="text-white">confused or surprised</strong>. Think of Perplexity as a <strong className="text-cyan-300">Surprise-O-Meter</strong>: we hide the next word in a real human sentence and ask the AI how many different words it is torn between:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-emerald-300">Low Perplexity (Score = 2 to 10)</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The AI reads <em>&quot;Peanut butter and ___&quot;</em> and is $90\%$ sure the next word is <strong className="text-white">&quot;jelly&quot;</strong>. It is barely surprised at all! <strong className="text-emerald-300">Lower Perplexity = Smarter Model.</strong>
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-emerald-300">
            PPL = 3 → Torn between only ~3 plausible words!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-rose-300">High Perplexity (Score = 500+)</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            An untrained AI reads <em>&quot;Peanut butter and ___&quot;</em> and thinks <em>&quot;tractor&quot;</em>, <em>&quot;cloud&quot;</em>, and <em>&quot;jelly&quot;</em> are all equally likely. It is totally confused!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-rose-300">
            PPL = 500 → Blindly guessing among 500 words!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/20 bg-blue-400/[0.04] px-6 py-6 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$\text{Perplexity} = e^{\text{Cross-Entropy Loss}} = \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \ln P(w_t \mid \text{context})\right)$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">
          The Golden Rule of Perplexity
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          If an AI predicts every word with $100\%$ certainty ($P = 1.0$), its Perplexity is the best possible score of <strong className="text-emerald-300">$1.0$</strong>. If it is guessing randomly among $10$ choices, its Perplexity is <strong className="text-amber-300">$10.0$</strong>. Just remember: <strong className="text-white">like golf, a lower Perplexity score always wins!</strong>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Beginner: BLEU vs. ROUGE (Grading Translations &amp; Summaries)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Perplexity only tells us if the AI is confident—it does not compare the AI&apos;s written output against a <strong className="text-white">Human Answer Key (Reference Text)</strong>.
      </div>
      <div>
        In the early 2000s, researchers created two sibling metrics that grade an AI by counting <strong className="text-violet-300">how many words and short phrases (N-grams) overlap</strong> between the AI&apos;s sentence and the Human Reference sentence:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-cyan-300">1. BLEU (The Strict Translator)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-cyan-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">Focus: Precision</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Used for <strong className="text-white">Machine Translation</strong>. Asks: <em>&quot;Out of all the words the AI wrote, what percentage actually appear in the human reference?&quot;</em> Penalizes the AI if it invents extra nonsense words!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">
            Matching Words / Total Words AI Wrote
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-violet-300">2. ROUGE (The Summary Checklist)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-violet-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-violet-300">Focus: Recall</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Used for <strong className="text-white">Text Summarization</strong>. Asks: <em>&quot;Out of all the key words in the human summary checklist, what percentage did the AI remember to include?&quot;</em>
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-violet-300">
            Matching Words / Total Words in Human Reference
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/15 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-3 text-lg font-bold text-white">Why BLEU Needs a &quot;Brevity Penalty&quot; (Stopping the 1-Word Cheater!)</h3>
        <div className="space-y-3 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          <div>
            Suppose the human reference translation is: <strong className="text-white">&quot;The cat sat on the mat.&quot;</strong> ($6$ words).
          </div>
          <div>
            What if a lazy AI translates that sentence as just one single word: <strong className="text-rose-300">&quot;The&quot;</strong>? Since $1$ out of $1$ words written by the AI appears in the reference, its raw Precision is $1 / 1 = 100\%$! To block this loophole, <strong className="text-cyan-300">BLEU multiplies the score by a Brevity Penalty</strong> that slashes the grade whenever the AI&apos;s output is shorter than the human reference!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check</span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          An easy way to remember BLEU vs. ROUGE is by looking at what they care about most. Which metric focuses on Recall (making sure no key points from the reference were left out of a summary)?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) ROUGE (Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>The &quot;R&quot; in ROUGE literally stands for <strong>Recall</strong>—it checks how much of the human reference summary was captured by the AI.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) Perplexity</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Perplexity only measures token prediction probabilities; it does not compare a generated summary against a reference checklist.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        BLEU and ROUGE have one massive blind spot: <strong className="text-rose-300">they only check exact word spelling, not meaning!</strong> Look at what happens when we compare these two sentences:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-5">
          <div className="text-xs font-mono uppercase text-slate-400">Human Answer Key</div>
          <div className="mt-1 text-base font-bold text-white">&quot;The <span className="text-cyan-300">kids</span> were <span className="text-emerald-300">thrilled</span>.&quot;</div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-5">
          <div className="text-xs font-mono uppercase text-slate-400">AI Output</div>
          <div className="mt-1 text-base font-bold text-white">&quot;The <span className="text-cyan-300">children</span> were <span className="text-emerald-300">happy</span>.&quot;</div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div>
        A human teacher gives that AI an <strong className="text-emerald-300">A+ ($100\%$)</strong> because <em>&quot;kids = children&quot;</em> and <em>&quot;thrilled = happy&quot;</em>. But what do BLEU and ROUGE-2 give it? <strong className="text-rose-300">A big fat $0.0$!</strong> Because not a single 2-word phrase is spelled the same way!
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
        <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-emerald-300">The Fix: BERTScore (Grading with Embeddings!)</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          Instead of checking if words are spelled the same, <strong className="text-white">BERTScore</strong> passes both sentences through a pretrained Transformer (like BERT or RoBERTa) to get their <strong className="text-cyan-300">Contextual Word Embeddings</strong>, and measures the <strong className="text-emerald-300">Cosine Similarity</strong> between the vectors! Because <code>Vec(&quot;kids&quot;)</code> and <code>Vec(&quot;children&quot;)</code> live right next to each other on the map of meaning, BERTScore gives the AI a <strong className="text-white">$0.96$</strong>!
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">

          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">

          </div>

          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Never grade code with BLEU! <strong className="text-white">pass@k</strong> gives the AI $k$ tries to write a function, <strong className="text-emerald-300">actually runs the code</strong> against unit tests, and checks if at least $1$ try passes!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check</span>
        </div>

              <span>Two programs can use completely different variable names and loops while solving the problem perfectly, or differ by a single <code>+</code> vs <code>-</code> sign and fail completely. Running unit tests via <code>pass@k</code> tests actual execution!</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
