  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Beginner: Why Word Order Matters &amp; How an RNN Works (The Sticky-Note Loop)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Imagine trying to read a mystery book through a tiny keyhole where you can only see <strong className="text-white">one word at a time</strong>—and the moment a new word appears, your brain completely wipes the previous word from memory! You would have no idea what the story is about.
      </div>
      <div>
        That is how a normal Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) works: it takes a fixed input, spits out an answer, and immediately forgets everything.
      </div>
      <div>
        A <strong className="text-cyan-300">Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)</strong> fixes this with a dead-simple trick: as it reads each word, it keeps a <strong className="text-white">Running Summary Note</strong> (called the <strong className="text-violet-300">Hidden State $h_t$</strong>) and passes that note forward to the next step!
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-cyan-300">1. New Word ($x_t$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Current word embedding</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The word the AI is looking at right now (for example, Word #3: <em>&quot;not&quot;</em>).
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-violet-300">2. Old Memory ($h_{t-1}$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Summary from step $t-1$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The sticky-note summary passed over from all the words read so far (<em>&quot;The movie is...&quot;</em>).
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-emerald-300">3. Updated Memory ($h_t$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Blend old note + new word</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Combines the old note and the new word into a fresh summary note to pass to Word #4!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/20 bg-blue-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$h_t = \tanh\left( W_{xh} x_t + W_{hh} h_{t-1} + b \right)$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">
          Plain-English Translation of That Formula
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          Do not let the letters scare you! It literally says: <strong className="text-emerald-300">New Memory ($h_t$)</strong> = squash together (<strong className="text-cyan-300">New Word $x_t$</strong> + <strong className="text-violet-300">Old Memory $h_{t-1}$</strong>). Even better, the RNN uses the <strong className="text-white">exact same weight matrices ($W_{xh}, W_{hh}$)</strong> at every single word step, whether the sentence has $5$ words or $500$ words!
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Beginner: Why Simple RNNs Forget Long Sentences (The Game of Telephone)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Simple RNNs work great on short 4-word phrases. But what happens when an RNN reads a long 40-word paragraph? Look at this sentence:
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-center text-base text-slate-200">
        &quot;I grew up in <strong className="text-cyan-300">France</strong>, where I loved eating fresh croissants by the river every morning, so I speak fluent <strong className="text-emerald-300">______</strong>.&quot;
      </div>
      <div>
        To guess the final word (<strong className="text-emerald-300">&quot;French&quot;</strong>), the AI has to remember the word <strong className="text-cyan-300">&quot;France&quot;</strong> from $18$ words ago! Why does a standard RNN fail at this?
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-rose-300">1. The &quot;Game of Telephone&quot; (Memory Overwriting)</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Because a simple RNN crams the new word into the exact same sticky note at every step, each new word waters down the old memory by half. After $20$ steps, the word <em>&quot;France&quot;</em> has been completely overwritten by words like <em>&quot;river&quot;</em> and <em>&quot;morning&quot;</em>!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-amber-300">2. Vanishing Gradients Through Time</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            During training, blame signals have to walk backward across all $20$ word steps (<strong className="text-white">Backpropagation Through Time</strong>). Multiplying a small fraction like $0.5 \times 0.5 \times \dots$ twenty times shrinks the learning signal to $0.000001$ (<strong className="text-rose-300">Vanishing Gradient</strong>), so the network never learns long-distance links!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">

          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check</span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">

          <details className="group">

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Medium: LSTMs (Long Short-Term Memory) — The Express Conveyor Belt
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        In 1997, Sepp Hochreiter and Jürgen Schmidhuber asked a brilliant question: <em>&quot;Why force the AI to overwrite its only memory note on every single word? What if we give it a separate <strong className="text-emerald-300">Long-Term Vault</strong> that glides straight through the sentence untouched unless a valve opens?&quot;</em>
      </div>
      <div>
        That invention is the <strong className="text-white">LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)</strong>! Instead of one memory note, an LSTM keeps <strong className="text-cyan-300">two memories</strong> and controls them using <strong className="text-violet-300">three smart valves (Gates)</strong> that open ($1.0$) or close ($0.0$) using Sigmoid switches:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 sm:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-emerald-300">The Cell State ($C_t$)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-emerald-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-emerald-300">Long-Term Conveyor Belt</span>
          </div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Think of $C_t$ as an <strong className="text-white">express luggage conveyor belt</strong> running straight across the top of the network. Important facts (like <em>&quot;grew up in France&quot;</em>) ride this belt for $50+$ words without getting squashed or overwritten!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-rose-300">1. The Forget Gate ($f_t$)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-rose-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-rose-300">The Eraser Valve (0 to 1)</span>
          </div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Decides what old info on the conveyor belt is no longer needed. When a sentence ends and a new topic starts, $f_t \to 0$ wipes the old subject clean!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-cyan-300">2. The Input Gate ($i_t$)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-cyan-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">The &quot;Save This!&quot; Valve</span>
          </div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Decides whether the current word is important enough to write onto the long-term conveyor belt (saves <em>&quot;France&quot;</em>; ignores filler words like <em>&quot;the&quot;</em>).
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-violet-300">3. The Output Gate ($o_t$)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-violet-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-violet-300">The Working Memory Valve</span>
          </div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Decides what part of the long-term conveyor belt needs to be pulled out <em>right now</em> into the short-term working memory ($h_t$) to predict the very next word!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.04] px-6 py-6 text-center">
        <div className="mb-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-emerald-300">The Secret of the LSTM Conveyor Belt (Addition Instead of Multiplication!)</div>
        <div className="text-lg">$$C_t = \underbrace{f_t \odot C_{t-1}}_{\text{Keep Old Memory}} + \underbrace{i_t \odot \tilde{C}_t}_{\text{Add New Fact}} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad h_t = o_t \odot \tanh(C_t)$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">
          Why That Plus Sign ($+$) Solved Vanishing Gradients
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          Look at the formula for $C_t$ above: it updates the long-term memory using <strong className="text-emerald-300">addition ($+$)</strong> instead of matrix multiplication! When the Forget Gate stays open ($f_t \approx 1$), memories and backward learning signals glide straight down the highway untouched—just like a ResNet skip connection!
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Medium: GRUs (Gated Recurrent Units) — The Fast, Streamlined Cousin
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        In 2014, Kyunghyun Cho and his team asked: <em>&quot;LSTMs work great, but do we really need TWO separate memory states ($C_t$ and $h_t$) and THREE separate gates? Can we simplify it?&quot;</em>
      </div>
      <div>
        They created the <strong className="text-orange-300">GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit)</strong>, which merges the long-term and short-term memory back into a <strong className="text-white">single vector $h_t$</strong> and uses only <strong className="text-cyan-300">two gates</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-orange-500/25 bg-orange-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-orange-300">1. The Update Gate ($z_t$) — Combined Forget + Input!</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            One single balance knob $(1 - z_t) \odot h_{t-1} + z_t \odot \tilde{h}_t$: if $z_t$ is $0$, keep $100\%$ of the old memory; if $z_t$ is $1$, replace it with $100\%$ of the new word!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">2. The Reset Gate ($r_t$)</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Controls how much of the past memory to consult when proposing a new candidate meaning for the current word.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full min-w-[720px] border-collapse text-left text-sm">

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Advanced: Bidirectional LSTMs &amp; Why Transformers Eventually Replaced Them
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        At the <strong className="text-white">Advanced / Architecture</strong> level, two big questions remain: how do we let an LSTM see the <em>end</em> of a sentence before judging the beginning, and why did ChatGPT switch from LSTMs to Transformers?
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Bidirectional LSTMs (Reading Both Ways!)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            Consider: <em>&quot;<strong className="text-white">Teddy</strong> bears are on sale&quot;</em> vs. <em>&quot;<strong className="text-white">Teddy</strong> Roosevelt was president.&quot;</em> You cannot know if &quot;Teddy&quot; is a toy or a person until you read the <strong className="text-cyan-300">next word</strong>! A <strong className="text-white">Bidirectional LSTM</strong> runs two LSTMs at once—one reading left-to-right ($\overrightarrow{h}_t$) and one reading right-to-left ($\overleftarrow{h}_t$)—and glues their two memories together!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">
            nn.LSTM(..., bidirectional=True) → Doubles Output Size (2 * hidden_size)
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-rose-300">2. The Fatal Bottleneck of LSTMs: Sequential Waiting!</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            Why did Transformers replace LSTMs for Large Language Models? Because an LSTM <strong className="text-white">cannot compute Word #50 until it has finished waiting for Word #1, #2, ..., #49 in a slow loop</strong>! GPUs have $10{,}000+$ parallel cores that sit idle waiting for the loop. Furthermore, cramming a $1{,}000$-word essay into a single fixed-size vector $h_t$ is like trying to pack an entire library into one small suitcase!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-rose-300">
            LSTMs Must Read Step-by-Step → Cannot Parallelize Across GPUs!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">
          Where Are LSTMs, GRUs, and Modern Recurrent Models (Mamba / RWKV) Used Today?
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          Because an LSTM/GRU only needs to keep a tiny, constant-size memory vector $h_t$ in RAM ($\mathcal{O}(1)$ memory per step), they are still widely used in <strong className="text-emerald-300">low-power IoT sensors, smartwatch heart-rate monitors, real-time audio noise cancellation, and robotics</strong>! Even more exciting: a new generation of <strong className="text-cyan-300">Linear Recurrent State-Space Models (like Mamba and RWKV)</strong> removes the non-linear squashing inside the recurrent loop so they can train in parallel like a Transformer while running as fast and cheap as an RNN!
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check</span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">

            <span className="block text-cyan-300">import torch</span>

            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 1. Define a Clean LSTM Sequence Classifier</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">class SentimentLSTM(nn.Module):</span>
            <span className="block">    def __init__(self, vocabSize: int, embedDim: int, hiddenDim: int):</span>

            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.embedding = nn.Embedding(vocabSize, embedDim, padding_idx=0)</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500">        # ALWAYS set batch_first=True so input shape is (Batch, SeqLen, EmbedDim)!</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.lstm = nn.LSTM(embedDim, hiddenDim, batch_first=True)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.classifier = nn.Linear(hiddenDim, 1)</span>

            <span className="block">    def forward(self, tokenIds: torch.Tensor) -&gt; torch.Tensor:</span>
            <span className="block">        # Step 1: Turn Token IDs (B, T) into Word Vectors (B, T, embedDim)</span>
            <span className="block">        x = self.embedding(tokenIds)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">        # Step 2: Read Sequence Left-to-Right!</span>
            <span className="block">        # allSteps has every word&apos;s output; (h_n, c_n) has the FINAL summary notes!</span>

            <span className="block">        finalSummary = hFinal[-1]               # Shape: (Batch, hiddenDim)</span>
            <span className="block">        return self.classifier(finalSummary)    # Shape: (Batch, 1)</span>

        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">

      Common Mistakes
    </h2>
    <div className="rounded-2xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-950/[0.15] p-6 sm:p-8">
      <div className="space-y-6">
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Forgetting batch_first=True in PyTorch&apos;s nn.LSTM or nn.GRU.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Without <code>batch_first=True</code>, PyTorch treats axis $0$ as sequence steps and axis $1$ as batch size, silently scrambling your sentences across the batch! Always set <code>batch_first=True</code>.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Unpacking nn.LSTM output as out, h_n = lstm(x) instead of out, (h_n, c_n) = lstm(x).
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Unlike <code>nn.RNN</code> and <code>nn.GRU</code> (which return one state tensor <code>h_n</code>), <code>nn.LSTM</code> returns a <strong className="text-white">tuple of two tensors <code>(h_n, c_n)</code></strong> as its second output!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Using the final step output out[:, -1, :] when sequences are right-padded with zeros.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            If a short sentence is padded with ten <code>&lt;PAD&gt;</code> tokens on the right, the final step has just read ten fake padding tokens! Either grab the index of the last real token, use <code>pack_padded_sequence</code>, or average across real tokens using a mask.

        </div>
