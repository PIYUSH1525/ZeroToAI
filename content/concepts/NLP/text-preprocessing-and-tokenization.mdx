In <strong className="text-emerald-300">Modern LLMs</strong> (like GPT-4), we <strong className="text-white">DO NOT remove stop words or lowercase text</strong>—because capital letters, punctuation, and words like <em>&quot;not&quot;</em> carry crucial meaning and code syntax!

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Intermediate: How Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) Learns Subwords
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        How does an AI decide which letter combinations should become subword tokens? It uses an elegant data-compression algorithm called <strong className="text-emerald-300">Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE)</strong> (used by GPT-2, GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and Llama-3)!
      </div>
      <div>
        BPE starts with a tiny vocabulary of <strong className="text-white">individual characters (or 256 raw UTF-8 bytes)</strong> and repeatedly merges the <strong className="text-cyan-300">most frequent adjacent pair</strong> until the vocabulary reaches the target size $|\mathcal{V}|$:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$\text{Best Merge Pair } (t_a, t_b) = \arg\max_{(t_i, t_j) \in \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{V}} \text{Count}(t_i, t_j)$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/15 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-4 text-lg font-bold text-white">Step-by-Step Worked Example: Training BPE on a Mini Corpus</h3>
        <div className="space-y-4 text-sm">
          <div>
            Suppose our training text contains the words <code>&quot;low&quot;</code> ($5$ times), <code>&quot;lower&quot;</code> ($2$ times), and <code>&quot;newest&quot;</code> ($6$ times). Watch how BPE builds subwords in 3 iterations:
          </div>
          <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">Iteration 1: Merge (&apos;e&apos;, &apos;s&apos;)</div>
              <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
                In <code>n e w e s t</code> ($6\times$), the pair <code>(&apos;e&apos;, &apos;s&apos;)</code> appears $6$ times. BPE merges them into the new token <strong className="text-white"><code>&quot;es&quot;</code></strong>!
              </div>
            </div>
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-violet-300">Iteration 2: Merge (&apos;es&apos;, &apos;t&apos;)</div>
              <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
                Now <code>(&apos;es&apos;, &apos;t&apos;)</code> appears $6$ times in <code>n e w es t</code>. BPE merges them into the suffix token <strong className="text-white"><code>&quot;est&quot;</code></strong>!
              </div>
            </div>
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">Iteration 3: Merge (&apos;l&apos;, &apos;o&apos;)</div>
              <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
                Across <code>low</code> ($5$) and <code>lower</code> ($2$), the pair <code>(&apos;l&apos;, &apos;o&apos;)</code> appears $5 + 2 = 7$ times, merging into <strong className="text-white"><code>&quot;lo&quot;</code></strong>, and next into <strong className="text-white"><code>&quot;low&quot;</code></strong>!
              </div>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">Comparing Modern Subword Algorithms: BPE vs. WordPiece vs. Unigram</h3>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Byte-Level BPE</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">GPT-4, Llama-3, Claude</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Starts from $256$ raw UTF-8 bytes and merges the highest-frequency pairs bottom-up. Can encode any emoji or language with zero <code>&lt;UNK&gt;</code> tokens!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-violet-300">2. WordPiece</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">BERT, DistilBERT</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Marks subword continuations with <code>##</code> (e.g., <code>[&quot;play&quot;, &quot;##ing&quot;]</code>) and merges pairs that maximize corpus likelihood $\frac{\text{Count}(ab)}{\text{Count}(a)\text{Count}(b)}$.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-emerald-300">3. Unigram (SentencePiece)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">T5, Gemma, Multilingual</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Works top-down: starts with a huge vocabulary and iteratively prunes subwords that least reduce probabilistic model likelihood, treating spaces as <code>_</code> characters.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

      </div>

              <td className="px-5 py-4">Prepended to the start of a prompt to signal the start of a new document and anchor attention.</td>

        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">

To batch them into a $(2, 5)$ tensor, we pad Sentence 2 with two <code>&lt;PAD&gt;</code> tokens: <code>[104, 892, 41, 0, 0]</code>. To prevent Self-Attention from paying attention to fake <code>&lt;PAD&gt;</code> tokens, the tokenizer outputs a matching <strong className="text-emerald-300"><code>attention_mask</code></strong> tensor: <code>[1, 1, 1, 0, 0]</code>!

        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">

        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Advanced: Pre-Tokenization Regex, Fertility, and Why LLMs Struggle to Count Letters
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        At the <strong className="text-white">Advanced / LLM Architecture</strong> level, tokenization explains many of the strangest behaviors, bugs, and cost differences in modern AI systems:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Why LLMs Struggle with &quot;How Many R&apos;s in Strawberry?&quot;</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            An LLM <strong className="text-white">never sees individual letters</strong>! To the tokenizer, <code>&quot;strawberry&quot;</code> is immediately converted into integer IDs like <code>[&quot;str&quot;, &quot;aw&quot;, &quot;berry&quot;] = [496, 675, 15717]</code>. Because the model only sees those 3 numbers, it cannot see the individual <code>&apos;r&apos;</code> characters inside token <code>15717</code> unless it spells the word out letter-by-letter first!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">
            Tokenization Hides Character-Level Spelling!
          </div>
        </div>

    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      7. Python Implementation: Training Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) From Scratch
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Here is a complete, runnable Python implementation of the <strong className="text-white">Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE)</strong> algorithm from scratch—showing how a tokenizer automatically discovers subword units like <code>&quot;ing&quot;</code> and <code>&quot;train&quot;</code> by iteratively merging the most frequent adjacent pairs:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
        <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
          <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">bpe_tokenizer_from_scratch.py</span>
          <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3.11+ · Subword BPE Engine</span>
        </div>
        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">from collections import Counter</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 1. Define a Training Corpus (Word -&gt; Frequency Count)</span>
            <span className="block">rawCorpus = &#123;&quot;train&quot;: 5, &quot;training&quot;: 6, &quot;trained&quot;: 3, &quot;learning&quot;: 4&#125;</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># Split each word into characters + end-of-word symbol &quot;&lt;/w&gt;&quot;</span>
            <span className="block">splits = &#123;word: list(word) + [&quot;&lt;/w&gt;&quot;] for word in rawCorpus&#125;</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def getPairCounts(wordSplits, corpusFreqs):</span>
            <span className="block">    pairCounts = Counter()</span>
            <span className="block">    for word, freq in corpusFreqs.items():</span>
            <span className="block">        symbols = wordSplits[word]</span>
            <span className="block">        for i in range(len(symbols) - 1):</span>
            <span className="block">            pairCounts[(symbols[i], symbols[i + 1])] += freq</span>
            <span className="block">    return pairCounts</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def mergePair(bestPair, wordSplits):</span>
            <span className="block">    a, b = bestPair</span>
            <span className="block">    for word, symbols in wordSplits.items():</span>
            <span className="block">        newSymbols = []</span>
            <span className="block">        i = 0</span>
            <span className="block">        while i &lt; len(symbols):</span>
            <span className="block">            if i &lt; len(symbols) - 1 and symbols[i] == a and symbols[i + 1] == b:</span>
            <span className="block">                newSymbols.append(a + b)</span>
            <span className="block">                i += 2</span>
            <span className="block">            else:</span>
            <span className="block">                newSymbols.append(symbols[i])</span>
            <span className="block">                i += 1</span>
            <span className="block">        wordSplits[word] = newSymbols</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 2. Run 6 BPE Merge Iterations to Discover Subwords Automatically!</span>
            <span className="block">for step in range(1, 7):</span>
            <span className="block">    counts = getPairCounts(splits, rawCorpus)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">    bestPair, maxFreq = counts.most_common(1)[0]</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">    mergePair(bestPair, splits)</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">    print(&quot;Merge&quot;, step, &quot;:&quot;, bestPair, &quot;-&gt;&quot;, bestPair[0] + bestPair[1], &quot;(freq:&quot;, maxFreq, &quot;)&quot;)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;Final Subword Splits:&quot;, splits)</span>
          </code>
        </pre>
      </div>

          Pro Tip (Production Libraries: tiktoken &amp; HuggingFace Tokenizers):

        <div>
