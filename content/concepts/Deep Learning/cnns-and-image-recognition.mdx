  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Beginner Foundations: Why Standard MLPs Fail on Images
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        To a computer, a color image is a 3D Tensor of pixel brightness values with shape <strong className="text-white">$(C, H, W) = (\text{Channels}, \text{Height}, \text{Width})$</strong>, where $C = 3$ for Red, Green, and Blue. Suppose we have a modest $224 \times 224$ RGB photo ($3 \times 224 \times 224 = 150{,}528$ numbers). What goes wrong if we flatten those pixels into a vector and pass them into a standard Dense layer (<code>nn.Linear</code>)?
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-rose-300">Problem 1: Parameter Explosion</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Connecting $150{,}528$ input pixels to just $1{,}000$ hidden neurons requires $150{,}528 \times 1{,}000 = 150.5\text{ Million}$ weights in a <strong className="text-white">single layer</strong>! The model immediately memorizes the training photos (overfits) and eats gigabytes of VRAM.
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-rose-300">
            1 Dense Layer = 150,528,000 Weights
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-amber-300">Problem 2: Zero Translation Invariance</div>
          <div className="mb-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            In a Dense layer, every pixel position has its own dedicated weights. If you train an MLP to recognize a cat in the <strong className="text-white">top-left corner</strong>, and then shift the exact same cat $10$ pixels to the <strong className="text-white">bottom-right</strong>, the MLP fails to recognize it!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg border border-white/10 bg-slate-950 p-3 text-center font-mono text-xs text-amber-300">
            Shifted Object = Completely Different Inputs
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">
          How CNNs Fix Both Problems at Once (Local Connectivity + Weight Sharing)
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          Instead of connecting every neuron to all $150{,}528$ pixels, a CNN uses a tiny <strong className="text-cyan-300">$3 \times 3$ Filter (Kernel)</strong> containing just $9$ weights per channel, and <strong className="text-emerald-300">slides that exact same filter across every $(x, y)$ position of the image</strong>! If a filter learns to detect a cat&apos;s ear in the top-left, it automatically detects that same ear anywhere in the image (<strong className="text-white">Translation Equivariance</strong>) using only a handful of shared weights!
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Beginner: How 2D Convolution Works (The Sliding Flashlight)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Imagine holding a $3 \times 3$ magnifying glass (the <strong className="text-violet-300">Kernel / Filter $\mathbf{K}$</strong>) over the top-left $3 \times 3$ patch of an image $\mathbf{I}$. At each stop, you multiply the $9$ filter weights by the $9$ underlying image pixels element-by-element and sum them up (a <strong className="text-white">Dot Product</strong>) to produce $1$ output pixel in the new <strong className="text-cyan-300">Feature Map (Activation Map)</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-violet-400/20 bg-violet-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$S(i, j) = (\mathbf{I} * \mathbf{K})(i, j) = \sum_{m=0}^{k_H - 1} \sum_{n=0}^{k_W - 1} \mathbf{I}(i + m, \; j + n) \cdot \mathbf{K}(m, n) + b$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-blue-400/15 bg-blue-400/[0.05] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-4 text-lg font-bold text-white">Intuitive Example: How a $3 \times 3$ Filter Detects Vertical Edges</h3>
        <div className="space-y-4 text-sm">
          <div>
            Suppose we use the classic vertical edge-detector filter $\mathbf{K} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 &amp; 0 &amp; -1 \\ 1 &amp; 0 &amp; -1 \\ 1 &amp; 0 &amp; -1 \end{bmatrix}$. Look at what happens when it slides over two different image patches:
          </div>
          <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4 text-center">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-slate-300">Patch A: Flat Uniform Sky (All 50s)</div>
              <div>$$(50 - 50) + (50 - 50) + (50 - 50) = 0$$</div>
              <div className="mt-2 text-xs text-slate-400">Left and right columns cancel out → Output is <strong>0 (No Edge)</strong>!</div>
            </div>
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/80 p-4 text-center">
              <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">Patch B: Bright Left (100), Dark Right (0)</div>
              <div>$$(100 - 0) + (100 - 0) + (100 - 0) = +300$$</div>
              <div className="mt-2 text-xs text-emerald-300">Strong mismatch across boundary → <strong>Fires +300 (Vertical Edge Found!)</strong></div>
            </div>
          </div>
          <div>
            In classical image processing, engineers designed filters like this by hand. In a <strong className="text-white">CNN</strong>, we initialize the filter weights randomly and let <strong className="text-cyan-300">Backpropagation learn the exact filter numbers automatically</strong> from data!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          If an input RGB color image has $3$ channels ($C_{\text{in}} = 3$) and we apply a single $3 \times 3$ convolutional filter to it, how many trainable weights (excluding bias) does that ONE filter have?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) 27 weights (3 channels × 3 height × 3 width)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>Every convolutional filter always spans the full depth $C_{\text{in}}$ of its input volume. So a $3 \times 3$ filter on an RGB image is actually a 3D block of shape $(3, 3, 3) = 27$ weights that sums across Red, Green, and Blue to output $1$ feature map channel!</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) 9 weights (3 height × 3 width)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>A filter only has $9$ weights if the input is a 1-channel grayscale image ($C_{\text{in}} = 1$). On a $C_{\text{in}}$-channel input, each filter has $C_{\text{in}} \times k_H \times k_W$ weights.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Intermediate: Padding, Stride, Pooling &amp; The Output Shape Formula
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Three hyperparameters control how a convolutional layer slides across an input of spatial size $W_{\text{in}}$ and what output spatial size $W_{\text{out}}$ it produces:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-cyan-300">1. Kernel Size ($K$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Usually $3 \times 3$ or $1 \times 1$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            The spatial window size of the filter. Without padding, a $3 \times 3$ kernel shrinks the image border by $2$ pixels on every layer!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-violet-300">2. Padding ($P$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">Usually $P = 1$ for $K = 3$</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Adds a border of $P$ zeros around the edges of the image (<strong className="text-white">&quot;Same&quot; Padding</strong>) so the output height and width stay the exact same size!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-emerald-300">3. Stride ($S$)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs text-slate-400">$S = 1$ (slide) or $S = 2$ (skip)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            How many pixels the filter jumps on each step. Setting <strong className="text-white">Stride $S = 2$</strong> skips every other pixel, cutting height and width in half!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center">
        <div className="mb-3 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-emerald-300">The Universal CNN Spatial Output Size Formula</div>
        <div className="text-lg">$$H_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \dfrac{H_{\text{in}} + 2P - K}{S} \right\rfloor + 1 \qquad \text{and} \qquad W_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \dfrac{W_{\text{in}} + 2P - K}{S} \right\rfloor + 1$$</div>
      </div>
      <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">Pooling Layers: Max Pooling vs. Global Average Pooling</h3>
      <div>
        To reduce memory and make the network invariant to small shifts, CNNs periodically downsample the spatial resolution $(H, W)$ using <strong className="text-white">Pooling Layers</strong> (which contain <strong className="text-cyan-300">$0$ learnable weights</strong>!):
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-500/25 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-blue-300">1. Max Pooling (nn.MaxPool2d(2, 2))</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Slides a $2 \times 2$ window with stride $2$ and keeps only the <strong className="text-white">single largest number</strong> in each $2 \times 2$ patch. Cuts $H$ and $W$ in half (reducing pixels by $75\%$) while preserving the strongest detected feature!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">2. Global Average Pooling (AdaptiveAvgPool2d(1))</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Used at the very end of modern CNNs (like ResNet): averages each $H \times W$ feature map down into a <strong className="text-white">single $1 \times 1$ number per channel</strong>, eliminating massive Dense layers and allowing the CNN to accept any input image resolution!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          You pass a batch of images with shape $(32, 3, 64, 64)$ into <code>nn.Conv2d(in_channels=3, out_channels=16, kernel_size=3, stride=2, padding=1)</code>. What is the exact output tensor shape?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) (32, 16, 32, 32)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>Batch size stays $32$, channels become <code>out_channels = 16</code>, and the spatial formula gives $\lfloor \frac{64 + 2(1) - 3}{2} \rfloor + 1 = \lfloor \frac{63}{2} \rfloor + 1 = 31 + 1 = 32$.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) (32, 3, 64, 64)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>With $K=3$ and $P=1$, the spatial size only stays $64 \times 64$ when <code>stride=1</code>. Because <code>stride=2</code>, the spatial dimensions are halved to $32 \times 32$, and channels change to $16$.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Intermediate to Advanced: Hierarchical Receptive Fields &amp; Classic Architectures
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        If a $3 \times 3$ filter only looks at a tiny $3 \times 3$ patch of pixels, how can a CNN see an entire car or human face? Through the <strong className="text-orange-300">Effective Receptive Field</strong>!
      </div>
      <div>
        A $3 \times 3$ filter in <strong className="text-white">Layer 1</strong> sees a $3 \times 3$ patch of raw pixels. A $3 \times 3$ filter in <strong className="text-white">Layer 2</strong> looks at a $3 \times 3$ patch of Layer 1 outputs—which covers a <strong className="text-cyan-300">$5 \times 5$ patch</strong> of the original image! Stacking three $3 \times 3$ layers covers a $7 \times 7$ receptive field using only $3 \times (3^2) = 27$ weights per channel instead of $7^2 = 49$ weights, with three non-linear ReLU folds instead of one!
      </div>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Advanced: $1 \times 1$ Convolutions, Depthwise Separable Convs &amp; Modern Vision Tasks
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        At the <strong className="text-white">Advanced Computer Vision</strong> level, three architectural techniques power everything from real-time mobile object detectors (YOLO) to medical image segmentation (U-Net):
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. What is a $1 \times 1$ Convolution? (Pointwise Bottleneck)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            At first glance, a $1 \times 1$ filter sounds useless because it only looks at $1$ pixel at a time! Remember, however, that a filter spans <strong className="text-white">all $C_{\text{in}}$ channels</strong>: a $1 \times 1$ convolution mixes information across channels and acts as a <strong className="text-emerald-300">channel-dimension bottleneck</strong> (e.g., compressing $256$ channels down to $64$ channels before an expensive $3 \times 3$ conv)!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">
            Used in: ResNet-50 Bottlenecks, Inception, and MobileNet
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-violet-300">2. Depthwise Separable Convolutions (9x Faster)</div>
          <div className="mb-3 text-sm text-slate-300">
            Factors a standard 3D convolution into two cheap steps: (a) a <strong className="text-white">Depthwise Conv</strong> (<code>groups=C_in</code>) that applies one spatial $3 \times 3$ filter per channel independently, followed by (b) a <strong className="text-white">$1 \times 1$ Pointwise Conv</strong> that mixes the channels together—slashing FLOPs by $\approx 8\text{--}9\times$!
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-lg bg-slate-950 p-3 font-mono text-xs text-violet-300">
            Powers: MobileNet, EfficientNet, Xception, and ConvNeXt
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">
          3. Beyond Classification: Object Detection (YOLO) &amp; Semantic Segmentation (U-Net)
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          • <strong className="text-cyan-300">Image Classification (ResNet / ViT)[cite: 10]:</strong> Outputs $1$ class label for the whole image $(B, K)$.<br />
          • <strong className="text-amber-300">Object Detection (YOLO / Faster R-CNN):</strong> Predicts bounding boxes $(x, y, w, h)$ + class labels for multiple objects in real time.<br />
          • <strong className="text-emerald-300">Semantic Segmentation (U-Net):</strong> Uses an Encoder-Decoder CNN with skip connections to classify <strong className="text-white">every individual pixel</strong> $(B, K, H, W)$!
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check </span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          In a Residual Block ($\mathbf{Y} = F(\mathbf{X}) + \mathbf{X}$), suppose the input tensor $\mathbf{X}$ has shape $(B, 64, 56, 56)$, and the main branch $F(\mathbf{X})$ doubles the channels and halves the spatial size to $(B, 128, 28, 28)$. How do we add $\mathbf{X}$ to $F(\mathbf{X})$ when their shapes do not match?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">

      </div>

        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">import torch</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">import torch.nn as nn</span>
            <span className="block">import torch.nn.functional as F</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 1. Define a ResNet-Style Residual Convolutional Block</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">class ResidualConvBlock(nn.Module):</span>
            <span className="block">    def __init__(self, channels: int):</span>
            <span className="block">        super().__init__()</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.conv1 = nn.Conv2d(channels, channels, kernel_size=3, padding=1, bias=False)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.bn1   = nn.BatchNorm2d(channels)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.conv2 = nn.Conv2d(channels, channels, kernel_size=3, padding=1, bias=False)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.bn2   = nn.BatchNorm2d(channels)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -&gt; torch.Tensor:</span>
            <span className="block">        identity = x                            # Save input for Skip Connection</span>
            <span className="block">        out = F.relu(self.bn1(self.conv1(x)))</span>
            <span className="block">        out = self.bn2(self.conv2(out))</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        return F.relu(out + identity)           # Residual Addition: F(x) + x!</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 2. Assemble the Full Image Recognition CNN</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">class MiniResNet(nn.Module):</span>
            <span className="block">    def __init__(self, numClasses: int = 10):</span>
            <span className="block">        super().__init__()</span>
            <span className="block">        self.stem = nn.Sequential(</span>
            <span className="block">            nn.Conv2d(3, 32, kernel_size=3, stride=1, padding=1, bias=False),</span>
            <span className="block">            nn.BatchNorm2d(32),</span>
            <span className="block">            nn.ReLU()</span>
            <span className="block">        )</span>
            <span className="block">        self.resBlock = ResidualConvBlock(32)</span>
            <span className="block">        self.downsample = nn.MaxPool2d(kernel_size=2, stride=2)</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">        self.globalPool = nn.AdaptiveAvgPool2d((1, 1))  # Pools any HxW down to 1x1!</span>
            <span className="block">        self.classifier = nn.Linear(32, numClasses)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -&gt; torch.Tensor:</span>
            <span className="block">        x = self.stem(x)                        # (B, 3, 32, 32)  -&gt; (B, 32, 32, 32)</span>
            <span className="block">        x = self.resBlock(x)                    # (B, 32, 32, 32) -&gt; (B, 32, 32, 32)</span>
            <span className="block">        x = self.downsample(x)                  # (B, 32, 32, 32) -&gt; (B, 32, 16, 16)</span>
            <span className="block">        x = self.globalPool(x)                  # (B, 32, 16, 16) -&gt; (B, 32, 1, 1)</span>
            <span className="block">        x = torch.flatten(x, start_dim=1)       # (B, 32, 1, 1)   -&gt; (B, 32)</span>
            <span className="block">        return self.classifier(x)               # (B, 32)         -&gt; (B, 10)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">model = MiniResNet(numClasses=10)</span>
            <span className="block">sampleImages = torch.randn(4, 3, 32, 32)        # Batch of 4 RGB images (32x32)</span>
            <span className="block">logits = model(sampleImages)</span>
            <span className="block">totalParams = sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters())</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;Output Logits Shape:&quot;, logits.shape, &quot;| Total Params:&quot;, totalParams)</span>
          </code>

      <div className="rounded-xl border border-teal-500/20 bg-teal-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-7 text-slate-300">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-teal-300">
          Pro Tip (Why We Set bias=False Inside Conv2d Before BatchNorm2d):
        </div>
        <div>
          Look at <code>nn.Conv2d(..., bias=False)</code> right before <code>nn.BatchNorm2d(32)</code> in the code above! Batch Normalization subtracts the channel mean $\mu$ from the convolution output, which <strong className="text-white">completely cancels out any constant bias $b$ added by the convolution</strong>—and BatchNorm already has its own learnable shift parameter $\beta$. Setting <code>bias=False</code> saves memory and eliminates redundant parameters!

    </h2>
    <div className="rounded-2xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-950/[0.15] p-6 sm:p-8">
      <div className="space-y-6">
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Passing Channels-Last (B, H, W, C) images into PyTorch&apos;s nn.Conv2d.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            OpenCV, PIL, and Matplotlib store images as $(H, W, C)$, whereas PyTorch strictly requires <strong className="text-white">Channels-First $(B, C, H, W)$</strong>! Always reorder axes with <code>x.permute(0, 3, 1, 2)</code> or <code>torchvision.transforms.ToTensor()</code>.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Forgetting to normalize pixel values from [0, 255] uint8 into float32 before training.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Raw integer pixels in $[0, 255]$ cause massive initial activations and exploding gradients. Always cast images to <code>float32</code> scaled to $[0, 1]$ and normalize by channel mean and standard deviation.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Flattening a large spatial feature map (e.g., 512 x 28 x 28) directly into a huge Linear layer.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Flattening $512 \times 28 \times 28 = 401{,}408$ activations into a dense layer createstens of millions of parameters and locks your model to a single input resolution. Use <code>nn.AdaptiveAvgPool2d((1, 1))</code> first to pool down to $512$ numbers!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Leaving bias=True on Conv2d layers that are immediately followed by BatchNorm2d.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Because BatchNorm subtracts the mean across the batch, any bias added by the preceding convolution is immediately subtracted out and receives zero effective gradient.
          </div>
        </div>
