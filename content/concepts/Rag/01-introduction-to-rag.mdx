<section>
  <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">1. Beginner: The Open-Book Exam</h2>
  <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
    <div>Picture two students taking the same exam about your company. The first student studied months ago and must answer purely from memory. The second student is allowed to bring the company handbook and look things up before writing each answer. Both are equally smart, but the second one will be more accurate, especially about details that are new, rare or private.</div>
    <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-rose-300">Closed-book student = a plain LLM</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">A <strong className="text-white">large language model (LLM)</strong> is an AI system trained on huge amounts of text to predict and generate language. Everything it knows is frozen inside its parameters at training time.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-emerald-300">Open-book student = a RAG system</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">The same model, plus a search step that fetches relevant pages from a document collection and places them in front of the model before it answers.</div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-500/20 bg-blue-500/[0.05] p-5">
      <strong className="text-blue-300">Key point:</strong> RAG does not make the model smarter and does not change its parameters. It changes what the model gets to read at the moment it answers.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">2. Beginner: What RAG Is in Three Steps</h2>
  <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
    <div>The name RAG describes the three things that happen every time a user asks a question:</div>
    <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-3">
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="text-sm font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-400">R · Retrieve</div>
        <div className="mt-2 text-sm leading-7">Search a collection of documents and pick the few passages most related to the question.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="text-sm font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-cyan-400">A · Augment</div>
        <div className="mt-2 text-sm leading-7">Build a new prompt that contains the question and the retrieved passages as context.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="text-sm font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-400">G · Generate</div>
        <div className="mt-2 text-sm leading-7">The LLM reads that prompt and writes an answer grounded in the context.</div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div>Here is a tiny example. A customer asks: <em>What is your refund policy?</em> A plain LLM has never seen your policy and might guess a common one. A RAG system first retrieves the passage <em>Returns are accepted within 30 days of purchase</em>, adds it to the prompt, and the model answers with the real policy.</div>
    <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/20 bg-violet-500/[0.05] p-5">
      <strong className="text-violet-300">Analogy:</strong> a librarian (the retriever) fetches the right books from the shelves, and a writer (the LLM) reads those books and composes the answer. Neither could do the whole job as well alone.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">4. Intermediate: RAG Terminology</h2>
  <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
    <div>RAG has its own vocabulary. Learn these words now, because every later lesson builds on them.</div>
    <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-xl border border-white/10">
      <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
        <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-slate-200">
          <tr>
            <th className="px-4 py-3">Term</th>
            <th className="px-4 py-3">Plain meaning</th>
          </tr>
        </thead>
        <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/10">
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Knowledge base</td><td className="px-4 py-3">The collection of documents the system can search.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Chunk</td><td className="px-4 py-3">A small piece of a document, such as a few paragraphs, stored and searched as one unit.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Embedding</td><td className="px-4 py-3">A list of numbers that represents the meaning of a text, so similar meanings get similar numbers.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Vector store</td><td className="px-4 py-3">A database that keeps embeddings and can quickly find the closest ones to a query.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Query</td><td className="px-4 py-3">The user question that the system must answer.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Retriever</td><td className="px-4 py-3">The component that finds the most relevant chunks for a query.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Top-k</td><td className="px-4 py-3">The number k of best-matching chunks the retriever returns.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Context</td><td className="px-4 py-3">The retrieved text placed inside the prompt for the model to read.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Generator</td><td className="px-4 py-3">The LLM that writes the final answer from the prompt.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Grounding</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Making sure the answer is supported by the retrieved context.</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Hallucination</td><td className="px-4 py-3">A confident-sounding answer that is false or unsupported.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <div>Two phases tie these terms together. In the <strong className="text-white">indexing phase</strong> (done ahead of time) documents are split into chunks, converted to embeddings and stored. In the <strong className="text-white">query phase</strong> (done for every question) the retriever searches that store, the prompt is built, and the generator answers. Lesson 3 covers this pipeline in detail.</div>
  </div>
</section>

    <div>RAG fits any situation where answers must come from a specific, changing or private body of text.</div>

      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-cyan-300">Customer support</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">A chatbot answers from help-center articles, return policies and product manuals.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-cyan-300">Document question answering</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Ask questions about contracts, reports or research papers instead of reading every page.</div>

        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Employees ask natural-language questions over wikis, tickets and meeting notes.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-cyan-300">Developer assistants</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Answers grounded in a project documentation or codebase rather than general memory.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-cyan-300">Research helpers</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Summaries and answers that point back to the papers they came from.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-cyan-300">Regulated domains</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Legal, medical or financial answers where citing the exact source matters.</div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5">
      <strong className="text-amber-300">Advanced note: RAG is only as good as its retrieval.</strong> There are two main ways it fails. If the retriever misses the right passage, the model has no evidence and may guess. If the retriever finds the right passage but the model ignores or misreads it, the answer is still wrong. Most of the later lessons in this course are about preventing these two failures.
    </div>
    <div>RAG is also not always needed. For general knowledge that the model already handles well, or tiny knowledge sets that fit directly in the prompt, a plain LLM call can be simpler and cheaper.</div>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 data-type="code" className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">7. Python Implementation: A Tiny RAG Pipeline from Scratch</h2>
  <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
    <div>This program uses only the Python standard library. It stores four short documents, turns each text into word counts, and measures similarity with <strong className="text-white">cosine similarity</strong>, which compares two count vectors by the angle between them:</div>
    <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5 text-center">$$\cos(\theta) = \dfrac{A \cdot B}{\lVert A \rVert \, \lVert B \rVert}$$</div>
    <div>A score of 1 means the texts use the same words in the same proportions, and 0 means they share no words. The program retrieves the top 2 documents for a question and builds the augmented prompt. It stops there, because the final step would be sending that prompt to an LLM. Real systems use learned embeddings instead of word counts, which you will meet in the embeddings lessons.</div>
    <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
      <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
        <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">tiny_rag.py</span>
        <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3.10+ · Standard library only</span>
      </div>
      <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
        <code>
          <span className="block text-cyan-300">import math</span>
          <span className="block text-cyan-300">from collections import Counter</span>
          <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
          <span className="block text-slate-500"># 1. The knowledge base (four tiny documents)</span>
          <span className="block">documents = [</span>
          <span className="block">    &quot;The company refund policy allows returns within 30 days of purchase.&quot;,</span>
          <span className="block">    &quot;Standard shipping takes 5 to 7 business days inside the country.&quot;,</span>
          <span className="block">    &quot;Premium members get free shipping on every order.&quot;,</span>
          <span className="block">    &quot;Our support team is available Monday to Friday from 9 am to 5 pm.&quot;,</span>
          <span className="block">]</span>
          <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
          <span className="block text-cyan-300">def tokenize(text):</span>
          <span className="block">    cleaned = &quot;&quot;.join(ch for ch in text.lower() if ch.isalnum() or ch == &quot; &quot;)</span>
          <span className="block">    return cleaned.split()</span>
          <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
          <span className="block text-cyan-300">def vectorize(text):</span>
          <span className="block">    return Counter(tokenize(text))</span>
          <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
          <span className="block text-cyan-300">def cosine(a, b):</span>
          <span className="block">    common = set(a) &amp; set(b)</span>
          <span className="block">    dot = sum(a[w] * b[w] for w in common)</span>
          <span className="block">    norm_a = math.sqrt(sum(v * v for v in a.values()))</span>
          <span className="block">    norm_b = math.sqrt(sum(v * v for v in b.values()))</span>
          <span className="block">    if norm_a == 0 or norm_b == 0:</span>
          <span className="block">        return 0.0</span>
          <span className="block">    return dot / (norm_a * norm_b)</span>
          <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
          <span className="block text-slate-500"># 2. RETRIEVE: score every document and keep the top k</span>
          <span className="block text-violet-300">def retrieve(question, docs, k=2):</span>
          <span className="block">    q = vectorize(question)</span>
          <span className="block">    scored = [(cosine(q, vectorize(d)), d) for d in docs]</span>
          <span className="block">    scored.sort(reverse=True)</span>
          <span className="block">    return scored[:k]</span>
          <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
          <span className="block text-slate-500"># 3. AUGMENT: put the context and the question into one prompt</span>
          <span className="block text-emerald-300">def build_prompt(question, retrieved):</span>
          <span className="block">    lines = [&quot;Answer using ONLY the context below.&quot;, &quot;&quot;, &quot;Context:&quot;]</span>
          <span className="block">    for score, text in retrieved:</span>
          <span className="block">        lines.append(&quot;- &quot; + text)</span>
          <span className="block">    lines.append(&quot;&quot;)</span>
          <span className="block">    lines.append(&quot;Question: &quot; + question)</span>
          <span className="block">    return lines</span>
          <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
          <span className="block">question = &quot;What is the refund policy for returns?&quot;</span>
          <span className="block">retrieved = retrieve(question, documents, k=2)</span>
          <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
          <span className="block">print(&quot;Retrieved passages:&quot;)</span>
          <span className="block">for score, text in retrieved:</span>
          <span className="block">    print(round(score, 3), &quot;|&quot;, text)</span>
          <span className="block">print(&quot;&quot;)</span>
          <span className="block">print(&quot;Prompt sent to the LLM:&quot;)</span>
          <span className="block">for line in build_prompt(question, retrieved):</span>
          <span className="block">    print(line)</span>
        </code>
      </pre>
    </div>
