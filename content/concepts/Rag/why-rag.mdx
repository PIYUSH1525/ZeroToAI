    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>

      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">

          </div>
          <div className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            <div><strong className="text-white">What it is:</strong> Knowledge stored implicitly inside the LLM&apos;s billions of neural network parameters ($\theta$) during pre-training.</div>
            <div><strong className="text-white">Human Analogy:</strong> Trying to recite a textbook chapter from memory 6 months after reading it. You remember the big ideas, but <strong className="text-rose-300">blur exact numbers, dates, and clauses</strong>!</div>
            <div><strong className="text-white">How to update it:</strong> Requires expensive GPU retraining or fine-tuning.</div>

            <div><strong className="text-white">How to update it:</strong> Edit or upload a file—updates in milliseconds!</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">

Common facts that appear 10,000 times on the web (like <em>&quot;Paris is the capital of France&quot;</em>) survive cleanly. Rare or specific facts (like <em>&quot;Part #AX-409 costs USD 42.50&quot;</em>) get blurred together with similar part numbers!

      3. Medium: Why Do LLMs Hallucinate? (The Mathematical Reason)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Beginners often wonder: <em>&quot;If an LLM doesn&apos;t know an answer, why doesn&apos;t it just stay quiet? Why does it make up a fake legal case or a fake API function with 100% confidence?&quot;</em>
      </div>
      <div>
        The answer lies in how an LLM is trained and how Softmax works:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.04] px-6 py-6 text-center">
        <div className="mb-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-emerald-300">The Softmax Probability Constraint (Something Must Sum to 100%!)</div>
        <div className="text-lg">$$P(w_t \mid w_1, \dots, w_{t-1}) = \text{softmax}(z_t)$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. No Built-In &quot;Truth Sensor&quot;</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            During pre-training, the model&apos;s Cross-Entropy Loss only rewards predicting <strong className="text-white">what word is statistically likely to follow the previous words</strong>. If you ask <em>&quot;What did the Supreme Court rule in Smith v. Acme (2024)?&quot;</em> (a fake case), the tokens that statistically follow legal case names are legal-sounding rulings—so the model generates a fluent, completely imaginary court ruling!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-violet-300">2. Snowballing (Exposure Bias)</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Because generation is <strong className="text-white">autoregressive</strong>, once an LLM samples one slightly wrong number or name at Token #5, that wrong token becomes part of the prompt for Token #6! The model then invents an entire fictional explanation to stay consistent with its own earlier mistake!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">
          Two Types of Hallucinations Engineers Track in Production
        </div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          <div>• <strong className="text-rose-300">Extrinsic Hallucination:</strong> The model invents facts, numbers, or citations that do not exist in the source material at all (common in standalone LLMs).</div>
          <div>• <strong className="text-amber-300">Intrinsic Hallucination:</strong> The model is given a source paragraph, but misreads or contradicts a detail inside that paragraph (solved in RAG by clean chunking and strict grounding prompts, which we cover in Module 9!).</div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
