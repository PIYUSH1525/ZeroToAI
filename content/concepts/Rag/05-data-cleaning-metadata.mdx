<section>
  <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">1. Beginner: Why Clean Data Matters</h2>
  <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
    <div>Imagine a library where the same book appears five times, some pages have the page number printed in the middle of sentences, and old editions sit next to new ones with no labels. A librarian searching it would hand you three copies of the same outdated page. A RAG retriever behaves exactly like that librarian: it returns whatever the index contains, in whatever condition it is in.</div>
    <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-3">
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-rose-300">Noise</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Page numbers, headers and odd symbols take up space in chunks and dilute their meaning.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-rose-300">Repetition</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Copies of the same text fill the top results, pushing out other useful passages.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-rose-300">Contradiction</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">An old and a new version of a policy both get retrieved, and the model may quote the wrong one.</div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-500/20 bg-blue-500/[0.05] p-5">
      <strong className="text-blue-300">Key point:</strong> retrieval cannot tell which passage is current, clean or authoritative unless you tell it. Cleaning and metadata are how you tell it.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">2. Beginner: Cleaning and Normalization</h2>
  <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
    <div><strong className="text-white">Cleaning</strong> removes what should not be there. <strong className="text-white">Normalization</strong> makes equivalent things look identical. Both aim at one goal: the same idea should always be written the same way.</div>
    <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-xl border border-white/10">
      <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
        <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-slate-200">
          <tr>
            <th className="px-4 py-3">Operation</th>
            <th className="px-4 py-3">Example</th>
            <th className="px-4 py-3">Be careful</th>
          </tr>
        </thead>
        <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/10">
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-violet-300">Collapse whitespace</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Many spaces and line breaks become single spaces</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Keep paragraph breaks if chunking will use them</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-violet-300">Normalize characters</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Non-breaking spaces and fancy variants become plain characters</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Keep accents that change meaning</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-violet-300">Remove boilerplate</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Lines such as Page 3 of 10, repeated headers, cookie notices</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Do not delete real content that looks similar</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-violet-300">Fix encoding errors</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Garbled symbols replaced by the intended characters</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Fix at the source if you can</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-violet-300">Standardize formats</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Dates and units written one consistent way</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Never change the meaning of a number</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/20 bg-violet-500/[0.05] p-5">
      <strong className="text-violet-300">Less is more:</strong> modern embedding models understand normal text well. You usually do <em>not</em> need to lowercase everything, remove punctuation or strip common words. Clean away real noise, but keep the natural wording, because aggressive cleaning can destroy meaning.

<section>
  <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">3. Intermediate: Duplicate Removal</h2>
  <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
    <div>Duplicates enter a knowledge base in many ways: the same file saved in two folders, an email thread quoted in every reply, a web page reachable through several URLs. They hurt in three ways: they fill the top-k results with identical text, they waste embedding and storage cost, and they inflate how important a passage looks.</div>
    <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-emerald-300">Exact duplicates</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Compute a <strong className="text-white">hash</strong>, a short fingerprint of the cleaned text. Two texts with the same hash are identical, and the second can be skipped. This is fast and cheap, and it works best after cleaning, because two copies that differ only in spacing become identical.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-semibold text-emerald-300">Near duplicates</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-7">Texts that are almost the same, such as a document with one changed sentence. Compare the overlap of their word sets, or use similarity search, and treat pairs above a chosen threshold as duplicates for review.</div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div>A simple overlap measure is the Jaccard similarity of two word sets $A$ and $B$:</div>
    <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5 text-center">$$J(A,B) = \dfrac{\lvert A \cap B \rvert}{\lvert A \cup B \rvert}$$</div>
    <div>If two passages each use 8 distinct words and share 7 of them, the union has $8 + 8 - 7 = 9$ words, so $J = 7/9 \approx 0.78$. A value of 1 means identical word sets and 0 means nothing in common.</div>
    <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5">
      <strong className="text-amber-300">Not every repeat is a mistake.</strong> Legal boilerplate or a standard disclaimer may legitimately appear in many documents. Decide per source type whether repeated text should be removed, kept once, or kept everywhere with its metadata.
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Timestamp</td><td className="px-4 py-3">2025-06-15</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Freshness filters and ordering</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Version and status</td><td className="px-4 py-3">v2, active</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Hide outdated versions</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Fingerprint</td><td className="px-4 py-3">a73e2f47</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Detecting duplicates and changes</td></tr>
          <tr><td className="px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-orange-300">Category or owner</td><td className="px-4 py-3">legal, HR, support</td><td className="px-4 py-3">Filtering and access rules</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <div>Notice that the document ID and the fingerprint are different things. The <strong className="text-white">document ID</strong> is a stable name for the document, derived for example from its source path, so it stays the same across versions. The <strong className="text-white">fingerprint</strong> is a hash of the content, so it changes whenever the text changes. Using both lets you say <em>this is the same document, but its content is new</em>.</div>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">5. Advanced: Document Versioning and Keeping the Index Fresh</h2>
  <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
    <div>Documents change. A good pipeline treats every incoming file as one of four cases, decided by comparing its document ID and fingerprint with what is already stored:</div>
    <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-4"><strong className="text-cyan-300">New ID.</strong> A brand-new document. Process it fully and mark it active as version 1.</div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-4"><strong className="text-cyan-300">Same ID, same fingerprint.</strong> Nothing changed. Skip it and save the embedding cost.</div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-4"><strong className="text-cyan-300">Same ID, new fingerprint.</strong> The content was updated. Create the next version, mark the old one superseded, and re-embed the new text.</div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-4"><strong className="text-cyan-300">Same fingerprint, different ID.</strong> A copy of an existing document. Skip it as a duplicate.</div>
    </div>
    <div>Marking the old version as superseded matters as much as adding the new one. If the old chunks stay searchable, retrieval can return both the 30-day and the 60-day return policy, and the model has no way to know which is current. Most vector stores let you filter on a status field, or delete the old chunks outright.</div>
    <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/20 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5">
      <strong className="text-cyan-300">Deletion and audit:</strong> when a document is withdrawn, its chunks must disappear from the index too, otherwise answers keep quoting content that no longer exists. Keeping a history of versions with timestamps also gives you an audit trail: you can explain what the system knew at any past date.
    </div>
    <div>Processing only what changed is called <strong className="text-white">incremental ingestion</strong>. It keeps the cost of freshness low, and later production lessons build on it.</div>
  </div>
</section>
