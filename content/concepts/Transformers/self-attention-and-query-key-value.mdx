      2. Beginner: What Are Query, Key, and Value (Q, K, V)? (The YouTube Search Analogy)

    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Every textbook says: <em>&quot;Self-Attention projects each token into a Query, a Key, and a Value.&quot;</em> Why those three weird names?
      </div>
      <div>

      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase text-cyan-400">1. Query (Q)</div>
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-white">&quot;What I Am Looking For&quot;</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Like typing <em>&quot;fluffy pet that meows&quot;</em> into the YouTube search bar. The word <strong className="text-cyan-300">&quot;it&quot;</strong> sends out a Query asking: <em>&quot;Hey, is there a tired noun earlier in this sentence?&quot;</em>

          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-white">&quot;My Name Tag / Title&quot;</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Like the title tags on every YouTube video. Every word in the sentence holds up a Key badge announcing what it is: <strong className="text-violet-300">&quot;animal&quot;</strong> holds up a tag saying <em>&quot;I am a living noun!&quot;</em>

The <strong className="text-emerald-300">Value</strong>! It is the actual rich meaning payload that gets passed over to update the word.

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Medium: The 4-Step Recipe of Scaled Dot-Product Attention
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Now that you know what $\mathbf{Q}$, $\mathbf{K}$, and $\mathbf{V}$ are, the famous equation from the 2017 <em>&quot;Attention Is All You Need&quot;</em> paper is super easy to read. It is just a <strong className="text-white">4-step kitchen recipe</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-lg">
        <div>$$\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{softmax}\left(\dfrac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 sm:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-cyan-300">Step 1: Score Every Pair ($\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T$)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Multiply every word&apos;s Query by every word&apos;s Key. If a sentence has $5$ words, this creates a $5 \times 5$ grid of raw match scores.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-violet-300">Step 2: Divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$ (Cool Down!)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            If vectors have $d_k = 64$ numbers, their dot products can get huge, which pushes Softmax into flat regions where gradients vanish! Dividing by $\sqrt{64} = 8$ keeps scores calm and balanced.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-amber-300">Step 3: Softmax (Turn into 100% Pie)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Applies Softmax across each row so all attention weights are positive percentages that add up to <strong className="text-white">$1.0$ ($100\%$)</strong>!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-emerald-300">Step 4: Blend the Values ($\times \mathbf{V}$)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Multiply each word&apos;s Value vector by its percentage weight (e.g., $0.80 \times \mathbf{v}_{\text{animal}} + 0.20 \times \mathbf{v}_{\text{tired}}$) and sum them up!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Medium: Causal Masking (How GPT Stops Cheating From the Future!)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        When we train a model like <strong className="text-white">GPT-4 or Llama-3</strong> to predict the next word in <em>&quot;The cat sat on the mat&quot;</em>, we feed all $6$ words into the GPU at the same time for speed.
      </div>
      <div>
        Wait a second! If all $6$ words are in the matrix at the same time, when Word #2 (<em>&quot;cat&quot;</em>) tries to guess Word #3 (<em>&quot;sat&quot;</em>), <strong className="text-rose-300">what stops &quot;cat&quot; from just looking ahead at Seat #3 and copying the answer?</strong>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-orange-500/25 bg-orange-500/[0.04] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-orange-300">The Fix: The Upper-Triangular Causal Mask ($-\infty$)</h3>
        <div className="space-y-3 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          <div>
            Right before Step 3 (Softmax), we apply a <strong className="text-white">Causal Mask (Look-Ahead Blinder)</strong>: for every word at seat $i$, we overwrite the attention scores of all future words ($j \gt i$) with <strong className="text-rose-300">$-\infty$ (negative infinity)</strong>!
          </div>
          <div>
            Why $-\infty$? Because in Softmax, <strong className="text-emerald-300">$e^{-\infty} = 0.0$</strong>! Every future word gets <strong className="text-white">exact $0\%$ attention</strong>, so Word #2 can only see Word #1 and Word #2—never the future!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check</span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          Why do we fill masked future positions with $-\infty$ (negative infinity) BEFORE running Softmax, instead of filling them with $0$?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) Because in Softmax, exp(-infinity) = 0.0 (zero attention), whereas exp(0) = 1.0 (which would give positive attention to future words!)</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>Softmax exponentiates every score ($e^x$). Setting a score to $0$ before Softmax makes $e^0 = 1$, leaking information from the future! Setting it to $-\infty$ guarantees $e^{-\infty} = 0.0$ after Softmax.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) Because PyTorch tensors cannot store the number 0</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Tensors store zeros easily; the reason is purely mathematical because $e^0 = 1$ inside the Softmax numerator.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

      5. Advanced: The $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ Memory Bottleneck &amp; FlashAttention
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        If Self-Attention is so great, what is its biggest weakness in modern Large Language Models?
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-rose-300">1. The $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ Quadratic Explosion</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Because every word compares itself with every other word, a sequence of length $T$ creates a <strong className="text-white">$T \times T$ Attention Matrix</strong>:
            <br />• $1{,}000$ tokens = $1\text{ Million}$ pairs
            <br />• $100{,}000$ tokens = <strong className="text-rose-300">$10\text{ Billion}$ pairs per head!</strong>
            <br />Writing that massive $T \times T$ grid out to slow GPU RAM (HBM) is what makes long-context LLMs run out of memory!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">2. FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022)</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            How do modern models read $128\text{k}+$ tokens without crashing? <strong className="text-white">FlashAttention</strong> splits $\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}$ into tiny blocks inside ultra-fast GPU chip cache (SRAM) and computes the exact same Softmax result <strong className="text-emerald-300">without ever saving the giant $T \times T$ matrix to GPU memory</strong>—giving a $3\text{--}5\times$ speedup!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      7. Python Implementation: Scaled Dot-Product Self-Attention in PyTorch
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Here is a clean, runnable PyTorch script that implements the 4-step <strong className="text-cyan-300">Scaled Dot-Product Self-Attention</strong> formula (with an optional <strong className="text-violet-300">Causal Mask</strong>) from scratch in 25 lines of code:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
        <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
          <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">self_attention_qkv.py</span>
          <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3.11+ · PyTorch 2.x</span>
        </div>
        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">import math</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">import torch</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">import torch.nn as nn</span>
            <span className="block">import torch.nn.functional as F</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">class SingleHeadSelfAttention(nn.Module):</span>
            <span className="block">    def __init__(self, dModel: int):</span>
            <span className="block">        super().__init__()</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.wQ = nn.Linear(dModel, dModel, bias=False)  # Creates Query (What I look for)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.wK = nn.Linear(dModel, dModel, bias=False)  # Creates Key   (My label tag)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        self.wV = nn.Linear(dModel, dModel, bias=False)  # Creates Value (My content)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor, causalMask: bool = True):</span>
            <span className="block">        B, T, dK = x.shape</span>
            <span className="block">        Q, K, V = self.wQ(x), self.wK(x), self.wV(x)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">        # Steps 1 &amp; 2: Compute Match Scores (Q @ K^T) and Scale by sqrt(d_k)</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">        scores = (Q @ K.transpose(-2, -1)) / math.sqrt(dK)   # Shape: (B, T, T)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">        # Optional Causal Mask: Block future words with -infinity!</span>
            <span className="block">        if causalMask:</span>
            <span className="block">            upperTri = torch.triu(torch.ones(T, T, device=x.device), diagonal=1).bool()</span>
            <span className="block text-violet-300">            scores = scores.masked_fill(upperTri, float(&quot;-inf&quot;))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">        # Step 3: Turn scores into 100% row probabilities via Softmax</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">        attnWeights = F.softmax(scores, dim=-1)              # Shape: (B, T, T)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">        # Step 4: Blend the Value (V) vectors using the attention weights!</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">        output = attnWeights @ V                             # Shape: (B, T, dModel)</span>
            <span className="block">        return output, attnWeights</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">torch.manual_seed(42)</span>
            <span className="block">attn = SingleHeadSelfAttention(dModel=16)</span>
            <span className="block">sampleSentence = torch.randn(1, 4, 16)                       # 1 sentence, 4 words, 16 sliders</span>
            <span className="block">outVectors, weights = attn(sampleSentence, causalMask=True)</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;Output Shape:&quot;, outVectors.shape)</span>
            <span className="block text-emerald-300">print(&quot;4x4 Causal Attention Matrix:\n&quot;, weights[0].detach().numpy().round(2))</span>
          </code>

          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>Self-Attention allows every token in a sequence to look directly at every other token in a single step ($1$-hop connection), eliminating the sequential bottleneck of RNNs.</span>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>Every token vector $\mathbf{x}$ is projected into three roles: <strong>Query ($\mathbf{Q}$)</strong> (what it is looking for), <strong>Key ($\mathbf{K}$)</strong> (its label tag for others to match against), and <strong>Value ($\mathbf{V}$)</strong> (the semantic content it passes along).</span>

        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>Scaled Dot-Product Attention computes $\text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}$, where dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ prevents large dot products from pushing Softmax into zero-gradient saturation.</span>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>Autoregressive Decoder models (like GPT and Llama) apply an upper-triangular <strong>Causal Mask of $-\infty$</strong> before Softmax so tokens cannot peek at future tokens during parallel training.</span>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-3">
          <span className="mt-0.5 font-bold text-emerald-400">✓</span>
          <span>Because the $T \times T$ attention matrix grows quadratically ($\mathcal{O}(T^2)$), modern LLMs use <strong>FlashAttention</strong> to compute exact attention in fast GPU SRAM tiles without materializing the full $T \times T$ matrix in memory.</span>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-rose-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      Common Mistakes
    </h2>
    <div className="rounded-2xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-950/[0.15] p-6 sm:p-8">
      <div className="space-y-6">
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Applying the Causal Mask AFTER Softmax instead of BEFORE Softmax.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            If you zero out future tokens <em>after</em> Softmax, the remaining probabilities in each row no longer sum to $1.0$ ($100\%$)! Always mask future positions with <code>-inf</code> <em>before</em> calling <code>F.softmax(scores, dim=-1)</code>.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Forgetting to divide by sqrt(d_k) before Softmax.
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Without the $\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}$ scaling factor, large embedding dimensions produce extreme dot products (like $+80$ and $-80$), turning Softmax into a hard one-hot step function whose backward gradients vanish to zero.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div>
          <div className="mb-1.5 font-bold text-rose-400">
            ✕ Transposing the batch dimension instead of the last two dimensions in K.transpose(-2, -1).
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            In batched PyTorch tensors of shape <code>(Batch, Seq, Dim)</code>, never write <code>K.T</code>! Always use <code>K.transpose(-2, -1)</code> so only the sequence and feature dimensions swap: <code>(B, T, d) @ (B, d, T) -&gt; (B, T, T)</code>.
          </div>
        </div>
