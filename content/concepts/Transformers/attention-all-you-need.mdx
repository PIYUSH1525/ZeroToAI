        <strong className="text-cyan-400">The Core Idea (NeurIPS 2017 · Vaswani et al.):</strong> Before June 2017, every top AI system for language translation and sequence modeling was built on <strong>Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs, LSTMs, and GRUs)</strong>. Because recurrent models process text word-by-word from left to right ($h_t$ must wait for $h_{t-1}$), they could not be parallelized across GPUs during training, and their memory of early words faded over long paragraphs. The <em>&quot;Attention Is All You Need&quot;</em> paper introduced the <strong>Transformer</strong>—the first sequence transduction architecture to eliminate recurrence and convolutions completely, relying entirely on Self-Attention and Position-wise Feed-Forward Networks to connect any two words in a sentence in a single $\mathcal{O}(1)$ direct step!
      </div>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. High-Level Architecture: How the Encoder and Decoder Work Together
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        The original 2017 Transformer was designed for <strong className="text-white">Sequence-to-Sequence (Seq2Seq) tasks</strong> like translating an English sentence into French. To do this, the architecture is divided into two main macro-components that work as a team:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="text-lg font-bold text-cyan-300">1. The Encoder Stack (Left Tower)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-cyan-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-cyan-300">The Reader</span>
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Takes the entire input sequence of token symbols $(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)$ all at once and transforms them through $N = 6$ stacked layers into a sequence of rich, context-aware continuous representations $\mathbf{Z} = (\mathbf{z}_1, \mathbf{z}_2, \dots, \mathbf{z}_n)$. Because the entire input sentence is already known, the Encoder processes all words <strong className="text-white">simultaneously in parallel</strong>.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="text-lg font-bold text-violet-300">2. The Decoder Stack (Right Tower)</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-violet-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-violet-300">The Writer</span>
          </div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Receives the Encoder&apos;s contextual memory matrix $\mathbf{Z}$ and generates the output sequence $(y_1, y_2, \dots, y_m)$ <strong className="text-white">one token at a time</strong>. The model is <strong className="text-violet-300">auto-regressive</strong>: at each step, it consumes the tokens it has already generated so far as additional input to predict the very next token.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-3 text-lg font-bold text-white">The Input Pipeline Before Layer 1 (Embeddings + $\sqrt{d_{\text{model}}}$ Scaling + Positional Waves)</h3>
        <div className="space-y-3 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          <div>
            1. <strong className="text-cyan-300">Scaled Token Embeddings:</strong> Input token IDs are converted into vectors of dimension $d_{\text{model}} = 512$ using a learned embedding table. Notably, the paper <strong className="text-white">multiplies the embedding weights by $\sqrt{d_{\text{model}}}$ ($\sqrt{512} \approx 22.6$)</strong> before adding positional encodings so that the word&apos;s semantic meaning is not drowned out by the $[-1, +1]$ positional wave values!
          </div>
          <div>
            2. <strong className="text-emerald-300">Sinusoidal Positional Encoding Addition:</strong> Because the Transformer contains zero recurrence and zero convolution, a $512$-dimensional vector of multi-frequency $\sin$ and $\cos$ waves is added element-wise to inject exact token order.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Inside the Encoder: The 2-Sublayer Architecture
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        The Encoder is composed of a stack of <strong className="text-white">$N = 6$ identical layers</strong> piled on top of one another. Every single Encoder layer maintains the exact same vector width ($d_{\text{model}} = 512$) from bottom to top, and contains <strong className="text-cyan-300">two distinct sub-layers</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase text-cyan-400">Encoder Sub-Layer 1</div>
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-white">Bidirectional Multi-Head Self-Attention</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            In this sub-layer, all three inputs—Queries ($\mathbf{Q}$), Keys ($\mathbf{K}$), and Values ($\mathbf{V}$)—come from the <strong className="text-white">output of the previous Encoder layer</strong>. Every position in the Encoder can attend to <strong className="text-cyan-300">every position in the entire input sentence</strong> (both left and right, with no causal mask). Across the $8$ parallel attention heads, each word gathers grammatical, coreference, and contextual clues from the whole sentence at once.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase text-violet-400">Encoder Sub-Layer 2</div>
          <div className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-white">Position-Wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN)</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Once Self-Attention has mixed context across words, each word vector passes <strong className="text-white">individually and identically</strong> through a 2-layer fully connected neural network. It expands the $512$-dimensional vector to an inner dimension of <strong className="text-violet-300">$d_{\text{ff}} = 2{,}048$</strong> ($4\times$ wider), applies a non-linear $\text{ReLU}$ activation, and projects it back down to $512$.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="my-6 rounded-2xl border border-cyan-400/20 bg-cyan-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center space-y-4">
        <div className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-cyan-300">Mathematical Flow Through One Encoder Block</div>
        <div className="text-base sm:text-lg">$$\mathbf{H} = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{X} + \text{MultiHeadAttention}(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{X})\right)$$</div>
        <div className="text-base sm:text-lg">$$\mathbf{Z} = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{H} + \text{FFN}(\mathbf{H})\right) \quad \text{where} \quad \text{FFN}(\mathbf{h}) = \max(0, \; \mathbf{h}\mathbf{W}_1 + \mathbf{b}_1)\mathbf{W}_2 + \mathbf{b}_2$$</div>
      </div>
      <div className="mt-8 rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-[#0A0D16] p-6 shadow-[0_0_25px_rgba(245,158,11,0.05)]">
        <div className="mb-2 flex items-center gap-2 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-widest text-amber-400">
          <span>⚡ Knowledge Check</span>
        </div>
        <div className="mb-5 text-base font-semibold text-white">
          Why did the authors force every single embedding layer, attention layer, and feed-forward output in the Transformer Base model to have the exact same dimension ($d_{\text{model}} = 512$)?
        </div>
        <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>A) To facilitate the Residual Skip Connections (x + Sublayer(x)), which require identical vector dimensions for element-wise addition</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/30 bg-emerald-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-emerald-400">✓ Correct!</strong>
              <span>As stated directly in Section 3.1 of the paper: <em>&quot;To facilitate these residual connections, all sub-layers in the model, as well as the embedding layers, produce outputs of dimension $d_{\text{model}} = 512$.&quot;</em></span>
            </div>
          </details>
          <details className="group">
            <summary className="flex cursor-pointer items-center justify-between rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-200 transition-colors hover:bg-white/[0.06] marker:content-['']">
              <span>B) Because GPUs cannot multiply matrices that are larger than 512</span>
              <span className="text-amber-400 transition group-open:rotate-180">▼</span>
            </summary>
            <div className="mt-3 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-500/10 p-4 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              <strong className="mb-1 block text-rose-400">✕ Incorrect.</strong>
              <span>Inside the FFN sub-layer, the vector actually expands to $2{,}048$ before projecting back down to $512$ so it can be added back to the $512$-D residual stream.</span>
            </div>
          </details>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Inside the Decoder: The 3-Sublayer Architecture &amp; Output Generator
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        The Decoder is also composed of a stack of <strong className="text-white">$N = 6$ identical layers</strong>. However, unlike the Encoder (which has 2 sub-layers per block), <strong className="text-violet-300">each Decoder block contains THREE sub-layers</strong>, all wrapped with Residual Connections and Layer Normalization:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase text-rose-400">Decoder Sub-Layer 1</div>
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-white">Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Allows each position in the Decoder to attend to all earlier generated positions up to and including itself. Future positions are masked out (set to $-\infty$ before Softmax) combined with shifting target outputs right by one position, ensuring predictions for position $i$ depend <strong className="text-rose-300">only on known outputs at positions less than $i$</strong>.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase text-emerald-400">Decoder Sub-Layer 2</div>
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-white">Encoder-Decoder Cross-Attention</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            This is the <strong className="text-emerald-300">bridge between the two towers</strong>! Here, the <strong className="text-white">Queries ($\mathbf{Q}$)</strong> come from the previous Decoder sub-layer, while the <strong className="text-cyan-300">Keys ($\mathbf{K}$) and Values ($\mathbf{V}$)</strong> come from the final output ($\mathbf{Z}$) of the Encoder stack. This lets every position in the Decoder attend over all positions in the input sequence!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase text-violet-400">Decoder Sub-Layer 3</div>
          <div className="mb-2 text-base font-bold text-white">Position-Wise Feed-Forward (FFN)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Identical in structure to the Encoder&apos;s FFN ($512 \to 2048 \to 512$ with $\text{ReLU}$). Processes the combined target context + source context at each position independently to prepare for predicting the next word.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-2xl border border-amber-500/25 bg-amber-500/[0.04] p-6">
        <h3 className="mb-2 text-lg font-bold text-amber-300">The Final Top Cap: Linear Projection + Softmax Generator</h3>
        <div className="space-y-3 text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
          <div>
            1. <strong className="text-white">The Linear Layer (Unembedding Projection):</strong> Projects the $512$-dimensional vector from the top of Decoder Layer #6 up to the size of the entire subword vocabulary ($|\mathcal{V}| = 37{,}000$ logits).
          </div>
          <div>
            2. <strong className="text-white">The Softmax Layer:</strong> Converts those $37{,}000$ raw logit scores into a valid probability distribution summing to $100\%$, and selects the next output token!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Residual Connections, Layer Normalization &amp; Training Tricks
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Around <em>every single sub-layer</em> in both the Encoder and Decoder, the authors wrapped a <strong className="text-emerald-300">Residual Skip Connection</strong> ($\mathbf{x} + \text{Sublayer}(\mathbf{x})$) followed by <strong className="text-cyan-300">Layer Normalization</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-emerald-300">1. Why the Residual ($+$) Wire?</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Without the skip connection, multiplying matrices across $6$ Encoder and $6$ Decoder layers ($30+$ sub-layers total!) causes gradients to vanish and destroys the token&apos;s original identity &amp; position signal. With the $+$ wire, each layer only needs to learn a small refinement ($\Delta \mathbf{x}$) added onto the main highway!
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-cyan-300">2. Why LayerNorm?</div>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Repeatedly adding vectors across dozens of layers causes their magnitudes to grow uncontrollably. LayerNorm normalizes the $512$ features of each individual token to mean $0$ and variance $1$ after every step.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/25 bg-cyan-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-cyan-300">1. 4,000-Step LR Warmup</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Ramps the learning rate up linearly over the first $4{,}000$ training steps before decaying by $\text{step}^{-0.5}$ so early attention gradients never explode.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-violet-300">2. Label Smoothing ($\epsilon_{ls} = 0.1$)</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Softens target one-hot labels to $0.9$ during training so the model learns not to be overconfident, improving translation accuracy and BLEU scores.
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-5">
          <div className="mb-1 font-bold text-emerald-300">3. 3-Way Weight Sharing</div>
          <div className="text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            Shares the exact same $37{,}000 \times 512$ weight matrix between the Encoder embedding, the Decoder embedding, and the pre-softmax Linear layer!
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
