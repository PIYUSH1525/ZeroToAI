    <section>
      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
        1. The Problem: Life Before Transformers
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-5 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          To understand why this paper is so brilliant, we first need to understand the problem it was trying to solve: Sequence Transduction. This is a fancy term for taking one sequence of data (like an English sentence) and turning it into another sequence (like a French sentence).
        </div>

        <div>
          Before 2017, the Al industry relied on two main architectures to process language:
          <ol className="mt-2 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
            <li>RNNs (Recurrent Neural Networks) and their advanced cousins, LSTMS (Long Short-Term Memories).</li>
            <li>CNNs (Convolutional Neural Networks).</li>
          </ol>
        </div>

        <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">The Fatal Flaw of Recurrence</h3>
        <div>
          RNNs process text exactly like a human reads a book: sequentially, one word at a time. If you give an RNN a 100-word sentence, it reads word 1, processes it, updates its memory, moves to word 2, and so on.
        </div>
        <div>This sequential nature created two massive bottlenecks:</div>

        <div className="my-6 grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-400/20 bg-blue-400/[0.06] p-5">
            <div className="mb-3 font-semibold text-blue-200">• The Memory Problem:</div>
            <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              By the time the RNN reaches word 100, it has largely "forgotten" the context of word 1. This made translating long paragraphs incredibly difficult.
            </div>
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-400/20 bg-rose-400/[0.06] p-5">
            <div className="mb-3 font-semibold text-rose-200">• The Speed Problem:</div>
            <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
              Because word 5 cannot be processed until word 4 is finished, you cannot use parallel processing. You could have a supercomputer with thousands of GPUs, but an RNN forces them to wait in a single-file line. Training these models took weeks or months.
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">The Attempted Fix</h3>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
          Researchers tried adding an "Attention Mechanism" to these RNNs. This allowed the model to "look back" at specific words in the input sentence when generating an output. However, because the underlying engine was still an RNN, the slow, sequential bottleneck remained.
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section>
      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
        2. The Big Idea: "Attention Is All You Need"
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          The Google research team asked a radical question: What if we completely throw away the Recurrent Neural Network, and just keep the Attention mechanism?
        </div>

        <div>
          They proposed a brand-new architecture called the Transformer. Instead of processing words one by one, the Transformer processes every word in a sentence simultaneously.
        </div>

        <div>By dispensing with recurrence and convolution entirely, the Transformer achieved two things:</div>

        <div className="my-7 space-y-4 rounded-xl border border-violet-400/15 bg-violet-400/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="border-l-2 border-emerald-400/60 pl-4">
            <div><strong className="text-white">1. Infinite Context:</strong> It could directly connect word 1 to word 100 in a single step.</div>
          </div>
          <div className="border-l-2 border-cyan-400/60 pl-4">
            <div><strong className="text-white">2. Massive Parallelization:</strong> Because words are processed simultaneously, the model could be trained on modern GPUs at unprecedented speeds.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section>
      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
        3. The Core Innovation: Self-Attention
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          If you aren't processing words sequentially, how does the Al know how words relate to each other? The answer is Self-Attention.
        </div>

        <div>
          Self-attention allows every word in a sentence to look at every other word and calculate how strongly they are connected. Consider the sentence:
        </div>
        
        <blockquote className="border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 font-mono text-emerald-200">
          "The bank of the river was steep, so I couldn't sit by the bank."
        </blockquote>

        <div>
          How does a computer know that the first "bank" is a geographical feature and the second is a financial institution? Through self-attention, the word "bank" looks at the surrounding words ("river", "steep") and updates its own mathematical definition based on that context.
        </div>

        <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">The Q, K, V Analogy</h3>
        <div>
          To compute self-attention, the Transformer creates three vectors (lists of numbers) for every single word: a Query (Q), a Key (K), and a Value (V).
        </div>
        
        <div>Think of it like searching for a book in a library:</div>

        <div className="my-8 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/50 p-5">
          <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-3">
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-pink-400/20 bg-pink-400/[0.05] p-5">
              <div className="mb-3 text-center text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wider text-pink-300">• Query (Q)</div>
              <div className="text-center text-sm leading-6 text-slate-400">
                What you are looking for. (e.g., "I am the word 'bank', and I am looking for words that clarify my meaning.")
              </div>
            </div>
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-400/20 bg-cyan-400/[0.05] p-5">
              <div className="mb-3 text-center text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wider text-cyan-300">• Key (K)</div>
              <div className="text-center text-sm leading-6 text-slate-400">
                The label on every book in the library. (e.g., "I am the word 'river', and my label says I relate to water and nature.")
              </div>
            </div>
            <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-400/20 bg-emerald-400/[0.05] p-5">
              <div className="mb-3 text-center text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-300">• Value (V)</div>
              <div className="text-center text-sm leading-6 text-slate-400">
                The actual contents of the book. (e.g., The core mathematical meaning of the word 'river').
              </div>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
          The Al measures how well a word's Query matches every other word's Key. If there is a strong match (like 'bank' and 'river'), the model takes the Value of 'river' and blends it into the meaning of 'bank'.
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section>
      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
        4. The Math: Scaled Dot-Product Attention
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          The paper introduces a specific mathematical formula to calculate this blending, called Scaled Dot-Product Attention.
        </div>

        <div className="my-8 rounded-2xl border border-orange-400/15 bg-orange-400/[0.04] px-6 py-8 text-center text-xl overflow-x-auto">
          $$
          Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax((QK^{T})/\sqrt{d}_{k})V
          $$
        </div>

        <div>Let's break this down without getting lost in the algebra:</div>

        <div className="space-y-4">
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
            <div><strong className="text-white">1. Q $K^{\wedge}T$ (Dot Product):</strong> The model multiplies the Queries by the Keys. This calculates a "score" of how much focus each word should put on other words.</div>
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
            <div><strong className="text-white">2. $\sqrt{d_k}$ (Scaling):</strong> The researchers noticed that when models get large, multiplying these numbers together creates massive values, which breaks the neural network (a problem called vanishing gradients). They solved this by dividing the scores by the square root of the dimension size (d_k). This simple scaling trick is crucial for stabilizing the model.</div>
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
            <div><strong className="text-white">3. Softmax:</strong> This function converts the raw scores into percentages (probabilities) that add up to 100%. For example, "bank" might pay 80% attention to "river", 15% to "steep", and 5% to "the".</div>
          </div>
          <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
            <div><strong className="text-white">4. Multiply by V:</strong> Finally, the model multiplies these percentages by the Values to get the final, context-aware meaning of the word.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section>
      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-pink-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
        5. Multi-Head Attention: Thinking in Multiple Dimensions
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          If you only use one attention mechanism, the model might get hyper-fixated on just one type of relationship (like grammatical structure). But human language is complex. Words relate to each other through grammar, emotion, logic, and historical context.
        </div>
        <div>
          To solve this, the paper introduced Multi-Head Attention. Instead of doing the Q, K, V math once, the Transformer does it h times in parallel (the original paper used 8 "heads").
        </div>
        <ul className="list-none space-y-3 pl-2">
          <li className="flex items-start"><span className="mr-2 text-pink-400">•</span> Head 1 might figure out the subject-verb relationship (Who is doing the action?).</li>
          <li className="flex items-start"><span className="mr-2 text-pink-400">•</span> Head 2 might figure out the emotional tone.</li>
          <li className="flex items-start"><span className="mr-2 text-pink-400">•</span> Head 3 might track pronouns (figuring out that "she" refers to "Sarah" from two sentences ago).</li>
        </ul>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-pink-400/15 bg-pink-400/[0.04] p-5">
          After all 8 heads do their independent thinking, their conclusions are concatenated (stitched together) and sent to the next layer.
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section>
      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
        6. Positional Encoding: Giving Words a Sense of Time
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          Because the Transformer processes all words at the exact same time to maximize speed, it entirely loses the concept of word order. To the raw Transformer, "The dog bit the man" and "The man bit the dog" look exactly the same.
        </div>
        <div>
          To fix this, the authors introduced Positional Encoding. Before the words are fed into the network, the model injects a special "time stamp" into the mathematical representation of the word.
        </div>
        <div>
          They used mathematical sine and cosine wave functions of different frequencies to create these stamps. This is a brilliant engineering trick because it allows the model to easily calculate the relative distance between words, regardless of how long the sentence is, without needing to learn the positions from scratch.
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section>
      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-amber-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
        7. The Architecture Breakdown
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          The complete Transformer is split into two halves: The Encoder (which reads and understands the input) and the Decoder (which writes the output).
        </div>

        <div className="my-8 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-slate-950/50 p-6">
          <div className="mb-6 text-center text-sm font-semibold uppercase tracking-[0.18em] text-slate-400">
            Figure 1: The Transformer model architecture.
          </div>
          
          <div className="grid gap-8 md:grid-cols-2">
            {/* Encoder Representation */}
            <div className="flex flex-col items-center gap-3 rounded-xl border border-blue-500/20 bg-blue-500/[0.02] p-5">
              <div className="w-full rounded bg-slate-900 px-4 py-3 text-center text-sm border border-white/5">Inputs</div>
              <div className="text-slate-600">↑</div>
              <div className="w-full rounded bg-pink-900/40 px-4 py-3 text-center text-sm border border-pink-500/30 text-pink-200">Input Embedding <br/><span className="text-xs text-pink-400">+ Positional Encoding</span></div>
              <div className="text-slate-600">↑</div>
              
              <div className="w-full rounded-lg border border-blue-400/30 bg-blue-900/20 p-4 relative">
                <div className="absolute -left-8 top-1/2 -translate-y-1/2 text-xs font-mono text-slate-500">Nx</div>
                <div className="flex flex-col items-center gap-2">
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-orange-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm border border-orange-500/30 text-orange-200">Multi-Head Attention</div>
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-yellow-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-xs border border-yellow-500/30 text-yellow-200">Add & Norm</div>
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-blue-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm border border-blue-500/30 text-blue-200 mt-2">Feed Forward</div>
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-yellow-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-xs border border-yellow-500/30 text-yellow-200">Add & Norm</div>
                </div>
              </div>
            </div>

            {/* Decoder Representation */}
            <div className="flex flex-col items-center gap-3 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.02] p-5">
              <div className="w-full rounded bg-slate-900 px-4 py-3 text-center text-sm border border-white/5">Outputs (shifted right)</div>
              <div className="text-slate-600">↑</div>
              <div className="w-full rounded bg-pink-900/40 px-4 py-3 text-center text-sm border border-pink-500/30 text-pink-200">Output Embedding <br/><span className="text-xs text-pink-400">+ Positional Encoding</span></div>
              <div className="text-slate-600">↑</div>
              
              <div className="w-full rounded-lg border border-emerald-400/30 bg-emerald-900/20 p-4 relative">
                <div className="absolute -right-8 top-1/2 -translate-y-1/2 text-xs font-mono text-slate-500">Nx</div>
                <div className="flex flex-col items-center gap-2">
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-orange-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm border border-orange-500/30 text-orange-200">Masked Multi-Head Attention</div>
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-yellow-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-xs border border-yellow-500/30 text-yellow-200">Add & Norm</div>
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-orange-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm border border-orange-500/30 text-orange-200 mt-2">Multi-Head Attention</div>
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-yellow-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-xs border border-yellow-500/30 text-yellow-200">Add & Norm</div>
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-blue-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm border border-blue-500/30 text-blue-200 mt-2">Feed Forward</div>
                  <div className="w-full rounded bg-yellow-900/40 px-3 py-2 text-center text-xs border border-yellow-500/30 text-yellow-200">Add & Norm</div>
                </div>
              </div>
              
              <div className="text-slate-600">↓</div>
              <div className="w-full rounded bg-purple-900/40 px-4 py-2 text-center text-sm border border-purple-500/30 text-purple-200">Linear</div>
              <div className="w-full rounded bg-purple-900/40 px-4 py-2 text-center text-sm border border-purple-500/30 text-purple-200">Softmax</div>
              <div className="w-full rounded bg-slate-900 px-4 py-3 text-center text-sm border border-white/5">Output Probabilities</div>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">The Encoder (The Reader)</h3>
        <div>
          The original paper stacks 6 identical Encoder layers on top of each other. Each layer does two things:
          <ol className="mt-2 list-decimal space-y-2 pl-6">
            <li>Multi-Head Self-Attention: Figures out how the input words relate to each other.</li>
            <li>Feed-Forward Neural Network: A standard, small neural network that processes the attention data and refines it.</li>
          </ol>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
          Note on Stability: Around both of these steps, the authors used Residual Connections (adding the original input back into the output) and Layer Normalization. This prevents data loss and keeps the math stable as information travels deep into the 6 layers.
        </div>

        <h3 className="mt-8 text-xl font-bold text-white">The Decoder (The Writer)</h3>
        <div>
          The Decoder also consists of 6 stacked layers, but it operates slightly differently because it is generating text one word at a time based on what the Encoder learned.
          <ol className="mt-2 list-decimal space-y-4 pl-6">
            <li>Masked Self-Attention: When generating the 4th word of a translation, the model is not allowed to look at the 5th or 6th word (because they haven't been generated yet). The "Mask" mathematically hides future words to prevent the Al from "cheating" during training.</li>
            <li>Cross-Attention: This is the bridge. The Decoder takes its own Queries (what it needs to write next) and matches them against the Keys and Values generated by the Encoder (the source material). This is how the model knows which part of the English sentence to look at while writing the French sentence.</li>
            <li>Feed-Forward Network: Refines the output, just like the Encoder.</li>
          </ol>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-yellow-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          The paper didn't just claim the Transformer was better; it provided mathematical and computational proof comparing it to RNNs and CNNs across three metrics:
        </div>

                <th className="border-b border-white/10 px-5 py-4 font-semibold text-rose-300">Recurrent (RNN)</th>
                <th className="border-b border-white/10 px-5 py-4 font-semibold text-cyan-300">Transformer</th>

              </tr>
            </thead>

                <td className="px-5 py-4 font-semibold text-white">Sequential Operations</td>

                <td className="px-5 py-4">$0(1)$</td>
                <td className="px-5 py-4">An RNN takes 100 steps for a 100-word sentence. The Transformer takes 1 step, processed in parallel.</td>
              </tr>
              <tr className="border-b border-white/10">
                <td className="px-5 py-4 font-semibold text-white">Maximum Path Length</td>
                <td className="px-5 py-4">$0(n)$</td>
                <td className="px-5 py-4">$0(1)$</td>
                <td className="px-5 py-4">How far information has to travel between word 1 and word 100. In a Transformer, every word is exactly 1 step away from every other word, eliminating memory loss.</td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td className="px-5 py-4 font-semibold text-white">Complexity per Layer</td>
                <td className="px-5 py-4">$\mathcal{O}(n \cdot d^2)$</td>
                <td className="px-5 py-4">$\mathcal{O}(n^2 \cdot d)$</td>
                <td className="px-5 py-4">For most sentences, n (sequence length) is smaller than d (representation dimension), making the Transformer mathematically faster to compute.</td>
              </tr>
            </tbody>
          </table>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section>
      <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-rose-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
        9. The Results that Shocked the Industry
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          To prove their architecture worked, the Google team tested the Transformer on standard Machine Translation benchmarks.
        </div>
        
        <ol className="list-decimal space-y-4 pl-6">
          <li><strong>English-to-German (WMT 2014):</strong> The model achieved a BLEU score of 28.4. Not only did it beat the existing state-of-the-art models, it beat massive ensembles (groups of models working together) by over 2 full BLEU points, which is a massive leap in Al research.</li>
          <li><strong>English-to-French (WMT 2014):</strong> It established a new single-model record with a BLEU score of 41.8.</li>
          <li><strong>The Training Cost:</strong> This was the killing blow. Previous state-of-the-art models took weeks to train on massive supercomputers. The Transformer's base model trained in just 12 hours on 8 GPUs. Even their massive "Big Model" took only 3.5 days. It was vastly smarter and incredibly cheap to train.</li>
        </ol>
      </div>
    </section>

        10. The Legacy
      </h2>

      <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
        <div>
          "Attention Is All You Need" was meant to be a paper about language translation. But the architecture was so universally powerful that it quickly took over the entire Al industry.
        </div>
        
        <ul className="list-none space-y-3 pl-2">
          <li className="flex items-start"><span className="mr-2 text-purple-400">•</span> Within a year, Google released BERT, an Encoder-only Transformer that revolutionized Google Search by understanding the context of user queries.</li>
          <li className="flex items-start"><span className="mr-2 text-purple-400">•</span> OpenAl released the GPT series (Generative Pre-trained Transformer), using a Decoder-only architecture to generate human-like text, eventually leading to ChatGPT.</li>
          <li className="flex items-start"><span className="mr-2 text-purple-400">•</span> Today, Transformers are not just used for text. They are used in computer vision (Vision Transformers), audio generation, and even biological protein folding (AlphaFold).</li>
        </ul>

          <strong>By proving that you don't need complex, slow sequential processing-and that attention really is all you need-this paper unlocked the era of highly scalable, deeply capable Artificial Intelligence.</strong>
        </blockquote>
