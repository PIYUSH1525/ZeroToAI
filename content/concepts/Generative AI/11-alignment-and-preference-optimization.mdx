    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Beginner: Why a Smart Model Is Not Yet a Good Assistant
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        After pre-training and fine-tuning, an LLM can write fluent text. But fluent is not the same as <strong className="text-white">helpful, honest and safe</strong>. A model may give a confident wrong answer, follow a harmful request, ramble, or flatter the user. <strong className="text-white">Alignment</strong> is the work of steering a model toward what people actually want.
      </div>
      <div>
        The hard part is that <em>good</em> is difficult to write down as a rule. It is much easier for a person to look at <strong className="text-white">two answers and say which one is better</strong>. Almost all modern alignment methods are built on that simple idea: learning from <strong className="text-white">preferences</strong>.
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-500/25 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-blue-300">Helpful</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-blue-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-blue-300">Does the job</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            <div>Answers the real question clearly and completely.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-emerald-300">Honest</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-emerald-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-emerald-300">Tells the truth</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            <div>Admits uncertainty instead of inventing facts.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-rose-500/25 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-rose-300">Harmless</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-rose-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-rose-300">Avoids harm</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            <div>Declines dangerous requests politely.</div>
          </div>
        </div>

      <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/20 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-cyan-300">Analogy:</strong> Pre-training builds a very knowledgeable student. Alignment is the mentoring that teaches the student how to behave in front of real people.
      </div>
    </div>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Beginner: Collecting Human Preferences
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        The raw material for alignment is a <strong className="text-white">preference dataset</strong>. For each prompt, the model writes two or more answers, and a person (or sometimes another AI) marks which one is better:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Prompt</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Answer A</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Answer B</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Better</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">How do I boil an egg?</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Put it in boiling water for 8 to 10 minutes, then cool it in cold water.</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Just google it.</td><td className="px-5 py-3">A (chosen)</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Is the Earth flat?</td><td className="px-5 py-3">No. Satellite photos, ship horizons and flights all show it is round.</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Some people say yes!</td><td className="px-5 py-3">A (chosen)</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div>
        Each pair gives a <strong className="text-white">chosen</strong> answer and a <strong className="text-white">rejected</strong> answer. Thousands to hundreds of thousands of such pairs are used. The quality and diversity of the labelers matters a lot, because the model will learn <em>their</em> idea of good.
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/20 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-amber-300">AI feedback:</strong> Collecting human labels is slow and costly, so some methods let a strong model judge the answers using a written list of principles (for example <em>Constitutional AI</em>). This is often called <strong className="text-white">RLAIF</strong>.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Medium: RLHF, Teaching a Reward Model
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        <strong className="text-white">RLHF</strong> (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) was the first widely used recipe. It has three stages:
      </div>
      <div className="space-y-3">
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-blue-500/20 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-blue-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-blue-300">1</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">SFT:</strong> Fine-tune the base model on good example conversations.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-violet-500/20 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-violet-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-violet-300">2</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Reward model:</strong> Train a second model that gives any answer a <strong className="text-white">score</strong> reflecting how much humans would like it.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-emerald-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-emerald-300">3</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Reinforcement learning:</strong> Let the language model write answers, score them with the reward model, and nudge the model toward higher scores (usually with an algorithm called <strong className="text-white">PPO</strong>).</div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div>
        The reward model learns from pairs using the <strong className="text-white">Bradley-Terry</strong> idea: the probability that answer A beats answer B depends on the difference of their scores.
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060913] p-5 text-center text-white">
        $$P(A \succ B) = \sigma\big(r(A) - r(B)\big)$$
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/20 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-amber-300">The leash:</strong> If the language model could chase reward freely it would find tricks that fool the reward model (called <strong className="text-white">reward hacking</strong>). So training adds a <strong className="text-white">KL penalty</strong> that keeps the new model close to the original one.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Medium: DPO, Skipping the Reward Model
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        RLHF works, but it is complex: three models, an unstable reinforcement-learning loop and many settings to tune. <strong className="text-white">DPO</strong> (Direct Preference Optimization) reaches a similar goal with <strong className="text-white">one simple loss</strong> that is trained like normal fine-tuning:
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060913] p-5 text-center text-white">
        $$\mathcal{L}_{DPO} = -\log \sigma\Big(\beta\big[(\log \pi(y_w) - \log \pi_{ref}(y_w)) - (\log \pi(y_l) - \log \pi_{ref}(y_l))\big]\Big)$$
      </div>
      <div>
        In plain words: compare the model with a frozen <strong className="text-white">reference</strong> copy. Raise the probability of the <strong className="text-white">chosen</strong> answer $y_w$ relative to the reference, lower the probability of the <strong className="text-white">rejected</strong> answer $y_l$, and the knob $\beta$ controls how far the model may drift.
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3"></th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">RLHF (PPO)</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">DPO</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Models needed</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Policy, reference, reward model (and a value model)</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Policy and a frozen reference</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Training</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Reinforcement-learning loop, sampling needed</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Plain supervised-style training on pairs</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Complexity</td><td className="px-5 py-3">High and sometimes unstable</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Much simpler</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Used when</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Maximum control, large teams</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Quick, stable preference tuning</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 data-type="code" className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      7. Python Implementation: A Reward Model and the DPO Loss
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        This script does two things from scratch. First it trains a tiny <strong className="text-white">reward model</strong> from noisy human choices between pairs of answers. Then it computes the <strong className="text-white">DPO loss</strong> for four different situations.
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
        <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
          <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">preference_learning.py</span>
          <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3.10+ · NumPy only</span>
        </div>
        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block">import numpy as np</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def sigmoid(x):</span>
            <span className="block">    return 1 / (1 + np.exp(-x))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">rng = np.random.default&#95;rng(0)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 1. Reward model: learn what humans prefer from pairs of answers (Bradley-Terry)</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># Each answer is described by 2 features: &#91;helpfulness, rudeness&#93;</span>
            <span className="block">w&#95;true = np.array(&#91;2.0, -3.0&#93;)                   # humans like helpful, dislike rude</span>
            <span className="block">A = rng.normal(size=(500, 2))                    # answer A features</span>
            <span className="block">B = rng.normal(size=(500, 2))                    # answer B features</span>
            <span className="block">prefer&#95;A = (sigmoid((A - B) @ w&#95;true) &gt; rng.random(500)).astype(float)   # noisy human choice</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">w = np.zeros(2)</span>
            <span className="block">for step in range(500):</span>
            <span className="block">    p = sigmoid((A - B) @ w)                     # model&apos;s chance that A beats B</span>
            <span className="block">    grad = (A - B).T @ (p - prefer&#95;A) / len(p)   # gradient of the logistic loss</span>
            <span className="block">    w -= 0.5 &#42; grad</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;Learned reward weights &#91;helpful, rude&#93;:&quot;, np.round(w, 2), &quot;| humans secretly used&quot;, w&#95;true)</span>
            <span className="block">agree = np.mean((sigmoid((A - B) @ w) &gt; 0.5) == (prefer&#95;A == 1))</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;Reward model agrees with human choices:&quot;, round(float(agree), 3))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 2. DPO loss: no reward model needed, only log-probabilities of the two answers</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def dpo&#95;loss(logp&#95;chosen, logp&#95;rejected, ref&#95;chosen, ref&#95;rejected, beta=0.1):</span>
            <span className="block">    margin = beta &#42; ((logp&#95;chosen - ref&#95;chosen) - (logp&#95;rejected - ref&#95;rejected))</span>
            <span className="block">    return -np.log(sigmoid(margin))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
