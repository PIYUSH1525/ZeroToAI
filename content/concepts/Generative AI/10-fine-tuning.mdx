  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Beginner: Why Fine-Tune a Model That Already Knows So Much?
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        After pre-training, a <strong className="text-white">base model</strong> knows a huge amount about language and the world, but it behaves like a very well-read autocomplete. Ask it <em>What is the capital of France?</em> and it might continue with <em>What is the capital of Germany?</em> because that is what often follows a question in its training text.
      </div>
      <div>
        <strong className="text-white">Fine-tuning</strong> continues training the model on a much smaller, carefully chosen dataset so that it learns a <strong className="text-white">behavior</strong>: follow instructions, answer politely, use a company&apos;s writing style, speak medical language, or output clean JSON.
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-2">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-500/25 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-blue-300">Pre-training</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-blue-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-blue-300">Learn the world</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            <div><strong className="text-white">Data:</strong> Trillions of raw tokens</div>
            <div><strong className="text-white">Goal:</strong> General knowledge and language skill</div>
            <div><strong className="text-white">Cost:</strong> Millions of GPU-hours</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-emerald-300">Fine-tuning</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-emerald-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-emerald-300">Learn a behavior</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            <div><strong className="text-white">Data:</strong> Thousands to hundreds of thousands of good examples</div>
            <div><strong className="text-white">Goal:</strong> A specific style, task or format</div>
            <div><strong className="text-white">Cost:</strong> A few GPU-hours to a few days</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/20 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-cyan-300">Analogy:</strong> Pre-training is like going through school: broad general knowledge. Fine-tuning is the job training: learning how to apply that knowledge in one specific role.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Beginner: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Instruction Data
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        The most common first step is <strong className="text-white">supervised fine-tuning (SFT)</strong>, also called <em>instruction tuning</em>. The training data is a set of examples written as <strong className="text-white">instruction and ideal answer</strong> pairs:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Instruction (prompt)</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Ideal response (target)</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Explain gradient descent to a 10-year-old.</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Imagine walking down a foggy hill by feeling the slope under your feet...</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Convert this to JSON: name Ana, age 31</td><td className="px-5 py-3">a JSON object with the name and age fields</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Summarize this email in one sentence.</td><td className="px-5 py-3">A one-sentence summary written in a clear and polite tone.</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div>
        The training loop is the <strong className="text-white">same next-token loss</strong> as pre-training, but usually computed <strong className="text-white">only on the response part</strong>. The model is not rewarded for repeating the question, only for producing a good answer.
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-emerald-300">Quality over quantity:</strong> A small set of <strong className="text-white">high-quality, varied</strong> examples often beats a huge noisy set. Many successful assistants were tuned with only thousands of carefully written examples.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Medium: Full Fine-Tuning and Its Problem
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        The simplest approach is <strong className="text-white">full fine-tuning</strong>: update <strong className="text-white">every</strong> weight of the model. It is powerful, but it has serious costs:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Problem</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">What it means for a 7B model</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Memory</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Weights, gradients and optimizer states together need roughly 16 bytes per parameter, so about <strong className="text-white">112 GB</strong> for 7 billion parameters. Far beyond a normal GPU.</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Storage</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Every fine-tuned version is a full copy of about 14 GB (16-bit weights).</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Forgetting</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Changing all weights can erase some of the original skills. This is called <strong className="text-white">catastrophic forgetting</strong>.</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/20 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-amber-300">The big question:</strong> Do we really need to change all 7 billion numbers to teach a new style? Research says no. The needed change is usually <strong className="text-white">simple</strong>, and that is what LoRA exploits.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Medium: LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        <strong className="text-white">LoRA</strong> keeps the original weight matrix $W$ <strong className="text-white">frozen</strong> and learns only a small <em>correction</em> next to it. The correction is the product of two thin matrices, $B$ and $A$:
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060913] p-5 text-center text-white">
        $$W_{new} = W + \frac{\alpha}{r} \, B A$$
      </div>
      <div>
        If $W$ is a $4096 \times 4096$ table, then $A$ is $r \times 4096$ and $B$ is $4096 \times r$, where the <strong className="text-white">rank</strong> $r$ is tiny, such as 8. So instead of training $16{,}777{,}216$ numbers we train only $2 \times 4096 \times 8 = 65{,}536$, about <strong className="text-white">0.39%</strong>.
      </div>
      <div className="space-y-3">
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-blue-500/20 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-blue-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-blue-300">1</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Freeze the base model:</strong> No original weight changes, so the original knowledge is protected.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-violet-500/20 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-violet-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-violet-300">2</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Add small adapters:</strong> Insert $A$ and $B$ next to chosen layers, usually the attention matrices.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-emerald-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-emerald-300">3</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Start safe:</strong> $B$ starts as all zeros, so the model begins <strong className="text-white">exactly</strong> as the pretrained one.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-orange-500/20 bg-orange-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-orange-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-orange-300">4</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Train only $A$ and $B$:</strong> Gradients flow into the adapters only, so memory and time drop sharply.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/20 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-rose-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-rose-300">5</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Ship a tiny file:</strong> The adapter is a few megabytes. You can load, swap or merge it into $W$ whenever you like.</div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-emerald-300">Why it works:</strong> Fine-tuning changes are usually <strong className="text-white">low-rank</strong>: they live in a small number of directions. A thin pair of matrices is enough to capture them, so quality stays close to full fine-tuning.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Advanced: QLoRA, Forgetting and Choosing the Right Tool
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        <strong className="text-white">QLoRA</strong> goes one step further. It stores the frozen base model in <strong className="text-white">4-bit</strong> numbers (about 0.5 byte per parameter, so a 7B model shrinks from about 14 GB to about 3.5 GB) and trains 16-bit LoRA adapters on top. This lets one consumer GPU fine-tune models that would otherwise need a data-center.
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Method</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Trains</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Rough memory for a 7B model</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Best when</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Prompting / RAG</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Nothing</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Just the model for inference</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Knowledge changes often, or you only need a better prompt</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Full fine-tuning</td><td className="px-5 py-3">All weights</td><td className="px-5 py-3">About 112 GB</td><td className="px-5 py-3">You have a big budget and need maximum change</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">LoRA</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Small adapters</td><td className="px-5 py-3">About 14 GB for the 16-bit base, plus a little</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Style, format or domain adaptation on one good GPU</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">QLoRA</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Small adapters on a 4-bit base</td><td className="px-5 py-3">About 4 to 6 GB in practice</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Limited hardware, such as a single consumer GPU</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">Fine-tune, prompt or retrieve?</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">Use <strong className="text-white">prompting</strong> first, it is free. Use <strong className="text-white">RAG</strong> when the model needs fresh or private facts. Use <strong className="text-white">fine-tuning</strong> to change <em>behavior</em>: tone, format, domain language or a skill. Fine-tuning is poor at teaching many new facts.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/20 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-amber-300">Next stage: alignment.</strong> After SFT, many assistants are refined with preference data, where humans or AI judges say which of two answers is better. Methods such as <strong className="text-white">RLHF</strong> and <strong className="text-white">DPO</strong> use this to make answers more helpful and safe. That is a topic of its own.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 data-type="code" className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      7. Python Implementation: Training a LoRA Adapter From Scratch
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        This script shows LoRA working on a small scale. The big weight matrix <code>W</code> is <strong className="text-white">frozen</strong>. The new skill is a low-rank change, and we recover it by training only the thin matrices <code>A</code> and <code>B</code>, which hold just a quarter of the parameters of <code>W</code> in this tiny example and less than half a percent in a real layer.
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
        <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
          <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">lora_from_scratch.py</span>
          <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3.10+ · NumPy only</span>
        </div>
        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block">import numpy as np</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">rng = np.random.default_rng(0)</span>
            <span className="block">d, r, n = 16, 2, 200</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">W = rng.normal(size=(d, d))                              # frozen pretrained weights</span>
            <span className="block">delta = rng.normal(size=(d, r)) @ rng.normal(size=(r, d)) / d    # the new skill is a low-rank change</span>
            <span className="block">X = rng.normal(size=(n, d))</span>
            <span className="block">Y = X @ (W + delta).T                                    # what the fine-tuned model should output</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">A = rng.normal(size=(r, d)) * 0.1                        # LoRA matrices: the ONLY things we train</span>
            <span className="block">B = np.zeros((d, r))                                     # B starts at zero, so we begin exactly at the pretrained model</span>
            <span className="block">lr = 1.0</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def current_loss():</span>
            <span className="block">    return np.mean((X @ (W + B @ A).T - Y) ** 2)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;Trainable parameters: LoRA =&quot;, A.size + B.size, &quot;| full fine-tuning =&quot;, W.size)</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;Loss before training:&quot;, round(float(current_loss()), 5))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">for step in range(601):</span>
            <span className="block">    E = X @ (W + B @ A).T - Y</span>
            <span className="block">    G = (2 / (n * d)) * E.T @ X                          # gradient with respect to the combined update B @ A</span>
            <span className="block">    dB, dA = G @ A.T, B.T @ G</span>
            <span className="block">    B -= lr * dB</span>
            <span className="block">    A -= lr * dA</span>
            <span className="block">    if step in (100, 300, 600):</span>
            <span className="block">        print(&quot;step&quot;, step, &quot;| loss&quot;, format(current_loss(), &quot;.2e&quot;))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># Same idea at real scale: one 4096 x 4096 layer with rank 8</span>
            <span className="block">full, lora = 4096 * 4096, 2 * 4096 * 8</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;One real layer: full =&quot;, format(full, &quot;,&quot;), &quot;| LoRA =&quot;, format(lora, &quot;,&quot;), &quot;|&quot;, round(100 * lora / full, 2), &quot;% of the size&quot;)</span>
          </code>
        </pre>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs font-bold text-emerald-300">EXPECTED OUTPUT (your numbers may differ slightly)</div>
        <div className="space-y-1 font-mono text-xs leading-6 text-slate-200">
          <div>Trainable parameters: LoRA = 64 | full fine-tuning = 256</div>
