  <section>

      3. Medium: Few-Shot Examples and Delimiters
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        <strong className="text-white">Zero-shot</strong> means asking with no examples. <strong className="text-white">Few-shot</strong> means showing the model a few input and output pairs first. Examples are often the strongest way to communicate a style or format, because the model copies the pattern.
      </div>
      <div>
        Use <strong className="text-white">delimiters</strong>, clear separators such as triple quotes or tags, to keep your instructions apart from the data. This also makes the prompt easier to read and safer against text in the data that looks like instructions.
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Technique</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">When to use it</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Zero-shot</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Simple, common tasks</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Few-shot (2 to 5 examples)</td><td className="px-5 py-3">You need a specific format, tone or label set</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Delimiters around data</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Always, when you paste documents or user text</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Ask for a format</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Whenever another program will read the answer</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/20 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-amber-300">Tip:</strong> Make examples <strong className="text-white">diverse</strong> and <strong className="text-white">correct</strong>. A wrong example teaches the wrong pattern.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Medium: Reasoning, Step by Step
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        For multi-step problems such as math, logic or planning, asking the model to <strong className="text-white">show its reasoning</strong> before the final answer often improves accuracy. This is called <strong className="text-white">chain-of-thought</strong> prompting. A simple version is to add a line such as <em>Think step by step, then give the final answer on its own line.</em>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/20 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-cyan-300">Newer reasoning models:</strong> Some models already reason internally before answering. For them, a simple clear question often works best and extra instructions to think step by step may not be needed. Test both ways on your task.
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">Breaking big tasks up</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">Instead of one huge prompt, split the work: first extract the facts, then write the summary, then check it. Each step is easier to get right and easier to debug.</div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
