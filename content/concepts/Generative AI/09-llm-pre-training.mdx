  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Beginner: What Is Pre-Training?
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        A brand-new LLM is just a huge pile of <strong className="text-white">random numbers</strong>. It cannot answer questions, write code or even finish a sentence. <strong className="text-white">Pre-training</strong> is the long first stage where it learns the general patterns of language by reading an enormous amount of text.
      </div>
      <div>
        The task it practices is incredibly simple: <strong className="text-white">guess the next token</strong>. Show it <em>The capital of France is</em> and it should give a high score to <em>Paris</em>. Repeat this trillions of times on text from books, websites, articles and code, and the model slowly picks up grammar, facts, reasoning patterns and even coding style, all as a side effect.
      </div>
      <div className="grid gap-5 md:grid-cols-3">
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-500/25 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-blue-300">Input</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-blue-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-blue-300">A piece of text</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            <div><em>The cat sat on the</em></div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-violet-500/25 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">
            <span className="font-bold text-violet-300">Task</span>
            <span className="rounded bg-violet-950/80 px-2 py-0.5 font-mono text-xs text-violet-300">Predict the next token</span>
          </div>
          <div className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-xs leading-relaxed text-slate-300">
            <div>Give every word in the vocabulary a probability.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/25 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-6">
          <div className="mb-1 flex items-center justify-between">

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Beginner: The Data Recipe
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Quality and variety of data matter as much as the model itself. A typical pre-training dataset is built in several steps:
      </div>
      <div className="space-y-3">
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-blue-500/20 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-blue-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-blue-300">1</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Collect:</strong> Web pages, books, scientific papers, code repositories, encyclopedias and forums.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-violet-500/20 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-violet-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-violet-300">2</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Clean:</strong> Remove menus, ads, broken text, unsafe content and personal data where possible.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-emerald-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-emerald-300">3</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Deduplicate:</strong> Remove repeated documents so the model does not just memorize them.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-orange-500/20 bg-orange-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-orange-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-orange-300">4</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Filter for quality:</strong> Keep well-written text and drop spam or gibberish, often using a small classifier.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-rose-500/20 bg-rose-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-rose-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-rose-300">5</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Tokenize and mix:</strong> Convert text to token ids with the tokenizer and blend sources in carefully chosen proportions.</div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/20 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-amber-300">Rule of thumb:</strong> <em>Better data beats more data.</em> Careful cleaning and de-duplication often improve a model more than simply adding another pile of raw text.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Medium: The Loss, Perplexity and the Training Loop
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        The model gives a probability to every possible next token. The <strong className="text-white">loss</strong> measures how surprised it was by the real one. This measure is called <strong className="text-white">cross-entropy</strong>:
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060913] p-5 text-center text-white">
        $$\text{loss} = -\log P(\text{correct next token})$$
      </div>
      <div>
        If the model gave the right word probability 1.0, the loss is 0. If it gave only 0.01, the loss is large (about 4.6). <strong className="text-white">Perplexity</strong> is just $e^{\text{loss}}$ and has a friendly meaning: <em>the model is as confused as if it had to choose between that many equally likely words</em>.
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Probability given to the correct word</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Loss</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Perplexity</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">0.90</td><td className="px-5 py-3">0.105</td><td className="px-5 py-3">1.11</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">0.50</td><td className="px-5 py-3">0.693</td><td className="px-5 py-3">2.00</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">0.10</td><td className="px-5 py-3">2.303</td><td className="px-5 py-3">10.0</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">0.01</td><td className="px-5 py-3">4.605</td><td className="px-5 py-3">100</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div>
        Each training step is the loop you already know from gradient descent: run a batch of text through the model, compute the loss, compute gradients with backpropagation, and update the weights with an optimizer (usually <strong className="text-white">AdamW</strong> with a warm-up and a gradual cosine decay of the learning rate).
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-emerald-300">Good news:</strong> This is the <em>same</em> loop you coded in the Gradient Descent lesson. Pre-training simply runs it at a gigantic scale with a gigantic dataset.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      4. Medium: Scaling Laws, Compute and Cost
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        One of the most important discoveries about LLMs is that their loss improves in a <strong className="text-white">smooth, predictable</strong> way as you increase three things together: the number of <strong className="text-white">parameters</strong> ($N$), the amount of <strong className="text-white">training tokens</strong> ($D$) and the <strong className="text-white">compute</strong>. Researchers can therefore predict how good a big model will be by training small ones first.
      </div>
      <div>
        Training compute is estimated with a famous rule of thumb: about <strong className="text-white">6 floating-point operations per parameter per token</strong>.
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060913] p-5 text-center text-white">
        $$\text{Compute (FLOPs)} \approx 6 \times N \times D$$
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-blue-500/20 bg-blue-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-blue-300">Chinchilla rule of thumb:</strong> For a given compute budget, a model trains best with roughly <strong className="text-white">20 tokens per parameter</strong>. A 7B model should see about 140 billion tokens. Many recent models deliberately train on far <em>more</em> than that, because a smaller model trained longer is cheaper to run for millions of users afterwards.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-cyan-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      5. Advanced: Training at Scale
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        One GPU cannot hold or train a large model, so real runs split the work across thousands of GPUs. You will hear four main strategies:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Strategy</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">What is split</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Simple picture</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Data parallel</td><td className="px-5 py-3">The batch of text</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Every GPU holds a full model copy and trains on different text, then they average their gradients</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Tensor parallel</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Single big matrices</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Each GPU computes a slice of every matrix multiplication</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Pipeline parallel</td><td className="px-5 py-3">The layers</td><td className="px-5 py-3">GPU 1 holds layers 1 to 10, GPU 2 holds layers 11 to 20, and so on, like an assembly line</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Sharding (ZeRO / FSDP)</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Weights, gradients, optimizer states</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Each GPU stores only a slice and fetches what it needs</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-bold text-white">Stability tricks</div>
        <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed text-slate-300">Large runs also use <strong className="text-white">mixed precision</strong> (16-bit numbers for speed, 32-bit for safety), <strong className="text-white">gradient clipping</strong> (cap huge updates), <strong className="text-white">checkpointing</strong> (save progress regularly) and constant monitoring of the loss curve, because a sudden loss spike can ruin days of work.</div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/20 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-amber-300">After pre-training:</strong> The result is a <strong className="text-white">base model</strong>. It is excellent at continuing text, but it does not follow instructions yet. It will happily continue your question with more questions. Turning it into a helpful assistant is the job of <strong className="text-white">fine-tuning</strong>, the next lesson.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 data-type="code" className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      7. Python Implementation: A Tiny Pre-Training Run and a Compute Calculator
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        This script pre-trains the world&apos;s smallest language model on a 11-word text: a table of scores for <em>which word follows which</em>. It uses exactly the real recipe: predict the next token, measure <strong className="text-white">cross-entropy</strong>, take a <strong className="text-white">gradient descent</strong> step. Then it uses the 6ND rule to estimate the cost of real models.
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
        <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
          <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">tiny_pretraining.py</span>
          <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3.10+ · NumPy only</span>
        </div>
        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block">import numpy as np</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">text = &quot;the cat sat on the mat the cat ate the rat&quot;</span>
            <span className="block">words = text.split()</span>
            <span className="block">vocab = sorted(set(words))</span>
            <span className="block">idx = &#123;w: i for i, w in enumerate(vocab)&#125;</span>
            <span className="block">ids = np.array([idx[w] for w in words])</span>
            <span className="block">V = len(vocab)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">inputs, targets = ids[:-1], ids[1:]       # next-token prediction: from token t, predict token t+1</span>
            <span className="block">rows = np.arange(len(targets))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">rng = np.random.default_rng(0)</span>
            <span className="block">W = rng.normal(scale=0.1, size=(V, V))    # logits table: row = current token, column = next token</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def softmax(z):</span>
            <span className="block">    z = z - z.max(axis=1, keepdims=True)</span>
            <span className="block">    e = np.exp(z)</span>
            <span className="block">    return e / e.sum(axis=1, keepdims=True)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">for step in range(201):</span>
            <span className="block">    probs = softmax(W[inputs])                         # predicted distribution at every position</span>
            <span className="block">    loss = -np.mean(np.log(probs[rows, targets]))      # cross-entropy on the true next token</span>
            <span className="block">    if step % 50 == 0:</span>
            <span className="block">        print(&quot;step&quot;, step, &quot;| loss&quot;, round(float(loss), 3), &quot;| perplexity&quot;, round(float(np.exp(loss)), 2))</span>
            <span className="block">    grad = probs.copy()</span>
            <span className="block">    grad[rows, targets] -= 1                           # gradient of the loss = probs - one_hot</span>
            <span className="block">    grad /= len(targets)</span>
            <span className="block">    np.add.at(W, inputs, -1.0 * grad)                  # gradient descent step, learning rate 1.0</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">probs = softmax(W[[idx[&quot;the&quot;]]])[0]</span>
            <span className="block">best = np.argsort(probs)[::-1][:2]</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;After the word &apos;the&apos;, the model predicts:&quot;, [(vocab[i], round(float(probs[i]), 2)) for i in best])</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># How much compute does real pre-training need?</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def training_cost(params, tokens, gpu_tflops=312, utilization=0.4):</span>
            <span className="block">    flops = 6 * params * tokens                        # rule of thumb: 6 FLOPs per parameter per token</span>
            <span className="block">    gpu_hours = flops / (gpu_tflops * 1e12 * utilization) / 3600</span>
            <span className="block">    return flops, gpu_hours</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">for name, n in [(&quot;1B&quot;, 1e9), (&quot;7B&quot;, 7e9), (&quot;70B&quot;, 70e9)]:</span>
            <span className="block">    tokens = 20 * n                                    # Chinchilla rule of thumb: ~20 tokens per parameter</span>
            <span className="block">    flops, hours = training_cost(n, tokens)</span>
            <span className="block">    print(name, &quot;| tokens&quot;, format(tokens, &quot;.1e&quot;), &quot;| FLOPs&quot;, format(flops, &quot;.2e&quot;), &quot;| A100 GPU-hours&quot;, format(round(hours), &quot;,&quot;))</span>
          </code>
        </pre>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs font-bold text-emerald-300">EXPECTED OUTPUT (your numbers may differ slightly)</div>
        <div className="space-y-1 font-mono text-xs leading-6 text-slate-200">
          <div>step 0 | loss 1.954 | perplexity 7.05</div>
          <div>step 50 | loss 0.739 | perplexity 2.09</div>
          <div>step 100 | loss 0.637 | perplexity 1.89</div>
          <div>step 150 | loss 0.606 | perplexity 1.83</div>
          <div>step 200 | loss 0.592 | perplexity 1.81</div>
