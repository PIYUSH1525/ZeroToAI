  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-blue-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      1. Beginner: Attention Cannot Tell Word Order
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Self-attention compares every token with every other token, but it does it like a <strong className="text-white">bag of words</strong>. If you shuffle the words, the raw attention math gives the same pairs of scores. To a plain attention layer, <em>dog bites man</em> and <em>man bites dog</em> look almost identical, yet they mean very different things.
      </div>
      <div>
        So we must <strong className="text-white">inject position information</strong> into the model. This is called <strong className="text-white">positional encoding</strong>. The question is <em>how</em>, and the answer changed a lot between the original Transformer (2017) and today&apos;s LLMs.
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-cyan-500/20 bg-cyan-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-cyan-300">Simple analogy:</strong> Imagine a class photo where nobody is standing in order. You know who is in the photo, but not who stands next to whom. Positional encoding is like putting a numbered sticker on every person.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-violet-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      2. Beginner: Three Ways to Add Position
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        Researchers have tried several families of ideas. Here is the whole story in one table:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">
        <table className="w-full text-left text-sm">
          <thead className="bg-white/[0.04] text-xs uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-400">
            <tr>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Method</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">How it works</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Used by</th>
              <th className="px-5 py-3">Main weakness</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Sinusoidal (absolute)</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Adds a fixed wave pattern for each position to the token embedding</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Original Transformer (2017)</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Struggles on text longer than seen in training</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Learned (absolute)</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Learns one vector per position, like a lookup table</td><td className="px-5 py-3">GPT-2, BERT</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Hard limit: no vector exists for unseen positions</td></tr>
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Relative (RoPE, ALiBi)</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Encodes the <strong className="text-white">distance</strong> between two tokens inside the attention score</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Most modern LLMs (RoPE is the most popular)</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Needs tricks to stretch to much longer text</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-emerald-300">The key insight:</strong> What usually matters in language is <strong className="text-white">how far apart</strong> two words are, not their exact seat numbers. Relative methods build that directly into attention, and that is why they won.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2 className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      3. Medium: RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) in Plain English
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        <strong className="text-white">RoPE</strong> gives each token&apos;s query and key vector a <strong className="text-white">rotation</strong> that depends on its position. Token at position 0 is not rotated, position 1 is rotated a little, position 2 a bit more, and so on, like turning a dial one notch per word.
      </div>
      <div>
        The magic happens in the dot product. When query and key are both rotated, the angle between them depends only on the <strong className="text-white">difference</strong> of their positions. The score between word 5 and word 2 is therefore the same as between word 105 and word 102, because both pairs are exactly 3 apart.
      </div>
      <div className="space-y-3">
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-blue-500/20 bg-blue-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-blue-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-blue-300">1</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Split the vector into pairs:</strong> A 128-number vector becomes 64 little 2D arrows.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-violet-500/20 bg-violet-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-violet-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-violet-300">2</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Pick a speed for each pair:</strong> The first pairs spin fast, the last pairs spin slowly. This lets the model feel both nearby and faraway distances.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-emerald-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-emerald-300">3</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Rotate by position × speed:</strong> The arrow of a token at position $m$ is turned by the angle $m \cdot \theta_i$.</div>
        </div>
        <div className="flex items-start gap-4 rounded-xl border border-orange-500/20 bg-orange-500/[0.04] p-4">
          <span className="flex h-8 w-8 shrink-0 items-center justify-center rounded-full bg-orange-500/20 font-mono text-sm font-bold text-orange-300">4</span>
          <div className="text-sm leading-relaxed"><strong className="text-white">Take the dot product as usual:</strong> The result now depends on the relative distance $m - n$.</div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060913] p-5 text-center text-white">
        $$\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d} \qquad \text{angle for pair } i = m \cdot \theta_i$$
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-amber-500/20 bg-amber-500/[0.05] p-5 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        <strong className="text-amber-300">Why it is elegant:</strong> RoPE adds <strong className="text-white">no extra parameters</strong> and does not touch the values. It only rotates queries and keys, and rotation never changes a vector&apos;s length, so the signal stays stable.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

      <div>
        A model trained with positions up to 4,096 has never seen a rotation angle for position 20,000. If you simply feed it longer text, the angles go outside the range it learned, and quality drops sharply. Several techniques stretch the usable context:
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-x-auto rounded-2xl border border-white/10">

          </thead>
          <tbody className="divide-y divide-white/5 text-slate-300">
            <tr><td className="px-5 py-3 font-semibold text-white">Position interpolation</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Squeeze new positions into the old range, so position 8,000 is treated as 4,000</td><td className="px-5 py-3">Needs a short fine-tune; nearby words become slightly harder to tell apart</td></tr>

    <h2 data-type="code" className="mb-6 border-l-4 border-teal-500 pl-4 text-2xl font-bold tracking-tight text-white sm:text-3xl">
      7. Python Implementation: RoPE From Scratch and the Relative-Position Test
    </h2>
    <div className="space-y-6 text-[16px] leading-8 text-slate-300">
      <div>
        This function rotates every pair of dimensions by the angle <code>position × speed</code>. Then we test the key promise of RoPE: <strong className="text-white">the score depends only on the distance between tokens, not on where they sit in the text</strong>.
      </div>
      <div className="overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border border-white/10 bg-[#060911]">
        <div className="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-white/10 bg-white/[0.03] px-5 py-3">
          <span className="font-mono text-xs font-semibold text-teal-400">rope_from_scratch.py</span>
          <span className="font-mono text-[11px] text-slate-400">Python 3.10+ · NumPy only</span>
        </div>
        <pre className="overflow-x-auto p-6 font-mono text-sm leading-7 text-slate-200">
          <code>
            <span className="block">import numpy as np</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-cyan-300">def rope(x, position, base=10000.0):</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500">    # Rotate each pair of dimensions (x[2i], x[2i+1]) by an angle that grows with position</span>
            <span className="block">    d = len(x)</span>
            <span className="block">    i = np.arange(d // 2)</span>
            <span className="block">    theta = base ** (-2 * i / d)          # one frequency per pair: fast to slow</span>
            <span className="block">    angle = position * theta</span>
            <span className="block">    cos, sin = np.cos(angle), np.sin(angle)</span>
            <span className="block">    out = np.empty_like(x)</span>
            <span className="block">    out[0::2] = x[0::2] * cos - x[1::2] * sin</span>
            <span className="block">    out[1::2] = x[0::2] * sin + x[1::2] * cos</span>
            <span className="block">    return out</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block">rng = np.random.default_rng(0)</span>
            <span className="block">q = rng.normal(size=8)</span>
            <span className="block">k = rng.normal(size=8)</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 1. Same distance (3 apart) at very different absolute positions</span>
            <span className="block">a = np.dot(rope(q, 5), rope(k, 2))</span>
            <span className="block">b = np.dot(rope(q, 105), rope(k, 102))</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;score at positions (5, 2):     &quot;, round(float(a), 4))</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;score at positions (105, 102): &quot;, round(float(b), 4))</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;Same score for the same distance:&quot;, np.isclose(a, b))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 2. A different distance gives a different score</span>
            <span className="block">c = np.dot(rope(q, 5), rope(k, 4))</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;score at distance 1:&quot;, round(float(c), 4), &quot;| at distance 3:&quot;, round(float(a), 4))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 3. Rotation never changes the length of a vector</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;Length before and after rotation:&quot;, round(float(np.linalg.norm(q)), 4), round(float(np.linalg.norm(rope(q, 50))), 4))</span>
            <span className="block">&nbsp;</span>
            <span className="block text-slate-500"># 4. Position interpolation: squeeze 8192 positions into the 4096 range seen in training</span>
            <span className="block">scale = 4096 / 8192</span>
            <span className="block">print(&quot;Position 8000 is treated as position&quot;, 8000 * scale)</span>
          </code>
        </pre>
      </div>
      <div className="rounded-xl border border-emerald-500/20 bg-emerald-500/[0.05] p-5">
        <div className="mb-2 font-mono text-xs font-bold text-emerald-300">EXPECTED OUTPUT (your numbers may differ slightly)</div>
        <div className="space-y-1 font-mono text-xs leading-6 text-slate-200">
          <div>score at positions (5, 2):      -1.5865</div>
          <div>score at positions (105, 102):  -1.5865</div>
          <div>Same score for the same distance: True</div>
          <div>score at distance 1: -1.6954 | at distance 3: -1.5865</div>
          <div>Length before and after rotation: 1.8627 1.8627</div>
          <div>Position 8000 is treated as position 4000.0</div>
        </div>
